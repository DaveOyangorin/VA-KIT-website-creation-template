Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Is Necessary

Having sewage water back up into your safe and clean spaces brings a lot of risks:

  • Health Risks: You can get seriously ill from harmful sewage bacteria, viruses, and parasites.
  • Structural Damage: Wastewater seeps into flooring, drywall, and foundations, weakening your home's structure.
  • Mold & Mildew Growth: Moisture from backups creates ideal conditions for mold and mildew to spread quickly.
  • Foul Odors: The lingering smell of sewage can make a home unlivable.

Common Causes of Sewage Backups

Understanding what leads to sewage backups can help you prevent future issues in your Ogden home. These are the most common reasons sewage is likely to back up into your home:

Clogged or Blocked Sewer Lines:

Grease, debris, or foreign objects blocking flow.﻿

Tree Root Intrusion:

In their search for water, roots from nearby trees can enter sewer lines and block drainage.

Heavy Rain & Flooding:

Excessive stormwater can overload systems, pushing wastewater back into your home.﻿

Old or Damaged Pipes:

Cracks and corrosion in aging pipes are common in older Ogden homes.﻿

Sump Pump Failure:

A malfunctioning sump pump can’t keep water levels under control.

To catch sewage backups in time, make sure to schedule regular sewer line inspections and cleanings.

Sewage Cleanup FAQs

  • How dangerous is raw sewage exposure?

    Very dangerous. It contains pathogens that can cause infections, respiratory issues, and more. Always call a professional.

  • Can I clean up a sewage backup myself?

    We don’t recommend it because DIY cleanup can expose you to harmful bacteria and leave hidden contamination behind.

  • Does homeowner’s insurance cover sewage damage?

    In most cases, yes - especially if the damage was sudden. We’ll help manage your claim and work directly with your provider.

  • How long does professional sewage cleanup take?

    Most jobs are completed in 1–3 days, depending on the extent of damage. Restoration may take longer if there’s structural damage.

  • What should I do before the professionals arrive?

    Avoid the area to reduce exposure. Turn off water if safe to do so, and don’t use plumbing fixtures.
