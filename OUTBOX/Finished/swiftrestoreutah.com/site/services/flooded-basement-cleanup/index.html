    Our Flooded Basement Cleanup Process

    When dealing with a flooded basement, safety is our top priority. Before entering any flooded area, we ensure that the area is safe to enter. This involves turning off the power supply and wearing protective gear, such as rubber boots and gloves, to avoid coming into contact with contaminated water.


    Once we have confirmed that it is safe to enter, the next step is to identify the source of the flooding and take immediate action to address it. For instance, if the flooding is caused by a burst pipe, we turn off the water supply. If it's due to heavy rainfall, we may divert water away from your home using sandbags.


    The next step is to remove any standing water. If the water is shallow, it can be removed using a wet-dry vacuum. However, if the water is deep, a submersible pump may be required.


    After removing the water, we dry the area as quickly as possible to limit damage and salvage as much as possible. This can be achieved by using industrial fans and dehumidifiers, and opening windows and doors to improve ventilation. Disinfecting surfaces that have come into contact with floodwater is vital to prevent the growth of mold and other bacteria.


    The next stage is to remove external debris or mud from the flooded area and properly dispose of any damaged items beyond repair, such as furniture or carpeting.


    Finally, repairing the damage caused by the flood is the last step in dealing with a flooded basement cleanup. Swift Restoration and Remodeling can assist with the necessary repairs and restoration to get your basement back to its pre-flood condition.

    The Cost of Flooded Basement Cleanup

    The cost of cleaning up a flooded basement cleanup can vary depending on several factors, including:

    water damage cleanup. A minor flood that only affects a small area of the basement will generally cost less to clean up than a major flood that has affected the entire basement.

  • Type of water: The type of water that has flooded the basement can also impact the cost of cleanup. Clean water from a broken pipe or appliance will generally cost less to clean up than contaminated water (grey or black water) from a sewer backup or floodwaters.
  • Time elapsed: The longer the water sits in your basement, the more damage it can cause, and the more expensive it will be to clean up. Immediate response is crucial to prevent further damage and keep the cleanup cost down.
  • Structural damage: If the flooding has caused significant damage to the structure of your home, such as compromising the foundation or structural walls, the cost of cleanup will likely be higher.
  • Insurance coverage: Whether or not your homeowner's insurance covers the cost of cleanup and restoration can impact the out-of-pocket expenses.
