Water damage can range from minor leaks, like a dripping faucet, to significant issues, such as a water heater burst or major flooding. In all cases, it’s vital to remove the water promptly from your home or office. Any excess water, if left unaddressed, can lead to mold, mildew, or even structural damage to your property. Swift Restoration and Remodeling offers professional water extraction services in Ogden to help homeowners and businesses deal with water damage, ensuring that every last drop of water is removed effectively.


Getting All the Water Out

The removal of leftover water wherever it may have migrated inside the building is referred to as the water extraction procedure. This remaining water that has seeped into walls, floors, and ceilings, as well as materials such as carpet and padding, can be a source of ongoing harm to impacted building components and a catalyst for deadly mold development. It is important to remove any remaining water. It is a technique that necessitates the use of professional equipment and processes created expressly for the purpose:

  • Residual water is extracted from surfaces and absorbent materials using powerful truck-mounted vacuum water extraction equipment. Portable extraction equipment is also used to reach harder-to-reach sections of the house.
  • Professional water extraction also involves technologies such as hygrometers, moisture meters, and other accessories for locating and measuring saturation levels.
  • Infrared imaging may be used to detect concealed water in locations such as wall voids or inside the ceiling framework.
  • Rapid removal of remaining water shortens the drying period of the interior, which in turn reduces mold contamination, which is frequently linked with water damage.

Removing all residual water shortens the drying period, ultimately reducing the risk of mold contamination. A rapid response to water damage

— particularly after a water heater burst or similar incident — is crucial to avoid long-term problems.

After the Water’s Gone

Following thorough water extraction, the drying step commences to remove trace moisture and excessive indoor humidity. To accelerate the evaporation of residual moisture, this phase generally employs industrial-grade dehumidifiers and high-volume air movers. If mold development is probable, impacted areas will be disinfected and air samples will be taken to identify the presence of airborne spores in the residence.

When to Call for Professional Water Extraction

Water extraction should be performed as quickly as possible in any place that has suffered water damage. The longer that water sits on a surface, the more likely it is that issues may emerge. Mold development and decaying wood are also possibilities in the exposed region. As a result, it is critical to contact a professional as soon as possible if you discover a leak of any kind so that it may be repaired without causing long-term harm.


Mold development can potentially lead to a variety of significant health issues if not adequately managed. As a result, it is critical to fully drain the water from your structure using the water extraction method in order to minimize the total drying time. If you see any leaks or floods, call a professional right once.

Common questions

01What is Water Extraction?

Water extraction is only one of the critical phases of a successful water damage restoration job. Once the first and most pressing problem, i.e., halting the source of water, has been addressed, the next priority is water removal. This entails eliminating deep standing water from anyplace in the house (particularly the basement), using submersible pumps or other suction equipment to extract huge quantities of water in a short period of time. Water extraction follows after the majority of the floodwater has been removed from the property.

02Why is Water Extraction Important?

Mold may grow within 24 to 48 hours after a house or workplace has flooded, according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and the Federal Emergency Management Agency ( FEMA). The longer a surface is exposed to water, the more likely it is to grow. Water damage not only has the potential to produce health risks such as mold, but it may also harm the infrastructure and foundation of a building over time since it damages the surfaces on which it develops.
