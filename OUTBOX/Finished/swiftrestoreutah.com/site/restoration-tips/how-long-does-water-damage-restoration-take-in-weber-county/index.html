If you've just found water pooling in your basement or dripping through a ceiling, your first question is probably: how long is this going to take?

Totally fair. At Swift Restoration and Remodeling, we've handled water damage restoration across Weber County for over 15 years, and the honest answer depends on a few key factors. Minor damage from a clean source might wrap up in about a week. A major flood with contaminated water? That can stretch to several weeks.

Let's break down what shapes the timeline so you know what to expect.

Understanding water categories helps set realistic expectations for your restoration timeline.

Beyond water type, the size of the affected area matters. One wet room is a completely different project than three rooms with saturated drywall. And response speed is critical: mold can start growing within 24 to 48 hours, so every hour counts.

The Five Stages of Water Damage Restoration

Every job follows a general sequence, whether it's a small leak in a Kaysville kitchen or a flooded Ogden basement.

1. Inspection and Assessment

A technician inspects the property using moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the full scope of damage. They classify the water category and build a restoration plan. This takes a few hours.

2. Water Extraction

Standing water gets removed with industrial pumps and truck-mounted extraction units. The faster this happens, the less moisture penetrates structural materials. Extraction usually takes a few hours to a full day.

3. Drying and Dehumidification

Here's where patience matters. Commercial air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously while technicians monitor moisture readings daily. Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days. The structure must meet IICRC standard moisture levels before the next phase begins.

4. Cleaning and Sanitizing

Affected areas get treated with antimicrobial solutions and deodorized. For Category 2 or 3 water, this step is essential. If belongings were impacted, contents cleaning may be part of this phase.

5. Reconstruction and Repairs

Damaged drywall, flooring, and baseboards get replaced. This can range from minor patches to a full rebuild and reconstruction project. At Swift Restoration and Remodeling, we handle this in-house so you don't coordinate between separate companies.

The reconstruction phase restores your home to pre-damage condition, or even better.

Seasonal Risk Factors for Weber County

  • Spring:

    Snowmelt runoff, rising water tables, peak basement flooding season

  • Summer:

    Flash thunderstorms can overwhelm gutters and cause sudden leaks

  • Fall:

    Clogged gutters direct water toward foundations

  • Winter:

    Frozen pipes and ice dams are the primary culprits

Spring is our busiest season. As mountain snowpack melts above Ogden Valley, groundwater rises and basements flood. If you're concerned about this season, our post on preventing spring runoff water damage has practical tips you can use now.

How to Speed Up Your Restoration

You can't control everything, but these steps genuinely shorten the timeline:

  1. Shut off the water source

    at the fixture or main shutoff valve.

  2. Call a restoration professional immediately.

    Every hour of standing water extends the project.

  3. Turn off electricity

    to affected areas if safe.

  4. Move furniture and valuables

    away from wet areas.

  5. Document everything with photos and video

    before cleanup begins for your insurance claim.

Common questions

01What Affects the Restoration Timeline?

The IICRC S500 Standard classifies water damage into three categories. Category 1 is clean water from a supply line. Category 2 is gray water from appliances or toilet overflows. Category 3 involves sewage or floodwater with serious health risks. Higher categories require more sanitation and a longer project.

04How long does the drying phase take after water damage?

Structural drying typically takes 3 to 5 days for most residential situations. Severe cases with saturated walls or multiple rooms can take longer. Technicians monitor moisture daily and won't move to reconstruction until readings meet IICRC standards.

05Does the type of water affect restoration time?

Yes. Clean water (Category 1) restores fastest. Gray water (Category 2) requires extra sanitation. Black water (Category 3) from sewage or flooding needs the most extensive protocols and typically adds several days to the timeline.

06Can I stay home during water damage restoration?

For minor, localized damage you can usually stay while equipment runs. For extensive damage involving contaminated water or multiple rooms, it may be safer to stay elsewhere. Your restoration team will advise based on your situation.

07Does spring runoff cause longer restoration timelines in Weber County?

It can. Rising groundwater may re-saturate areas already dried if the source isn't controlled. Restoration companies are also busiest during spring in Northern Utah, which can affect scheduling. Calling early helps you get ahead of the rush.
