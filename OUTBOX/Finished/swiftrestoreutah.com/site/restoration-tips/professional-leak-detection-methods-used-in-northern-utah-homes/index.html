Why Leak Detection Matters in Northern Utah

Northern Utah homes deal with freezing winters that stress plumbing joints, spring snowmelt that pushes groundwater against foundations, and clay-heavy soils that shift with moisture. According to the EPA's WaterSense program, the average home with leaks wastes roughly 9,300 gallons of water every year. But water bills are only part of the problem. Undetected leaks lead to mold growth, structural damage, and costly repairs that early detection would have prevented.

Thermal Imaging Cameras

Thermal imaging is one of the most powerful tools we use. These cameras detect temperature differences across surfaces. When moisture sits inside a wall or floor, evaporative cooling creates a temperature variation that shows up clearly on the camera display.

Thermal imaging reveals temperature differences that point to hidden moisture behind walls and under floors.

This lets us scan large areas quickly without cutting into anything. Our IICRC-certified technicians use thermal imaging during water damage restoration assessments across Weber County. It's especially useful in older homes where pipe runs aren't always where the blueprints say they should be.

Moisture Meters and Moisture Mapping

If thermal imaging is the wide-angle lens, moisture meters are the magnifying glass. These handheld instruments measure the moisture content of specific materials like drywall, wood, and concrete. Pin-type meters use small probes to measure electrical resistance in the material. Pinless meters use electromagnetic signals to detect moisture below the surface without leaving a mark.

Once we've identified wet zones, we create a moisture map documenting exactly where levels are elevated and how far damage extends. That information is critical for building an accurate restoration plan, and it's essential documentation if you're filing an insurance claim for water damage.

Video Pipe Inspection

Some leaks come from the drain side, where cracked sewer pipes, tree root intrusion, or deteriorating joints allow water to seep into the soil around your foundation. A small, waterproof camera on a flexible cable is fed into the pipe, sending a live video feed to a monitor so we can see cracks, blockages, and root intrusion in real time.

A video camera provides real-time visuals of the interior condition of sewer and drain lines.

This method is particularly valuable during spring in Northern Utah. Saturated soil from snowmelt pushes against aging sewer lines, widening cracks and separating joints. If you've noticed slow drains or wet patches in your yard, video inspection can confirm whether the issue is a damaged pipe rather than a simple clog.

Acoustic Leak Detection

Water escaping a pressurized pipe creates sound, but often it's too subtle for human ears. Acoustic detection equipment amplifies those frequencies using sensitive ground microphones. As the sensor moves across the suspected area, changes in sound intensity help pinpoint the leak's exact location.

This method works best on pressurized supply lines and is especially useful for slab leaks, where a pipe running beneath your home's concrete foundation develops a break. Catching slab leaks early saves thousands in foundation repair and flooded basement cleanup costs.

  • There are warm or damp spots on floors

04Can thermal imaging detect leaks behind tile walls?

Yes. Thermal imaging picks up temperature differences caused by moisture, even behind tile and stone. The cooler zone shows up clearly on the display, letting our technicians identify the problem without removing any tile.

05How long does a leak detection inspection take?

Most inspections take between one and three hours depending on the size of the home. Simple cases may be resolved in under an hour, while slab leaks or multi-story homes may need more time.

06What is the most common cause of hidden leaks in Ogden area homes?

The most common hidden leaks come from aging supply line connections, particularly in homes built between the 1970s and 1990s. Temperature swings from harsh winters stress pipe joints over time, and spring snowmelt can aggravate foundation-level plumbing issues. Regular inspections can catch these problems before they lead to serious water damage from snowmelt and spring runoff.
