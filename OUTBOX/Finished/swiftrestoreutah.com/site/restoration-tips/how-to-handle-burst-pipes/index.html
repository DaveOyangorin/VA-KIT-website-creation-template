Burst Pipes

Darin Jenks

Burst pipes are one of the most common and sudden water damage scenarios in homes and businesses. When a pipe ruptures, it can lead to flooding, structural damage, mold growth, and ultimately water damage restoration services in Ogden, UT. Understanding why pipes burst and how to respond quickly can help keep damage to a minimum and prevent costly repairs. In this blog post, we’ll explore why pipes burst, what to do in an emergency, and how to prevent future incidents.

Reasons Pipes Burst: Common Factors

What causes a pipe to suddenly burst? Several factors lead to this outcome, and recognizing the risks and signs of water damage can help homeowners take preventive measures. So, let's take a closer look:

Below Zero Conditions

One of the most common reasons pipes burst is freezing weather. But why do frozen pipes burst

? Falling temperatures can cause water inside pipes to turn to ice and expand. This creates pressure within the pipe, eventually causing it to crack or even burst. Pipes that are most vulnerable to freezing include:

  • Those in unheated basements, attics, and crawl spaces
  • Outdoor hoses and sprinkler lines
  • Pipes near exterior walls with little insulation

High Water Pressure

Intense water pressure can weaken pipes over time and lead to sudden bursts. Keep in mind that normal household water pressure should be between 40-60 psi (pounds per square inch). When pressure breaches this range, it stresses the pipes and fittings, making them more likely to fail. Common causes of high water pressure include:

  • Malfunctioning pressure regulators
  • Blockages in the plumbing system
  • Sudden surges from the municipal water supply

Rust and Aging Pipes

Whether due to corrosion, rust, or mineral buildup, it’s undeniable that pipes deteriorate over time. Older homes with galvanized steel or copper pipes are especially prone to corrosion-related failures. When pipes become weak or brittle, the chances are they’ll crack under normal water pressure. Signs of corroding pipes include:

  • Discolored or metallic-tasting water
  • Frequent leaks or small cracks
  • Green or white residue on copper pipes

Emergency Response: What to Do When a Pipe Bursts

A burst pipe can quickly turn into a disaster, but taking the right steps can help minimize water damage. Here’s what you should do immediately:

If water is near electrical outlets or appliances, turn off power to those areas at the circuit breaker to avoid electrical hazards.

After shutting off the water, open all faucets in your home to drain any remaining water and reduce pressure in the pipes.

Use towels, mops, and wet/dry vacuums to soak up excess water. The faster you remove water, the lower the risk of structural damage and mold growth.

Contact a professional water damage restoration company like Swift Restoration and Remodeling to assess the water damage and begin repairs at once.

Long-Term Solution: How to Prevent Burst Pipes

Taking proactive steps can help you avoid the stress and expense of dealing with burst pipes.

Monitor your home’s water pressure and install a pressure regulator if needed. If you notice signs of high pressure, contact a plumber for an inspection.

During freezing temperatures, allow faucets to drip slightly to keep water moving, reducing the risk of freezing.

Have a professional inspect your plumbing system once a year to check for corrosion, leaks, and weak spots before they become a problem.
