Cost of Fire Damage Restoration Services in Ogden 2026

Get a Quote (385) 412-8633

Darin Jenks

A house fire stops everything. Once the flames are out, the next question homeowners in Ogden, Layton, and across Weber County almost always ask is the same one: how much is this going to cost?

Figuring out fire damage restoration services pricing before you're in the middle of it can save you from serious sticker shock. We've put together this guide to walk you through what you're actually paying for, why numbers vary so much, and what you can realistically expect in 2026.

We're Swift Restoration and Remodeling, and after 15 years of working fire jobs across northern Utah, we've seen the full range. Small kitchen fires. Total losses. Everything in between. The breakdown below reflects real-world field experience, not national averages from a spreadsheet.

Timing plays a role too. Corrosive soot residue starts degrading surfaces within hours. The longer restoration is delayed, the deeper smoke odor embeds into walls, ceilings, and ductwork. That's not a sales pitch. It's just chemistry.

8 Fire Damage Restoration Costs, Broken Down

Here are the individual components of a full restoration and what they typically cost in the Ogden market in 2026. Your actual quote depends on your specific property.

1. Emergency Board-Up and Property Securing

After a fire, windows may be broken and walls may be compromised. Emergency board-up services in northern Utah typically run between $300 and $1,200 depending on how many openings need coverage. This protects your home from weather and further damage while a restoration plan is developed.

Emergency board-up is often the first step after a fire, protecting your property from weather and further damage while restoration planning begins.

2. Smoke and Soot Removal

Smoke travels far beyond the fire's origin, settling into walls, furniture, ductwork, and insulation throughout the home. Professional smoke and soot removal generally costs $2,000 to $6,000 for a mid-sized home. This phase involves dry-chemical sponges, HEPA vacuuming, and specialized post-fire cleaning agents. According to the USFA's guide to recovering after a fire, improper cleaning can permanently damage materials that could otherwise be salvaged.

3. Odor Elimination

Smoke smell doesn't go away with regular cleaning products. Odor removal is its own phase, often involving thermal fogging, ozone treatment, or hydroxyl generators. Standalone odor elimination runs $500 to $3,500. For severe fires, it's typically bundled into the broader restoration scope. Skipping this step, or trying to mask it with air fresheners, is one of the most common mistakes we see.

4. Content Cleaning and Pack-Out

Your belongings need attention too. Furniture, clothing, electronics, and personal items can often be restored if addressed quickly. Content cleaning and pack-out services, where items are removed, catalogued, professionally cleaned off-site, and stored during restoration, typically run $1,000 to $5,000 depending on volume and damage level.

5. Structural Drying from Firefighting Water

Firefighters use a lot of water. After a significant fire, water damage from suppression efforts is almost always part of the picture. Structural drying with industrial dehumidifiers typically costs $1,500 to $4,000. Left untreated, that moisture becomes a mold problem within 24 to 48 hours. We've covered this in detail in our complete guide to fire damage for Ogden homeowners.

6. Structural Repairs

Once smoke, soot, and moisture are handled, repair work begins. This covers drywall, framing, flooring, ceilings, and anything the fire compromised structurally. A contained kitchen fire might need $3,000 to $8,000 in repairs. A fire that spread through multiple rooms or into the attic can push structural work into the $20,000 to $50,000 range or higher. Our reconstruction services team handles this as part of the full scope, which keeps the project on a single timeline with clear accountability.

7. HVAC Cleaning and Ductwork

Smoke moves through your HVAC system quickly. Duct cleaning after a fire is not optional if you want the odor actually gone and want clean air circulating once your system runs again. Professional HVAC cleaning and duct decontamination in northern Utah runs $800 to $2,500 depending on system size and smoke infiltration level.

8. Full Reconstruction and Rebuild

For the most severe fires, the job ends with a significant rebuild: new flooring, new cabinetry, new drywall, repainting, sometimes entirely new room configurations. Full reconstruction typically starts at $25,000 for a modest single-room rebuild and can exceed $100,000 for whole-home restorations. Our team has documented both ends of that range, from the Roy kitchen fire project to our complete Ogden home transformation. Every job is different, which is why every estimate needs to be custom.

Total Cost Context

A complete restoration for a mid-sized Ogden home with moderate fire damage often totals $15,000 to $50,000 when all phases are combined. Total losses can exceed this significantly. An on-site assessment is the only way to get an accurate number.

Most standard Utah homeowners insurance policies cover fire damage restoration, but the claims process often requires careful documentation and a contractor experienced in working with adjusters.

That said, "covered" doesn't mean the process is simple. Insurance claims for fire damage can get complicated. Adjusters assess damage on their own standards, which don't always align with what a restoration contractor actually sees on-site. Disputes over scope, repair methods, and material costs are more common than most homeowners expect going in.

Our team works directly with insurance companies on fire claims and has helped homeowners in Ogden, Farmington, Kaysville, and throughout Davis County navigate this process and document damage thoroughly. The Ready.gov home fire recovery resources also outline documentation steps worth reviewing before you meet with an adjuster.

How to Choose the Right Fire Restoration Contractor in Ogden

Price matters, but it's not the only factor worth evaluating. Here's what to look for when choosing a contractor in northern Utah:

  • IICRC certification:

    The IICRC S700 standard governs fire and smoke damage restoration. Swift Restoration and Remodeling is IICRC-certified, meaning our team works to recognized industry protocols.

  • Full-service capability:

    A contractor who handles everything from board-up through final rebuild keeps the job moving without accountability gaps between separate companies.

  • Insurance experience:

    Ask directly whether they work with your insurer and handle documentation. This is a practical question, not a small one.

  • Local track record:

    Someone familiar with northern Utah construction, older home materials, and regional building codes will handle your job better than an out-of-area firm.

  • Written estimates:

    Any reputable company will provide a written estimate breaking down phases. Walk away from verbal-only quotes on a fire job.

Common questions

01What Actually Affects Fire Restoration Costs?

Fire restoration is not one-size-fits-all. The final number depends on the size of the fire, which rooms were affected, how deeply smoke and soot penetrated the structure, whether there's water damage from firefighting suppression efforts, and whether hazardous materials like older lead paint are involved.

03Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Fire Damage Restoration?

In most cases, yes. Standard homeowners insurance policies in Utah typically cover fire damage, including smoke and soot removal, structural repairs, content cleaning, and temporary housing if your home becomes uninhabitable.

04How much does fire damage restoration cost in Ogden, UT in 2026?

Fire damage restoration in Ogden ranges from roughly $5,000 to $15,000 for small contained fires, $15,000 to $50,000 for moderate multi-room damage, and $100,000 or more for severe or whole-home losses. The only way to get an accurate number is an on-site assessment by a licensed restoration contractor.

05Does homeowners insurance cover fire damage restoration in Utah?

Yes, standard homeowners insurance in Utah typically covers fire damage restoration, including smoke removal, structural repairs, content cleaning, and temporary housing if needed. The claims process can involve disputes over scope and costs, so working with a restoration company experienced in insurance documentation helps homeowners get fair outcomes.

06Why does fire restoration end up costing more than expected?

Because fire restoration involves more than cleaning visible damage. Smoke penetrates walls, ceilings, and ductwork throughout the home. Firefighting water creates moisture that must be dried professionally to prevent mold. Contents need pack-out and off-site cleaning. When all phases are combined, the total often surprises homeowners who only accounted for what the fire visibly damaged.

07How long does fire damage restoration take in the Ogden area?

Smoke and soot removal with minor repairs may take one to two weeks. Moderate jobs involving structural repairs typically run three to six weeks. Full reconstruction after a major fire can take several months. Your restoration contractor should provide a phase-by-phase timeline as part of the written estimate.
