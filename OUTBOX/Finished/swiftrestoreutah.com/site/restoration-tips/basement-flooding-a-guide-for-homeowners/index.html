Basement Flooding

Get a Quote (385) 412-8633

Darin Jenks

Basement flooding is more than just a frustrating inconvenience - it’s a major contributor to home water damage that can lead to costly repairs and long-term problems like mold growth, structural issues, and ruined belongings. If your basement has ever taken on water, you know how quickly a small leak can turn into a big headache. For homeowners in Ogden, UT, where seasonal weather and old foundations are common, understanding the causes of basement flooding and how to prevent it is essential.

In this blog post, we’ll explain everything you need to know about the causes, risks, and prevention, and how Swift Restoration and Remodeling can help with expert flooded basement cleanup in Ogden.

Poor Drainage Around the Foundation

When rainwater or melting snow isn’t directed away from your home properly, it can pool around the foundation. And the result? Water seeping into the basement through the walls or floors. Clogged gutters, downspouts that don’t extend far enough from the house, and improper grading are all frequent causes of poor drainage.

Foundation Cracks

Even the tiniest cracks in your foundation can allow water to enter your basement. As the soil around your home expands and contracts with weather changes, it can put stress on the foundation, creating or worsening these cracks. Water finds the weakest point, and a crack in your wall or floor is an open invitation.

Sump Pump Failure

A sump pump is designed to remove excess water from your basement during heavy rain or snowmelt. If the pump malfunctions due to mechanical failure, power outages, or lack of maintenance, your basement could quickly flood.

Heavy Rain or Snowmelt

Sudden weather events, like a thunderstorm or rapid snowmelt, can overwhelm your home’s drainage system. If the water has nowhere to go, it may end up inside your basement.

Sewer Backup

If your sewer system becomes backed up, it can cause dirty water to flow back into your home through drains. This is not only damaging but also poses serious health risks.

Preventing mold after water damage is crucial because mold thrives in damp environments and can begin to grow within two days of water exposure. It can damage materials and pose health hazards, especially for people with allergies or breathing difficulties.

Items stored in the basement, like furniture, documents, electronics, and keepsakes, can be permanently damaged by flood water.

Flooding can affect wiring and electrical systems, increasing the risk of short circuits or fires.

Prevention Tips: How to Protect Your Basement

While not every flood can be prevented, many can be avoided with a few proactive steps:

Install a Reliable Sump Pump

Sump pump failures are one of the leading causes of water damage in homes, so you’ll want to have it working properly. Make sure your sump pump is installed the right way and that it’s tested regularly. You may wonder: How do I know if my sump pump is working properly? Test your sump pump by pouring water into the pit. If the pump turns on and removes the water, it’s working. You can also add a battery backup to ensure no glitches during power outages.

Maintain Proper Drainage

Keep gutters clean and extend downspouts at least 6 feet away from your home’s foundation. Make sure the soil slopes away from your house to direct water away naturally.

Seal Foundation Cracks

Inspect your basement walls and floor regularly. Make sure to seal any cracks with hydraulic cement or have a professional evaluate the need for more serious foundation repair.

Get a Backwater Valve

To prevent sewage from backing up into your basement, installing a backwater valve on your main sewer line could be a good solution.

Waterproof Your Basement

Consider professional basement waterproofing services, including interior drain tiles, vapor barriers, and wall coatings.
