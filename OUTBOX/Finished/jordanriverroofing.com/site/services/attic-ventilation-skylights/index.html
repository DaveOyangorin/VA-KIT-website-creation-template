Why attic ventilation matters

An attic that can’t breathe gets very hot in summer and holds moisture in winter. That heat and moisture shorten the life of shingles and roof decking, can lead to mold, and help ice dams form along the eaves in a Utah winter. Balanced ventilation, with air coming in low at the soffits and leaving high at the ridge, keeps the attic closer to the outside temperature.

Ridge vents, not a patchwork

Many older homes have a row of box (“turtle”) vents. When we re-roof, we’ll talk through whether a continuous ridge vent with soffit intake is the better design for your attic. One customer, Kevin, wrote that the crew “helped me achieve a fully ventilated attic space.” Our guide explains the trade-offs: Ridge Vent vs. Turtle Vents: Which Is Better?.

Skylights

A skylight brings daylight into a room, but only if it stays watertight. We install new skylights and repair existing ones: worn flashing, failed seals and leaks around the frame.

Signs to look for

  • Hot, stuffy upstairs rooms in summer
  • Ice dams along the eaves in winter
  • Condensation or fogging between skylight panes
  • Ceiling stains or mildew in upper rooms
  • Musty smells in the attic

Questions

Attic Ventilation & Skylights: common questions

Should I keep my turtle vents when I add a ridge vent?

Usually not. The vent manufacturer Air Vent advises against mixing different types of exhaust vents over one attic, because it can short-circuit the airflow. More in our guide, Ridge Vent vs. Turtle Vents: Which Is Better?.

Can you fix a leaking skylight?

Yes. We repair skylight leaks, including worn flashing and failed weather seals, and replace skylights when repair isn’t worth it.
