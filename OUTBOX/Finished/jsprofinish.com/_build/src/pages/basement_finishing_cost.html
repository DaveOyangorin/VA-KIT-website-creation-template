What moves the price

Two basements with the same square footage can price thousands apart. These are the variables that matter.

Adding a bathroom

The single biggest price swing. If your builder left a rough-in in the slab (capped drain pipes sticking out of the concrete), a bathroom is far more affordable. Cutting concrete for new plumbing runs adds real money; published guides put new plumbing at $8,000 to $15,000 or more.

Egress windows

Every legal bedroom needs one. If the window opening exists, it's a modest line item. Cutting a new opening through the foundation and digging a window well costs more, and it's not optional if you want the room to count as a bedroom.

Ceiling height and soffits

Ducts, beams, and plumbing under the floor above have to be boxed in. Older homes in Davis and Weber County with 7 to 7.5 foot ceilings take more planning and framing labor than newer 9 foot basements.

Square footage

More area means more of everything, but the cost per square foot usually drops a little as size goes up, because fixed costs like permits and mobilization spread across more space.

What's already there

A clean, dry, open basement is the cheapest starting point. Tear-out of old paneling or DIY framing, moisture problems, or outdated wiring all add prep work before the finish can start.

Wet bars and kitchenettes

Cabinets, counters, a sink, and the plumbing to feed it. Worth it for a space you'll entertain in, but it moves the project up a tier.

What's different about Davis and Weber County basements

Parts of Davis County sit on a high water table, especially near the Great Salt Lake in Syracuse, West Point, and parts of Clearfield. Moisture and drainage get checked before any finish work starts, and if a sump pump or drainage fix is needed, it belongs in the budget up front, not discovered behind drywall later.

The Wasatch bench that runs through Kaysville, Farmington, Bountiful, east Layton, and Weber County's east side means a lot of walk-out and daylight basements. Those finish beautifully but carry extra window, door, and insulation detail on the daylight side.

Every city we work in requires a building permit for basement finishing, and each city sets its own fee based on project value. Bedrooms need code-compliant egress windows everywhere. We handle the permits and inspections, and they're included in the written estimate.

Basement finishing cost questions

What does basement finishing cost per square foot in Utah?+

Published Utah cost guides put most projects in a range that depends almost entirely on scope: a straightforward finish with no bathroom sits at the low end, adding a bathroom and bedroom lands mid-range, and custom features push it higher. The table on this page shows the ranges. For your basement, we measure and give you a free written estimate with the price fixed before work starts.

Does adding a bathroom double the cost?+

No, but it's the biggest single variable. If your basement has a plumbing rough-in already in the slab, which most newer Davis County homes do, a bathroom is a manageable add. If concrete has to be cut for new drain lines, it costs meaningfully more. We check for a rough-in during the estimate.

Do I need a permit, and what does it cost?+

Yes. Every city we work in, including Clearfield, Layton, Syracuse, Ogden, and Roy, requires a building permit for basement finishing, and bedrooms need code-compliant egress windows. Each city sets its own permit fee based on project value. We handle the permit and inspections, and it's accounted for in your written estimate.

What's the most cost-effective way to finish a basement?+

Keep the layout simple, put bathrooms near the existing rough-in, and spend where you touch the space daily: flooring, lighting, and paint. A basement's footprint and foundation already exist, which is why finishing one is usually the cheapest way to add living space to a Utah home.

Do you offer financing?+

No. We don't sell financing, we give you a number you can plan around: a free on-site estimate, in writing, with the price fixed before work starts. No payment surprises mid-project.

Why do estimates from different contractors vary so much?+

Usually scope and what's left out. A low bid that excludes permits, egress work, or moisture prep isn't cheaper, it just bills you later. Our estimates are written, itemized, and fixed, so you can compare line by line.
