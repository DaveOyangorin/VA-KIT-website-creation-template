The Best Time of Year to Replace a Roof in Utah

What Salt Lake City’s weather data and shingle makers say about timing a new roof.

By EZ RoofingPublished 17 Oct 20267 min read
Free Inspection (888) 575-3025
Short answer: in Salt Lake City, late spring and early fall are the easiest times to replace a roof. NOAA’s climate normals show highs at or above 40°F on nearly every day from March through October, and very little snow after March. Winter replacements are possible on mild days, with extra cold-weather steps. Summer works too, with early starts to beat the heat.

A new roof is a big job, so it’s fair to wonder whether the calendar matters. In Utah it does, a little. Not because roofs can only go on in one season, but because temperature, snow and heat change how the work is done.

Here’s what the weather data says month by month, what shingle makers say about cold-weather installs, and how to pick the right window for your home.

Two numbers stand out. December and January average only about 14 days with a high of 40°F or more. From March through October, it’s nearly every day.

Why 40°F matters for asphalt shingles

Most asphalt shingles have a strip of sealant on the back that bonds each shingle to the course below. Owens Corning, whose shingles we install as a Preferred Contractor, explains in its cold-weather technical bulletin that direct sun activates that thermally activated sealant.

The bulletin treats installation at or below 40°F (4°C) as cold-weather work. In those conditions, shingles “may not immediately seal until warmer temperatures are present,” and they may take longer to lay flat. Roof slope, which way the roof faces, and the daily temperature during and after the install all change how long sealing takes.

So cold doesn’t forbid a new roof. It changes the method. Owens Corning recommends:

  • Hand sealing every shingle along the roof perimeter with dabs of ASTM D4586 asphalt roof cement, to protect against wind uplift before the strip activates.
  • Leaving a small gap, no more than 1/16 inch, at shingle end joints so they don’t buckle when they expand in warm weather.
  • Taking extra care when handling shingles in the cold.

Spring in Utah: good weather, but book early

March and April bring highs in the mid-50s to low 60s on average, and snow mostly tapers off after March. That makes spring a comfortable time for crews and materials alike.

Spring is also when many homeowners find out what winter did to their roof: lifted shingles, leaks around flashing, or damage from heavy snow. If you notice problems after the thaw, start with an inspection. Our guide to signs it’s time to replace your roof covers what to look for.

Summer: long days and early starts

June through August are dry in Salt Lake City, with well under an inch of precipitation a month on average. That’s helpful for a job that opens up your roof.

The trade-off is heat. NOAA’s normals show about 24 days in July with a high of 90°F or more. Hot afternoons make for hard roof work, so ask your roofer how they schedule around the heat. It isn’t a reason to wait. It just shapes the work day.

Fall: the sweet spot for many homes

September and October average highs of about 81°F and 66°F, with almost no snow. Shingles still get enough warmth to seal before winter, and your new roof is in place before the snowiest months.

November is a transition. Highs average about 51°F and there are usually still around 25 days that reach 40°F, but the first real snowstorms often arrive. If you’re planning a fall replacement, earlier is easier.

Winter: possible, with the right precautions

Roofs do get replaced in Utah winters, especially when a leak or storm damage can’t wait. Work happens on clear, milder days, with the cold-weather steps above. Snow and ice on the roof, or wet decking, are what stop a job, so expect some schedule flexibility.

If your roof is leaking in January, don’t wait for spring. A repair or temporary fix now can prevent water damage inside, and the full replacement can follow when weather allows. Our winter guide, protecting your home during snowstorms, has more on cold-season care.

Is any season cheaper?

It’s one of the most common questions people ask Google about roofing. Honestly, we haven’t found reliable Utah data showing that one season costs less, so we won’t claim a seasonal discount.

What changes the price far more is the roof itself: its size, pitch and number of layers, the materials you choose, and whether the decking needs repair. The best way to save is to compare clear, written estimates that spell out exactly what’s included.

The real answer: replace it when it needs replacing

The calendar matters less than your roof’s condition. A roof with active leaks or storm damage shouldn’t wait for the perfect month. A roof that’s aging but sound gives you time to plan for spring or fall.

Questions about timing a roof replacement

Can a roof be replaced in winter in Utah?

Yes, on many days. Owens Corning treats 40°F and below as cold-weather installation and recommends extra steps, such as hand sealing shingles along the roof perimeter with asphalt roof cement. Snow, ice and wet decking are what actually stop work, so winter jobs get scheduled around storms.

What is the cheapest time of year to replace a roof?

We haven’t found reliable Utah data showing one season is cheaper, so we won’t promise a discount by month. Price depends far more on roof size, pitch, materials and how much tear-off is needed. Ask for a written estimate and compare what each one includes.

How long does a roof replacement take?

It depends on the roof’s size and pitch, the weather, and whether the decking needs repairs. Ask your roofer for a written timeline before the job is booked.

Is fall or spring better for a new roof in Salt Lake City?

Both work well. NOAA’s normals show highs at or above 40°F on almost every day from March through October, with little snow after March. Fall has the extra benefit of getting the work done before the snowiest months.
