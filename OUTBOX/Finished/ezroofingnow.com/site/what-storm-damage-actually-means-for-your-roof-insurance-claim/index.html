What “Storm Damage” Actually Means for Your Roof Insurance Claim

By EZ RoofingPublished 2026-04-20
Free Inspection (888) 575-3025

When people hear “storm damage,” they picture a tree through the roof or shingles scattered across the yard. But the storm damage that insurance actually covers — and that leads to thousands of dollars in claim payouts for Arkansas homeowners — often looks a lot less dramatic than that.

Here’s what you actually need to know about storm damage, what insurance looks for, and why getting a professional inspection matters more than you might think.

What Counts as “Storm Damage” for Insurance Purposes?

Insurance policies typically cover damage caused by sudden, accidental events — including:

  • Hail impact — even small hail (1″ or less) can cause significant granule loss, crack shingles, and dent metal components in ways that void your manufacturer’s warranty and compromise your roof’s ability to shed water
  • Wind damage — high winds can lift, crack, or remove shingles and damage flashing; Arkansas sees frequent severe wind events, especially during spring storm season
  • Falling debris — branches, limbs, and other debris driven by storm winds
  • Ice and water intrusion — in colder months, ice dams and freeze-thaw cycles can force water under shingles

What insurance doesn’t cover is wear and tear — gradual deterioration from age and normal exposure. This is why the timing of your inspection matters: damage needs to be connected to a specific weather event, not general aging.

Why Hail Damage Is So Often Missed by Homeowners

The most common and most misunderstood form of storm damage in Arkansas is hail impact. From the ground, a hail-hit roof can look completely normal. But up close, the signs are there:

  • Soft, circular dents in shingles where hail compressed the granule layer
  • Exposed asphalt in circular patterns (the granules were knocked loose)
  • Dented gutters, downspouts, vents, and AC units — these are often the clearest indicators of hail size and coverage
  • Cracked or split shingles along the impact point

An experienced inspector knows exactly what adjusters look for — because we work directly with insurance companies on claims. EZ Roofing uses third-party lab analysis (NTS or ITEL) to document material-specific damage, giving your claim the strongest possible foundation.

How the Insurance Claim Process Actually Works

Here’s something that surprises many homeowners: the insurance adjuster’s estimate largely determines the scope and price of your repair — not competing contractor bids. This means the most important thing you can do is ensure the adjuster sees all the damage and documents it correctly.

EZ Roofing attends every adjuster meeting with our clients. We bring documentation, measurements, and third-party analysis to ensure nothing is missed. Insurance companies respond to evidence — and we know how to present it.

The claim process typically looks like this:

  • Free inspection by EZ Roofing — we document all damage with photos and measurements
  • You file the claim — we help you through this step
  • Adjuster meeting — we attend with you and advocate for a complete, accurate scope
  • Approval — most claims are approved within 7–14 days; your insurer sends an ACV (Actual Cash Value) check
  • Work is completed — after installation, we invoice your insurance for the remaining recoverable depreciation

The Depreciation Window — Why You Shouldn’t Wait

Insurance companies pay claims in two parts. The first check covers the Actual Cash Value (ACV) — the depreciated value of your old roof. The second, larger payment — the recoverable depreciation — is only released after work is completed and your contractor submits an invoice.

Most policies give you a limited window — sometimes 6 to 12 months from the date of loss — to collect that second payment. If you wait too long to act, that money disappears.

Beyond the depreciation window, deductibles are also climbing. Many Arkansas homeowners now face deductibles of 1–2% of their home’s insured value. On a $300,000 home, that’s up to $6,000 out of pocket. Waiting for the next storm before addressing existing damage only increases your exposure.

What to Do After a Storm in Arkansas

  • Don’t get on the roof yourself — let a professional assess safely
  • Document what you can from the ground — photos of the yard, gutters, and any visible shingle damage
  • Call EZ Roofing for a same-day free inspection — we’ll tell you exactly what’s there and whether it qualifies for a claim
  • Don’t sign anything with any contractor before your inspection — compare reliability, reputation, and credentials, not bids
