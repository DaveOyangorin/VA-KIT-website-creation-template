Architectural, industrial and commercial installations

A decorative garden pond and an irrigation reservoir need very different liners. We look at size, depth, what the water will touch, sun exposure and how the site is accessed before we recommend a membrane. Then we install it with the seams and edges done properly, because that's where liners fail.

Already have a liner that's leaking? The same skills we use for roof waterproofing apply. We can often find and repair the problem.

Planning a pond liner project

A liner is only as good as the ground under it and the way it's sized and joined. Here's what we look at before we order material.

Size and depth

A common rule of thumb for sizing a flexible liner is the pond's length plus twice its deepest depth, plus extra at each edge for anchoring, and the same for the width. Shelves, waterfalls and streams add more. We measure the finished excavation, not the plan, so there are no short edges.

What goes under the liner

Rocks, roots and sharp gravel will puncture a liner from below over time. The base gets cleared and smoothed, and a protective underlayment goes down before the membrane.

What the water touches

Fish and plants, irrigation water and industrial catchments all put different demands on a membrane. That's one of the biggest factors in choosing between rubber and thermoplastic liners.

Seams and edges

On large ponds the liner is made from several panels. Seams and the anchored edges are where liners fail, so that's where we spend the most care.

Already have a pond that's losing water? Tell us how fast the level drops and where the liner is visible. Leaks are often at the edges, around a waterfall or at a seam, and many can be repaired.

Pond and liner questions

What pond liner materials do you install?

We work with thermosets, including EPDM, neoprene and butyl, and with thermoplastics such as CSPE and PVC, plus other copolymers on the market. The right one depends on the size of the pond, what it holds and how it will be used.

Do you install liners for large commercial or agricultural ponds?

Yes. Our listed projects run from a 1,000 square foot feature to the 100,000 square foot Assey Pond in Orem. We install liners for architectural, industrial and commercial uses, including catchments and reservoirs.

Why would a roofing company install pond liners?

A pond liner and a flat roof membrane solve the same problem: keeping water exactly where you want it. We understand these membranes, how they're joined and how they behave in heat and cold, so the skills carry straight over.
