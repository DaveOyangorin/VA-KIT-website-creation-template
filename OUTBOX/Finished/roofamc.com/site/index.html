Roofers in Salt Lake City

Your roof is the first line of defense

The airport alone averages about 65 inches of snow a year, and the benches get closer to 100. Add July sun at 4,300 feet and storms that roll off the Great Salt Lake, and you can see why roofs here wear out the way they do.

Salt Lake City has harsh winters. Without a sound roof you aren't protected, so it has to be taken care of properly: the right shingles or membrane, real waterproofing, and drainage that moves snowmelt off the roof instead of under it.

That's the work we do. As a roofing contractor in Salt Lake City we handle roof repair, new roofs and re-roofing for homes and commercial buildings, plus waterproofing and even pond liners. Same crew, same standard, whether it's a family home or a flat roof downtown.

Roofing materials we install

Four materials that work in Utah

The right pick depends on your home, your budget and how long you plan to stay. Here's the short version.

Most common

Asphalt shingles

Dozens of colors, fits almost any house and costs less than the others. Warranties usually run 20 to 30 years.

Longest life

Metal panels & tiles

The fastest-growing home roofing material. Goes up quickly, needs little care and may carry a lifetime warranty.

Heavy duty

Concrete & clay tile

Lasts like metal and can mimic wood or slate. It's heavy, so the framing has to be checked first.

Classic look

Wood shakes

Thick shakes take hail well and look great. Fire concerns have made them less common.

Winter in the valley

Snow isn't the problem. Meltwater is.

Heat leaking into the attic melts the snow on top. The water runs down, freezes again at the cold eaves and builds an ice dam that pushes water up under the shingles.

The fix starts inside: air sealing, insulation and venting that keeps the roof deck cold. Then good flashing, clear gutters and drains that work. Lake-effect snowbands hit the valley several times a winter, so this isn't a once-in-a-decade problem here.
