Questions we answer before we quote a commercial roof

A good commercial roof bid starts with the building, not a price sheet. These are the questions our roofers work through on every job:

  • Is there enough insulation below and above the deck?
  • Does the roof need more reflectivity?
  • Do the existing slopes drain properly, or should they be steepened toward the drains?
  • Does the drainage system need more primary or secondary drains?

Get those right and the roof lasts. Get them wrong and even a brand-new membrane will pond water and fail early.

More than moisture control

Commercial roofs do more than keep water out. Some clients want a rooftop garden or photovoltaic (PV) panels. Others have HVAC units, satellite dishes and the technicians who service them walking across the roof every month.

As a commercial roofing contractor serving Salt Lake City, we check that work by other trades doesn't damage your roofing system, and we plan for the loads and foot traffic up front.

Five things to weigh before a new commercial roof

When we take on any job, we consider these five factors, and we'd encourage you to do the same.

1. Building construction

Know what the building and the existing roof structure are made of. That tells you whether the support structure can stay or should be replaced with something better suited to the roofing material you choose. It's often a good idea to upgrade the insulation when you replace the roof, because it can lower the building's energy costs a lot.

2. Investment objectives

Commercial property can have any number of goals. Knowing yours sets a budget that makes sense and still earns a return when you sell. Ask yourself: How long do I plan to own the property? What length of warranty do I want? What's my budget, and do I have the capital now?

3. Property use

What happens inside affects the roof you choose. Will there be regular roof traffic, or only maintenance workers? What would a leak damage, and what would it cost if it ruined inventory or equipment?

4. Location

Here in the valley, that means heavy snow loads, freeze-thaw cycles and strong sun at elevation. Reflectivity matters too: the U.S. Department of Energy's Guide to Cool Roofs explains how solar reflectance and thermal emittance decide how hot a roof gets, and notes that many roofs lose reflectivity over time.

5. Building codes

Different cities have different codes. You need to know the ones that apply to commercial roofing in Salt Lake City and the surrounding area before you settle on materials or a design. We're familiar with local codes and can help with this step.

Commercial roofing questions

Do you work on flat and pitched commercial roofs?

Yes. We handle most commercial roofing needs, whether it's a flat roof on a strip center or a pitched roof on an office building. We check insulation, reflectivity and drainage at the same time, so the new roof works with the building instead of fighting it.

Can you re-roof without shutting down my business?

In most cases, yes. We know how important it is to keep your business running, so we plan the work in stages and around your hours. Tell us what can't be disrupted, like a loading dock or a busy entrance, and we'll schedule around it.

What is a commercial roof maintenance program?

It's a planned schedule of inspections and small repairs. We check seams, flashing, drains and any damage from other trades on the roof, then fix small problems before they turn into leaks. The goal is a longer roof life and fewer surprise repair bills.

Should we add insulation when we replace the roof?

Often, yes. Re-roofing is the cheapest time to add insulation above the deck because the roof is already open. It can noticeably lower a building's energy costs. We'll look at what's there now and tell you whether an upgrade makes sense.

Do you help with commercial roofing building codes?

Yes. Commercial roofs in Salt Lake City and nearby cities have to meet local building codes. We're familiar with them and can help you choose materials and a design that meet code from the start.
