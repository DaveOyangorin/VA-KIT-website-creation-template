Signs & Symptoms

When your mobile home isn’t level, it can cause a range of issues, which may require home foundation repair. These problems often begin small but can worsen over time. Common signs that your mobile home may need leveling include:

  • Uneven floors that slope or dip
  • Doors and windows that don’t close properly
  • Cracks in walls or ceilings

In extreme cases, neglecting these signs can result in severe damage to your home's structure, including compromised supports and essential syst ems.

Common questions

01What causes my mobile home to become unlevel?

Over time, mobile homes settle due to soil shifting, moisture changes, or other environmental factors, necessitating home releveling. Ground settling can impact the mobile home's foundation and cause the i beam to become misaligned, leading to structural issues such as uneven floors and doors that don’t close properly.

02How do I know if my mobile home needs leveling?

Look for signs like uneven floors, sticking doors, cracks in your walls and ceilings, or items shifting on the floor. These are common indicators that your home is no longer level. If you notice any of these, it’s time to have a professional inspection done.

03How much does Utah mobile home leveling cost?

The cost of mobile home leveling in Utah varies depending on the size of the home and the extent of the work needed. Mobile home costs are influenced by whether you have a single wide or double wide home, as double wides require more blocks and block sets for proper support. We offer inspections to assess the situation and provide a clear estimate for the job.

04Can leveling my mobile home fix all the structural problems?

Yes, leveling your mobile home is a crucial step in addressing structural issues caused by settling, and our pros can assist with the installation. It helps to prevent further damage, like cracks or misaligned doors, from worsening. 

05How long will it take to level my mobile home?

Most leveling projects are completed in one to two days, depending on the complexity of the job.
