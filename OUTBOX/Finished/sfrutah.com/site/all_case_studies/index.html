Case Study lifted Orem Home

Construction of the residence dates back to 1995. This 5-bedroom, 3-bathroom split-level house offers 2,100 square feet of living space in a well-established neighborhood. Over the years, severe foundation shifting caused one corner of the structure to sink 11 inches. Damage reached a point where the city planned to condemn the building. That was the moment Superior Foundation Repair Utah entered the story—and thankfully, not wearing a cape or anything, just work boots and a hard hat.

Excavation near the affected wall revealed a surprising challenge. Soil beneath the home smelled like swamp conditions instead of typical Utah ground. High moisture levels, a natural spring running under the front of the property, and a high water table across the street at Springwater Park created unstable support. The area is known for lakebed sediment and formerly being a pond. No flooding occurred in the community, but underground water movement quietly affected the foundation. Artesian Road earned its name for a reason, and the park to the south is called Springwater Park with zero irony intended.

Proven Foundation Repair Solutions for Long-Term Stability

To complete the foundation lifting, the Superior Foundation Repair team installed 23 helical piers. Each pier was placed to a minimum capacity of 1,500 PSI. Depths ranged from 33 to 40 feet, with some piles extending down 43 feet to reach competent load-bearing soil. Helical pier installation provided a stable system designed for long-term results. Adjustable steel brackets attached securely to the side of the foundation allowed the structure to rise evenly across all four walls.

Push pile systems were considered, but helical piers offered clear advantages. Push piles can feel abrupt and may cause additional cracking during a lift if not handled carefully. Helical piles create controlled support with greater efficiency and reliability. Nearby homes in the neighborhood confirmed that difference. One homeowner selected another contractor and used push piles. Halfway through the project, funds ran out and the work stopped short of completion. Catastrophic structural issues require steady methods, not experiments, and Superior Foundation Repair Utah specializes in systems proven along the Wasatch Front.

Gas lines and utility piping on the Artesian Road home received special attention. Workers dug out the gas lines by hand to provide relief and prevent damage while the house was lifted. Many contractors focus only on concrete lifting, but the pipes entering a home move as well. Careful excavation created enough room so lines remained protected. The image of that process may not win a beauty contest, but it would absolutely win a “done correctly” contest, which is the one people actually care about.

After stabilization and foundation lifting were finished, an independent structural engineer inspection took place. The home passed inspection with flying colors. Plans for condemnation were canceled, and the once-abandoned property returned to safe condition. The investment firm that purchased the house chose wisely by selecting Superior Foundation Repair Utah as the contractor. Smart investors recognize value, and smart foundations recognize torque—those are just scientific facts with hard hats.

Void fills were also completed beneath the lifted sections of the structure. These fills restored support to empty spaces created during the lift and ensured even bearing. The helical pier system now holds the foundation securely without additional concrete driveways damage. Common questions

01In lifting a home with helical Piers how are they different from push piers?

Push piers are hydraulically driven into the ground using the home's weight, making them better for heavier structures but requiring more excavation and unsuitable for lighter buildings 

like residential homes.
