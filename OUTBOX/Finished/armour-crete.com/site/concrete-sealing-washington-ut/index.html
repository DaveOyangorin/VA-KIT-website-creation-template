From Coral Canyon to Green Springs to the older streets near the fields, the work is the same four steps (clean, dry, seal, cure), with prep first when the surface needs it and a named Deco product matched to the exposure rather than whatever was on the truck.

New concrete is the
best time to seal.

A lot of Washington's concrete is young, and young concrete has a window: once it has fully cured and given up its moisture, sealing it protects a surface that has not yet taken damage, which beats restoring one that has. The desert does not wait long. Hundred-degree summer slabs, winter nights below freezing, salt pools and softener discharge all start working on an unsealed surface from year one.

If your build is recent, the assessment tells you whether the slab is ready yet. If your street is one of the older ones, it tells you what prep comes first. Either way it costs nothing and commits you to nothing.
