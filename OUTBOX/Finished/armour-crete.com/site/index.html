Why concrete fails here

Concrete does not crack because it is weak.

01 / 04

It moves.

Concrete expands and contracts with temperature: roughly 5.5 millionths of an inch, per inch, per degree. Swing a 50-foot driveway 100°F between a January night and a July afternoon and that is about a third of an inch of movement. Stress opens hairline cracks; control joints exist to choose where.

02 / 04

It drinks.

Concrete is porous. Water wicks into the capillary pores and down every crack the movement just opened, and it carries whatever is dissolved in it: salt off the pool, salt out of the softener, salt already in the soil and the hard water.

03 / 04

Then it breaks from the inside.

Water expands about nine percent when it freezes, and southern Utah nights do drop below freezing. Salt does the same job without the cold: as water evaporates it crystallises, and crystals growing in a confined pore lift the surface off. Scaling, spalling and efflorescence are all the same story.

04 / 04

Sealing starves it.

Sealing doesn’t stop concrete moving. Nothing does. It keeps the pore network from staying saturated, which starves freeze–thaw and salt crystallisation of the one thing they both need.

The process

Four steps. No shortcuts.

In southern Utah the timing is most of the job: sealer goes down early in the morning, before the slab has taken on the day’s heat. On a hot surface it flash-dries and leaves whiting and streaks.

24hours until you can walk or drive on it
  1. 01Clean

    The slab gets cleaned end to end. Nothing goes down over dirt.

  2. 02Dry

    We wait for it to dry completely. Sealer over moisture is how finishes fail.

  3. 03Seal

    Deco product, matched to the surface and its exposure, put down early before the slab takes the day’s heat.

  4. 04Cure

    Twenty-four hours before you walk or drive on it. Then it’s yours again.

We come back every two years.
Sealer isn’t permanent. On a two-year cycle we clean it and put a fresh coat down, so the slab never gets far enough gone to need restoring.

For contractors

Questions

Good to know.

01How long before I can walk or drive on it?

Twenty-four hours. That’s the real number. Plan for one day without the surface and you won’t be caught out.

02How often does it need doing again?

It depends on the product and how hard the surface gets used; a shaded patio and a west-facing driveway wear very differently. Our maintenance program runs on a two-year cycle, or just call us when it looks tired.

03Will it make the surface slippery?

Not on pool decks: Salt Shield 303 soaks in and dries clear with no film, and the manufacturer states it doesn’t change skid or slip resistance. High-shine finishes do leave a layer, so we talk that through at the assessment.

04Can you seal brand-new concrete?

Not immediately. Fresh concrete has to cure and give up its moisture first. If you’re mid-build, talk to us at the pour and we’ll schedule it.

05What does it cost?

It depends on the surface, its condition, the prep and the finish, so any number before someone has looked at it is a guess. The on-site assessment is free, with no obligation.
