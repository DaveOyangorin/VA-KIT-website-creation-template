Older slabs, honest answers

Restore what's worth
restoring. Say so when it isn't.

Decades of sun, sprinklers and winter nights leave their record in a slab: scaling at the edges, joints gone crumbly, the white bloom of efflorescence where water has been moving through. Most of it can be corrected and sealed, and the result is a surface that stops getting worse. Some of it is past that, and the honest call is replacement before protection.

That call is what the free assessment is for. We tell you which side of the line your concrete is on, what the prep would involve, and what the sealed surface will and will not look like afterwards. On your own slab, not a brochure's.

Santa Clara questions

Asked in
Santa Clara.

Our driveway is thirty years old. Is sealing even worth it?

Often, yes, once it has been corrected. If the surface is sound or can be made sound, sealing stops the decline where it is. If it is past saving we will say so plainly, because sealing a failed slab wastes your money and our name.

Trees shade half our patio. Does that change anything?

It changes the wear pattern. Shaded concrete and full-sun concrete age at very different rates, and organic debris holds moisture against the surface. Same four-step process, but the product choice and the maintenance cycle take the shade into account.

How long before we can use the surface again?

Twenty-four hours after the sealer goes down. One day, and it is yours again.
