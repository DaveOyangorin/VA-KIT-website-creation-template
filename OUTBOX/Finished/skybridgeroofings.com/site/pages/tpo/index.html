Sky Bridge Roofing installs TPO roofing in South Salt Lake on flat and low-slope roofs, mostly on commercial buildings but also on flat sections of homes. TPO is a single-ply membrane, usually white, whose seams are welded together with hot air so the sheets become one continuous surface.

What TPO is

TPO (thermoplastic polyolefin) is a flexible sheet that softens with heat. That is what makes it useful: the overlapping edges of two sheets are heated and pressed together so they fuse, instead of being glued. A white TPO surface also reflects sunlight, which helps keep heat out of the building in summer.

How it is built up

  • Deck checked and repaired
  • Insulation boards
  • Cover board where the system calls for it
  • TPO membrane, mechanically fastened or adhered
  • Heat-welded seams and detailed flashing at curbs, walls and drains

TPO or EPDM?

Both are single-ply membranes for flat roofs. TPO is welded and usually white; EPDM is rubber, usually black, with taped or glued seams. The right one depends on the building, the roof traffic and the budget. We can quote both.

Commercial roofs

Questions about tpo roofing

Q.01Why are TPO seams welded?

Because TPO melts with heat, the seams can be fused into one piece of membrane, which is stronger than a glued joint.

Q.02Can TPO go on a home?

Yes, on flat or very low-slope sections such as porches, additions and modern flat roofs.

Q.03Can TPO be repaired?

Yes. Punctures and failed seams can be patched and re-welded.

Q.04Do you work from plans?

Yes. Send blueprints and RFPs to cesar@skybridgeroofing.com.
