Common questions

01What causes sunken or uneven concrete in St. George?

Settlement here is driven by desert soils, caliche layers, irrigation leaks, and storm runoff that erodes support under slabs. Poor compaction during construction and downspouts aimed at joints accelerate voids. As soil washes out or collapses, panels drop and tilt. We diagnose root causes - drainage, irrigation, or voids - and correct them with lifting plus joint sealing and optional soil stabilization so the issue doesn’t return.

02Polyjacking vs. mudjacking - which is best for Southern Utah?

Polyjacking uses closed-cell foam that doesn’t wash out and cures fast, making it ideal for driveways, pool decks, and interior slabs. Mudjacking uses a sand-cement slurry and can be cost-effective on thicker exterior panels when conditions allow. In St. George, polyjacking typically offers longer warranties and same-day use; mudjacking can be a budget option. We’ll specify what’s best for your soil, void size, and load demands.

03How long does polyurethane foam lifting last in Utah’s climate?

Closed-cell structural foams are engineered to resist moisture, shrinkage, and decay. With proper drainage and sealed joints, lifts commonly last many years. We address water entry points and stabilize soils where needed to extend longevity. We’ll review coverage and care steps during your walkthrough.

04Do you handle Blue Stakes of Utah 811 utility locates?

Yes. We submit Blue Stakes 811 before drilling or injecting. Utah requires at least 3 business days’ notice and provides a ticket that’s valid for 21 days. We also white-line proposed work areas, use utility locators, and adjust injection points to maintain required clearances. This protects gas, electric, communications, irrigation, and landscape lighting lines so the job is safe and compliant.

05How soon can I use my driveway or sidewalk after lifting?

With polyurethane, most sidewalks and patios are ready for foot traffic within 30 minutes. Many driveways support vehicle traffic the same day, often within a few hours depending on temperature and volume injected. Mudjacking may require longer set times. We’ll provide exact guidance at completion so you know when to walk, park, and place heavy items back on the slab.

06How much does concrete leveling cost near St. George UT?

Typical residential foam lifting projects range $650-$2,400 based on slab size, number of panels, void depth, and access. Mudjacking can run $450-$1,400 when suitable. Large commercial or multi-panel jobs are quoted per mobilization and material volume. We provide a free, itemized estimate so you can compare options and decide what best fits your goals and budget.

07Can you lift cracked slabs and steps?

Often, yes. Hairline to moderate cracks can be stabilized and lifted using dowel pinning, bonding agents, and careful lift sequencing. For heavily broken panels, we may recommend replacement or a hybrid approach. Steps and stoops are commonly lifted by supporting the front edge and filling voids. After lifting, we route and seal cracks to keep water out and help prevent future movement.

08How big are the drill holes, and will they show?

Polyjacking uses small ports - typically 5/8-3/4 inch - patched with a color-matched mortar for a clean finish. Mudjacking requires larger holes. We plan port locations along joints or inconspicuous spots whenever possible. After cleanup and sealing, most clients find the repairs blend well with existing concrete, especially once the surface weathers evenly.

09What areas and zip codes do you serve?

We provide concrete leveling St. George UT and throughout Washington County, including Washington, Santa Clara, Ivins, Hurricane, La Verkin, Toquerville, Leeds, and Dammeron Valley. Neighborhoods include Bloomington, Green Valley, Little Valley, SunRiver, and Washington Fields. Common zip codes: 84770, 84790, 84780, 84765, 84738, and 84737. If you’re nearby, reach out - chances are we service your area.

10Do you seal joints after lifting and recommend post-lift sealing?

Yes. We route and fill joints and cracks with polyurea or urethane to block water infiltration - the biggest driver of future settlement. We can also apply a penetrating or acrylic concrete sealer after curing to shed moisture and reduce surface wear. These steps protect your investment and extend the life of the lift, especially around driveways, pool decks, and irrigation-heavy areas.
