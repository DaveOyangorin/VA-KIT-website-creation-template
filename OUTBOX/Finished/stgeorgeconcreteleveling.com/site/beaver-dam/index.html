Concrete Lifting & Repair Services in Beaver Dam, UT

We focus on long‑lasting concrete lifting and leveling that solves settlement, voids, and drainage issues. Every project is measured with a laser level and completed with safety, cleanliness, and durability in mind.

Polyurethane Foam Injection

Uneven slabs create trip hazards and drainage problems. We lift and stabilize concrete using high‑density polyurethane foam injected through 5/8 in. ports for precise, fast‑curing results. Our crew runs a heated hose foam rig and monitors pressure with gauges for controlled stage lifts. Hydrophobic foam options resist moisture, ideal for irrigation or high water tables. Benefits include small drill holes, minimal mess, and traffic readiness often in 15-30 minutes.

Driveway Leveling

Sunken driveway panels can scrape vehicle undersides and channel water toward your garage. We raise settled sections with foam or slurry, correct approach angles, and restore proper slope to shed water. Tools include rotary hammers, injection ports, and laser levels to hit grade precisely. In most cases, you can drive on the slab the same day. We finish with color‑matching patch mortar at ports, and can add joint caulking to keep debris and water out. It’s a cost‑effective alternative to tear‑out and replacement.

Sidewalk Leveling

Sidewalk height differences can create ADA compliance issues and liability risks. We lift slabs to meet ADA 2010 transition limits, then blend edges and, when needed, perform light trip‑grinding for a smooth, safe path. Our team manages traffic control and silica dust controls per OSHA 29 CFR 1926.1153. We can provide before/after measurements for your records or HOA files. Get safer walkways across your property without replacing entire runs of concrete.

Concrete Slab Lifting

Interior floors, patios, RV pads, and warehouse slabs often settle due to loose subgrade or water migration. We underseal slabs, fill voids, and lift in controlled stages to protect finishes. For larger issues, we offer soil stabilization and deep foam injection to densify subgrade below weak zones. On request, we can coordinate GPR scanning to check for utilities or rebar before drilling. The result: flatter floors, reduced rocking at joints, and improved load support for long‑term performance.

Trip Hazard Removal

Even a 1/2 in. lip can be a serious liability on walkways, entries, or warehouse aisles. We mitigate hazards by lifting panels to target elevation and, when appropriate, blending transitions with diamond grinding to meet ADA limits. Crews set cones and signage, manage dust with shrouds and vacuums, and reopen areas quickly to minimize downtime. Ideal for HOAs, municipal sidewalks, storefronts, and private paths that need fast, documented compliance.

Common questions

01What factors influence the cost of concrete lifting in Box Elder County?

Pricing depends on slab size, thickness, amount of settlement, access, and whether void filling or soil stabilization is needed. Materials matter too - polyjacking uses high‑density foam and smaller ports, while mudjacking uses a cementitious slurry. Corners, joints, and obstacles can add time. During your free concrete leveling estimate in Beaver Dam, we’ll measure with a laser, map injection points, and provide line‑item options like crack sealing or joint caulking so you can choose the best approach.

02How long does polyurethane foam concrete lifting last?

High‑density structural foams are engineered to resist water, erosion, and compression, delivering long‑term support when soils are properly prepared. We often pair lifting with undersealing or deep foam injection to densify weak zones. With proper drainage and sealed joints, foam lifts can last for many years.

03Is mudjacking or polyjacking better for my project?

Both work, but the right choice depends on goals and site conditions. Polyjacking uses 5/8 in. ports, cures fast, adds minimal weight, and is ideal for settled driveways, sidewalks, and interior floors. Mudjacking uses 1-1.5 in. ports, adds mass, and can be beneficial in some soils or budget‑driven scenarios. We’ll explain pros/cons, including polyfoam vs. mud slurry, expected results, and warranties so you can decide with confidence.

04How soon can I walk or drive on the slab after lifting?

With polyurethane foam, foot traffic is typically allowed within 15-30 minutes, and vehicle traffic can often resume the same day once sealants have skinned over. Mudjacking may require longer cure times. We’ll confirm a safe reopening timeline on site and set cones or signage until the area is ready. Our goal is minimal downtime with long‑lasting results.

05Do you seal joints and cracks after leveling?

Yes - upon request we route and fill cracks with epoxy or flexible sealants, and we use backer rod plus self‑leveling polyurethane joint sealant at control joints. Sealing keeps water and fines out, protecting the lift and reducing future settlement. We can also apply a penetrating silane/siloxane sealer to help shed moisture and deicing salts on driveways and walkways.

06Do you offer agricultural slab leveling for barns, silos, or grain bins in Box Elder County?

Absolutely. We level and stabilize agricultural slabs, including barns, grain bin pads, dairies, and shop floors. Our methods address voids from washouts and heavy traffic, and we can perform soil stabilization or deep injections for added subgrade support. We schedule around operations and provide documentation and elevation checks so you know the floor is back in tolerance.

07Can concrete leveling be performed during winter in Northern Utah?

Yes, with adjustments. We use heated foam rigs, cold‑weather best practices, and pay close attention to substrate temperature and moisture. Some sealants and coatings have temperature limits, so we’ll plan sequencing accordingly. If conditions aren’t ideal for finishing work, we can return to complete joint sealing or surface treatments when temperatures rise.

08Is the polyurethane foam environmentally safe and moisture‑resistant?

We install high‑density, closed‑cell structural foams that are inert after cure and won’t wash out. Hydrophobic formulations resist water absorption, making them suitable near irrigation or high‑moisture soils. We’ll recommend the right foam for your site and provide product data upon request. Proper drainage and sealed joints further protect the repair and extend service life.

10Can you scan for utilities or rebar before drilling injection ports?

On request, we can coordinate utility locating or GPR scanning to identify embedded steel and conduits before drilling. It’s especially helpful for shops, warehouses, and utility rooms. We’ll review your site plans, mark potential conflicts, and choose safe port locations. This added step reduces risk and supports a smooth, efficient lift.
