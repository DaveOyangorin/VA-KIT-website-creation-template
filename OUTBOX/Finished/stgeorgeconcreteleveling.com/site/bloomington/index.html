Concrete Lifting & Leveling Services for Homes and Businesses

We specialize in precision concrete lifting and leveling using high‑density polyurethane foam and proven slabjacking techniques. Our process is clean, fast, and engineered for long‑term results.

Polyurethane Foam Injection

Sunken slabs create pooling water, sticky garage doors, and trip hazards. We inject high‑density closed‑cell foam (e.g., AP Lift 430) through 5/8-3/4 in injection ports to lift slabs and fill voids. Heated hoses and a two‑component proportioner ensure consistent expansion, while staged injections protect slab integrity. You get quick cure times - often traffic‑ready in 15-30 minutes - minimal dust, and small, color‑matched patches. This modern “polyjacking” is lighter than slurry, resists washout, and is ideal for driveways, sidewalks, and patios in Bloomington.

Driveway Leveling

Slab settlement at the garage apron or center panels can crack concrete and trap water after summer monsoons. We use foam jacking to restore pitch toward the street and protect joints. Our crew performs void detection via sounding and level checks, lifts in controlled lifts, and seals seams to keep water out. Benefits include preserved concrete, less downtime than replacement, and a cleaner look. It’s a smart alternative to tearing out and re‑pouring - often up to 50% less than replacement in Washington County.

Sidewalk Leveling

Offset panels over 1/2 inch are both a liability and an ADA concern. We correct vertical displacement using edge lifting and deep foam injection if subgrade has washed out. Ports are patched flush, and we can bevel small transitions per ADA Section 303. Our team sets traffic control cones/signage and reopens walks the same day in most cases. From Bloomington Country Club paths to city walks, we remove trip hazards and help protect HOAs and property managers.

Garage Floor Leveling

Interior slabs can settle at the stem wall or mid‑span, creating slopes that affect doors, storage, and epoxy coatings. We perform precise interior foam injections with dust control per OSHA 29 CFR 1926.1153 and use hydrophobic foams if moisture is present. After lift, we seal joints to limit future water intrusion. You’ll see immediate improvements in door alignment and floor flatness - perfect for epoxy/urethane coatings and organizing your garage.

Concrete Void Filling

Hollow sounds or washouts along the Virgin River corridor and irrigation lines are common in 84790. We stabilize soils and fill voids beneath slabs with lightweight foam that won’t add loads like mudjacking slurry. Where needed, we use deeper injections (“Deep Foamjection”) to densify weak zones. Finishing includes seam and crack sealing to keep water out. This approach extends slab life, reduces future settlement, and prevents erosion under driveways, patios, and pool decks.

Common questions

01How long does polyurethane concrete lifting take, and when can I use the area?

Most projects in Bloomington finish in a few hours. Polyurethane foam cures fast, so typical return‑to‑service is 15-30 minutes for sidewalks and driveways. We’ll stage the lift, verify elevation with level checks, and post cones/signage. You’ll get written guidance on reopening times for vehicles and pedestrians so everything is safe and compliant.

02Is polyurethane foam safe for my property and landscaping?

Yes. The high‑density closed‑cell foam we use is inert and non‑toxic. It won’t leach into soil or groundwater and is stable once cured. We select products that are appropriate for residential settings, including options that meet NSF/ANSI/CAN 61 when potable‑safe requirements apply. Foam is also lightweight, which helps prevent additional soil settlement compared to heavy slurries.

03What areas around St. George do you serve?

We serve Bloomington, Bloomington Hills, SunRiver, Green Valley, Washington Fields, Southgate, and Tonaquint - plus nearby cities like Washington, Santa Clara, Ivins, and Hurricane. If you’re within about 25-30 miles of St. George (ZIPs 84790, 84770, 84791 and nearby 84738, 84765, 84780), we can help with residential and commercial slab lifting.

04What trip hazards qualify for ADA remediation by slab lifting?

Under the 2010 ADA Standards Section 303, vertical changes up to 1/4 inch are permissible; 1/4-1/2 inch should be beveled. Larger offsets typically require a ramp or correction such as slab lifting. We evaluate your sidewalks, plan the appropriate lift, and can bevel or seal as needed to help reduce liability and improve accessibility.

05How does foam jacking compare to replacement on cost?

Foam lifting is often up to about 50% less than full tear‑out and replacement in Washington County, with far less downtime. You also avoid landscape disruption and dumpster/haul‑off costs. After lifting, we seal key joints to keep water out, helping the repair last longer in St. George’s monsoon season and summer heat.

06What size holes are drilled for foam jacking, and do you patch them?

Foam jacking typically uses smaller holes - about 5/8-3/4 inch - compared to larger mudjacking ports. After lifting, we patch each port with cementitious material and color‑match as closely as possible. The result is a clean finish that blends with existing concrete and keeps debris and moisture from entering the ports.

07Do I need to call 811 before drilling for concrete leveling in Utah?

Yes. Blue Stakes of Utah 811 requires at least 3 business days’ notice before drilling. Marks are generally valid for 21 days. We handle the locate request, coordinate schedules, and confirm utility clearances before we start, protecting your property and keeping the project compliant.

08How long does polyurethane foam concrete lifting last?

Closed‑cell foam resists water intrusion and won’t shrink like some grouts. We also offer joint and crack sealing to keep water from re‑entering and undermining the lifted slab.

09Can you compare foam jacking vs mudjacking?

Foam uses 5/8-3/4 in holes, cures in minutes, and adds minimal weight to soils - great for weak or saturated subgrades. Mudjacking uses larger holes, adds heavier slurry, and can take longer to reopen to traffic. We provide both options where appropriate and will recommend the best method for your slab, budget, and schedule.
