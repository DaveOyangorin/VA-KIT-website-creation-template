Concrete Lifting & Repair Services Built for Southern Utah

We focus on safe, accurate concrete lifting and stabilization that stands up to Kane County’s freeze-thaw cycles and varied soils. Every project is measured with laser levels and completed using industry‑approved materials and methods.

Polyurethane Foam Injection

Sinking slabs create trip hazards and drainage issues. We lift and stabilize concrete with high-density closed-cell polyurethane foam injected through 5/8"-5/8 to 1.5" ports. Our heated proportioner rig delivers precise foam expansion for controlled lifts. Benefits include smaller drill holes than mudjacking, rapid cure (often in 15-30 minutes), and excellent performance in sandy or damp soils using hydrophobic resins when needed. Ideal for sidewalks, patios, porches, and interior slabs where clean, low‑impact work is critical.

Driveway Leveling

Sunken driveways can damage vehicles and funnel water toward your garage. We map lift points, perform staged foam injection to prevent cracking, and confirm slope with laser levels. Our process addresses voids and weak subgrade with deep foam injection when required. Results: a smoother approach, corrected drainage, and same‑day access in most cases.

Sidewalk Leveling

Raised edges and gaps are an ADA liability for businesses, HOAs, and cities. We mitigate trip hazards by lifting panels or selectively grinding where elevation changes are minimal. Our team follows 2010 ADA walking surface guidance and uses dust collection per OSHA silica standards when routing and sealing joints. The result is a safer, compliant walkway with clean, durable patches that blend with existing concrete.

Garage Floor Leveling

Voids and settlement in garage slabs cause cracked coatings, standing water, and tripping at the entry. We stabilize subgrade with deep foam injection, then lift the slab with carefully staged pressure to limit stress. Moisture meters and soil probes guide our approach. We finish by sealing joints to reduce future water intrusion and protect against freeze‑thaw damage. Most homeowners in Kane County can park in the garage the same day.

Pool Deck Leveling

Settling around pools is common near sandy soils and fluctuating water tables. We use hydrophobic foam where moisture is present and maintain tight lift control to protect coping and tile. Cones and barriers keep the area safe while we work, and our low‑impact drill-pump-patch process keeps your backyard tidy. Restored elevations improve drainage and reduce stub‑toe hazards for family and guests.

Common questions

01How does polyurethane concrete lifting work?

We drill small ports (typically 5/8") through the slab and inject high-density polyurethane foam beneath the concrete. The foam expands, fills voids, and gently raises the slab in controlled stages. We monitor elevations with a laser level and adjust injection points for uniform lift. Most areas can be used the same day because the material cures quickly. It also resists water, making it ideal for Kane County soils with seasonal moisture.

02Polyjacking vs. mudjacking - which is better in Southern Utah?

Polyjacking uses lightweight, closed-cell foam with smaller holes and faster cure times than cementitious mudjacking. In Southern Utah’s freeze-thaw and sandy soils, foam’s moisture resistance and lower added weight are big advantages. Mudjacking can be effective for certain large, thick slabs, but it adds heavy slurry and larger holes. We’ll explain both options on-site and recommend the right method for your slab, soil, and budget.

03How much does concrete leveling cost in Kane County?

Pricing depends on slab size, number of lift points, void depth, access, and whether deep foam injection or joint sealing is needed. Small sidewalk panels may start in the low hundreds, while larger driveways or garage floors vary more. After a quick site visit in Kanab, Orderville, or Duck Creek, we provide a written, line‑item estimate so you can compare leveling vs. replacement costs with confidence.

04How long until I can drive on my leveled driveway?

With polyurethane lifting, most driveways are ready for light foot traffic in 15-30 minutes and vehicle traffic in about 1-2 hours, depending on temperature and lift magnitude. We’ll confirm on-site. We also recommend sealing joints the same day to reduce water intrusion that can trigger future settlement in Kane County’s freeze-thaw cycles.

05Will the drill holes be visible after patching?

We patch injection holes flush and color-match as closely as possible. On broom-finish slabs, patches blend well after weathering. On stamped or colored concrete, patches may remain faintly visible up close, but they’re small and far less noticeable than replacement scars. Most customers in Kane County find the aesthetic tradeoff worth the savings and speed.

06Can you level concrete in cold weather or winter?

Yes. We run a heated foam rig and use materials designed for colder temperatures. We clear snow/ice, check moisture, and adjust injection rates for proper expansion. Winter schedules fill quickly in higher-elevation areas like Cedar Mountain and Bryce Woodlands, so contact us early. Severe weather may require short delays for best results and safety.

07What causes concrete to sink in Kane County’s soils?

Common causes include erosion from irrigation, poor drainage, undersized base material, and voids from freeze-thaw cycles. Sandy soils near Lake Powell and Big Water can wash out, while clay pockets along US‑89 may swell and shrink seasonally. We address the root causes with deep foam injection, drainage corrections, and joint sealing to reduce future movement.

08Do I need a permit for concrete leveling in Kane County?

Most leveling projects do not require a building permit because they restore existing slabs without structural changes. However, public right‑of‑way work, ADA sidewalk corrections, or associated repairs may involve coordination with the Kane County Building Department and local jurisdictions. We’ll advise you during the estimate and help with any approvals if needed.
