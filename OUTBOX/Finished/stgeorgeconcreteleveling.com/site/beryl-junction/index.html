Concrete Leveling & Lifting Services in Beryl Junction

We specialize in lifting, leveling, and stabilizing settled concrete using industry-standard techniques tailored to Southern Utah soils. Every project is measured with laser levels and completed with safety and durability in mind.

Polyurethane Foam Injection

Sunken slabs don’t need replacement. We restore them by injecting high-density polyurethane through 5/8" ports to gently raise and stabilize the concrete. Using a polyurethane proportioner with heated hoses, pressure gauges, and flow meters, we control lift in small increments for accurate results. Foam expands, fills voids, and cures in minutes, limiting downtime. It’s clean, lightweight, and ideal for residential, commercial, and agricultural slabs. We’re trained on HMI/Alchemy-Spetec systems and follow OSHA/NIOSH guidance for isocyanate handling.

Driveway Leveling

Heaved or settled panels can crack tires, collect water, and damage garage thresholds. We level driveway and RV pad sections using foam jacking or mudjacking based on slab thickness and soil. Our team maps voids, sequences lifts to protect joints, and seals with polyurea where needed. You’ll get smoother entries, improved drainage, and extended slab life without the cost of replacement - often in a single visit.

Sidewalk Leveling

Trip hazards near entrances, mailboxes, and curb ramps create risk and ADA issues. We correct vertical offsets and slopes using staged lifts, edge beveling, and crack routing when appropriate. Our crew references 2010 ADA Standards §303 to target thresholds and transitions. Clean 5/8" injection holes minimize patch marks, and most walks reopen within an hour. We also provide post-leveling sealing for added protection.

Concrete Slab Lifting

Garage floors, patios, porches, interior slabs, and warehouse panels sink when desert soils consolidate or wash out. We combine void filling, deep foam injection for subgrade improvement, and compaction grouting where needed. Laser instruments confirm elevations to within fractions of an inch. This approach restores function, reduces slab rocking, and prevents equipment vibration in shops and barns.

Trip Hazard Removal

Insurance carriers flag offsets over 1/4". We remove hazards by lifting low panels to match adjacent slabs or gently beveling minor edges. Our process addresses the cause - voids and poor subgrade - using undersealing to stabilize soils. The result is safer walkways, improved curb appeal, and ADA-friendly thresholds, completed quickly with minimal disruption to foot traffic.

Common questions

01How does concrete lifting work, and what’s the difference between polyurethane and mudjacking?

Both methods fill voids and raise slabs, but they use different materials and hole sizes. Polyurethane foam is injected through 5/8" ports; it expands, stabilizes soils, and cures in minutes with minimal added weight. Mudjacking pumps a cementitious slurry through ~1.5" holes to lift heavier panels where mass is beneficial. We choose based on slab thickness, load, access, and soil conditions in Iron County to deliver a durable, cost-effective result.

02What types of slabs can you lift?

We level residential sidewalks, driveways, RV pads, patios, porches, steps, garage and basement floors, and pool decks. We also service commercial storefronts, warehouse and loading dock slabs, municipal panels, and agricultural floors and grain bin pads around Beryl Junction. For interior slabs, we use dust-controlled drilling, low-odor foams, and careful lift sequencing to protect finishes and door thresholds.

03How long does concrete leveling take, and when can I use the area?

Most residential jobs finish in a few hours. With foam jacking, areas are typically ready for light foot traffic in 15-30 minutes and vehicle traffic in about 1-2 hours, depending on temperature and slab thickness. Mudjacked areas may require longer set times. We’ll confirm reopen times on-site so you can plan around garage access, deliveries, or business operations.

04What causes concrete to sink in Iron County?

Common drivers are dry-out and consolidation of desert soils, wind erosion at slab edges, irrigation or downspout discharge washing out fines, and inadequate base preparation. Freeze-thaw cycling and rodents can also create voids. We address the root causes with void filling, undersealing, soil stabilization, and drainage recommendations to help prevent future settlement in and around Beryl Junction.

05Is foam jacking better than mudjacking?

Foam jacking is cleaner, uses smaller 5/8" holes, adds little weight, and cures rapidly - great for sidewalks, interiors, and sensitive soils. Mudjacking can be ideal for thick, heavily loaded panels that benefit from added mass. We provide both and recommend what fits your slab, soil, and budget after an on-site evaluation.

06What size holes are drilled for foam jacking?

Polyurethane injection typically uses 5/8" holes laid out in a grid to control lift and foam travel. For mudjacking, holes are usually around 1.5". We patch holes flush after the lift, and our team uses laser levels to verify elevations before final sealing and cleanup.

07Will leveling fix cracks, or do they need sealing too?

Leveling closes many cracks but won’t “glue” them together. After the lift, we often route and seal cracks and joints with polyurea or epoxy mortar, add backer rod where needed, and apply a silane/siloxane sealer for protection. This helps lock in the repair and limit water intrusion that can re-open voids.
