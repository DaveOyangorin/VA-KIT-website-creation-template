Common questions

01Can a leaning retaining wall be repaired or does it need replacement?

Many leaning walls can be repaired without full replacement. We stabilize lateral pressure using helical tieback anchors, deadman systems, or soil nails, then correct drainage and realign the wall where feasible. If the wall is severely displaced, lacks proper footing, or has extensive material failure, partial reconstruction with geogrid reinforcement may be more cost‑effective. We’ll inspect, measure movement, and present side‑by‑side repair vs. replacement options with timelines and warranties.

02How do you stabilize a failing retaining wall?

We start with a condition assessment using a laser level and, if needed, a drain camera. Typical solutions include helical tiebacks for lateral restraint, soil nailing with shotcrete facing for cut slopes, or geogrid reinforced reconstruction for SRW walls. We address water with perforated drain pipe, washed gravel, geotextile fabric, and properly spaced weep holes. Finally, we straighten the wall with hydraulic bracing and restore caps. All work follows CMHA/NCMA best practices and engineer requirements.

03Do I need a permit to build or repair a retaining wall in St. George, Utah?

In St. George, walls over 4 feet in height (measured from bottom of footing to top of wall) typically require a permit and engineered design under City Code Title 10. Repairs that change structure, add tiebacks, or modify height may also need permits. Submittals often include site plan, wall drawings, soils information, and calculations. We coordinate with local engineers and the City, and we can prepare HOA documentation for neighborhoods like Little Valley, Green Springs, and SunRiver.

04How much does retaining wall repair cost in Southern Utah?

Costs vary by wall type, access, and engineering. Typical residential repairs range from $3,500-$18,000. Helical tieback anchors often price by anchor and depth; many jobs use 4-8 anchors, with per‑anchor cost influenced by torque, length, and hardware. Drainage retrofits, cap replacements, and partial rebuilds add to scope. After inspection, we’ll provide a written estimate, including repair vs. replacement comparisons for St. George, Washington City, and Ivins.

05Are tieback anchors or soil nails better for my wall?

It depends on wall type, soils, and access. Tieback anchors work well for CMU/block and poured concrete walls where we can install through the wall into competent soils. Soil nails are ideal for stabilizing cut slopes or where a shotcrete facing is planned. Segmental retaining wall (SRW) systems often benefit from geogrid reinforced reconstruction. We’ll evaluate your site and recommend the approach that provides the safest, most durable result.

06What drainage is required behind a retaining wall?

Walls need free‑draining backfill, a perforated drain pipe at the base, washed gravel, and geotextile to keep fines out. Weep holes help relieve pressure on solid walls. Lines must daylight to a proper outlet - sometimes with a French drain assembly. We often space weep holes every 6-8 feet, adjusting for wall length and design. Our team inspects outlets in areas like 84770 and 84790 to ensure water actually leaves the system.

07Can you provide an engineer‑stamped letter of stability after repairs?

Yes. When engineering is required or requested by your HOA, we coordinate with a licensed Utah structural engineer to review our torque logs, installation data, and as‑built details. You’ll receive an engineer‑stamped letter of stability for the city/HOA file. This is common for leaning retaining wall repair, added tiebacks, and SRW reconstruction projects across Washington County.

08What post‑repair monitoring do you perform over the first 12 months?

We offer a post‑repair monitoring plan with scheduled plumb/level checks and drain outlet verifications - especially after heavy rain events. We document readings, confirm no renewed movement, and recommend maintenance like clearing debris from weep holes.

09Can stabilization be done in winter in St. George without full excavation?

Often, yes. Helical tiebacks and soil nails can be installed with compact equipment in cooler months, and many drainage retrofits require only targeted trenching. We sequence work to avoid frozen backfill and protect subgrades. Limited‑access yards benefit from minimally invasive rigs year‑round. After inspection, we’ll confirm feasibility, timelines, and any weather‑related adjustments to compaction or curing.

10Do you offer minimally invasive anchor solutions for tight backyards?

Yes. Our helical anchor drive heads and compact skid steers fit through many side yards and gates, reducing excavation and preserving landscaping. We also use core drills for through‑wall installations, minimizing block removal. This approach is ideal for established neighborhoods in Bloomington Hills, The Ledges, and Washington Fields where access is tight but stabilization is urgent.
