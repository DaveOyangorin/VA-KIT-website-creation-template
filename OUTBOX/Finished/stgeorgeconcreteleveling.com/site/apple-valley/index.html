Core Concrete Leveling & Repair Services in Apple Valley

We focus on concrete lifting and stabilization that fixes the root cause - voids, settlement, and drainage - using proven methods and materials. Our crews follow OSHA safety standards and use laser levels to deliver accurate, long-lasting results.

Polyurethane Foam Injection

Sunken slabs create trip hazards and drainage issues around driveways, sidewalks, and porches. Our high-density closed-cell polyurethane foam is injected through 3/8-5/8 inch ports using a calibrated poly injection rig and heated hoses. We lift in controlled stages with a grid drilling pattern and dwell times to prevent over-lift. Benefits include fewer/smaller holes than mudjacking, clean job sites, and same-day return to service. It’s ideal for foam concrete lifting in Apple Valley’s sandy soils and washout-prone areas along the SR-59 corridor.

Driveway Leveling

Uneven driveway panels can crack tires, channel water toward your garage, and lower resale value. We correct settlement with incremental foam lifts, laser-level verification, and slope correction so water sheds away from foundations. When needed, we stabilize subgrade with deep foam injection for subbase densification. After lifting, we seal joints with polyurea to reduce water intrusion. You’ll get a smooth, long-lasting approach you can drive on within hours - an ideal alternative to replacement.

Sidewalk Leveling

Uneven sidewalks pose real liability - especially for HOAs and businesses. We eliminate trip hazards to help meet ADA 2010 Standards §303 and PROWAG cross-slope guidance. Our team lifts panels precisely, then routes and seals joints to keep water from eroding soils. Where minor lips remain, we finish with targeted trip hazard grinding. Need documentation for compliance? We can provide before/after measurements and photos for your records.

Concrete Slab Lifting

Garage floors, patios, and interior slabs often drop due to voids, plumbing leaks, or poor compaction. We map voids, inject foam through small ports, and monitor lift with a digital slope meter to protect doors, walls, and finishes. For larger washouts, we use deep foam injection and void mapping to re-support the slab. It’s a clean process with HEPA-filtered dust control and minimal disruption. Great for residential and commercial spaces across Washington County.

Trip Hazard Removal

Small misalignments at joints can still trigger injuries and claims. We evaluate each panel to choose the best fix: micro-lift with foam, precision grinding with a concrete grinder and scarifier, or a hybrid approach. We also seal joints to slow future movement. This service is ideal for fast, budget-conscious repairs for HOAs, property managers, and business owners seeking ADA-friendly walkways with minimal downtime.

Common questions

01How do I know if my concrete needs lifting?

Common signs include trip lips at joints, pooling water near foundations, doors that drag, rocking slabs, or gaps under stoops and steps. In Apple Valley’s soils, washouts after storms often leave hollow sounds when tapped. We’ll inspect with a laser level, probe soils, and check slopes to confirm settlement. If the slab is structurally sound, lifting and void fill restore support and drainage without costly replacement.

02How long does polyurethane concrete lifting take and when can I use it?

Most residential projects in Washington County finish in 2-4 hours. Our high-density foam expands and cures rapidly, allowing foot traffic in about 30 minutes and vehicle traffic typically within 1-4 hours, depending on slab thickness and temperature. Because we drill only small ports and keep sites clean, disruption is minimal compared to demolition and replacement.

03What is the difference between polyjacking and mudjacking?

Mudjacking uses a cementitious slurry (sand/Portland) injected through larger holes. It’s heavier and may add load to weak soils. Polyjacking uses lightweight, closed-cell polyurethane foam injected through small 3/8-5/8 inch ports. Foam resists water, won’t wash out, and reaches strength quickly, so slabs can be used the same day. In Southern Utah, polyjacking often outperforms mudjacking for void fill and long-term stability.

04How much does concrete leveling cost compared to replacement?

Leveling usually saves 50-70% versus tearing out and repouring. For Concrete Leveling Apple Valley UT, typical residential pricing ranges by slab size, access, and void depth. Many projects fall between a few hundred and a few thousand dollars. After inspection, we provide a fixed written quote and options like joint sealing to protect your investment. You’ll know exact costs before work begins.

05Does concrete leveling work for large voids or washouts?

Yes. We use deep foam injection, staged lifts, and void mapping to stabilize slabs over larger washouts caused by irrigation leaks, downspouts, or storm runoff. Our closed-cell foam expands to fill gaps and densify weak subgrade. For complex sites, we can coordinate GPR utility scans and recommend drainage corrections to prevent recurrence.

06Do you offer crack and joint sealing after lifting?

Absolutely. After raising slabs, we route and seal cracks and joints with polyurea or polyurethane sealant and backer rod where appropriate. Sealing limits water intrusion, which is a leading cause of repeat settlement in Apple Valley. We also offer pressure washing and penetrating sealers (silane/siloxane) to protect surfaces and improve curb appeal.

07Do I need a permit to level a sidewalk or driveway in Apple Valley?

Most residential foam lifting and sidewalk leveling projects don’t require a building permit. For work in public rights-of-way or commercial properties, the Town or UDOT may require coordination. We handle Utah 811 Blue Stakes utility locates before drilling and can advise on any local requirements, insurance, or HOA approvals needed.

09What causes concrete to sink in Washington County?

Common causes include poorly compacted fill, erosion from downspouts and irrigation, plumbing leaks, and seasonal moisture changes. In Apple Valley, storm runoff and sandy soils can create voids under slabs, leading to settlement. We address the cause by filling voids, stabilizing soils, correcting slopes, and recommending maintenance like joint sealing and drainage improvements.
