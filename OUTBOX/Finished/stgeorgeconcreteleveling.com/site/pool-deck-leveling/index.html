Common questions

01Why is my pool deck sinking or uneven?

Settlement usually comes from poorly compacted backfill around the pool, water washing out fines near plumbing trenches, or expansive/clayey soils that shrink and swell in our desert climate. Over time, voids form under the slab and panels drop, creating trip edges and puddling. We address the root cause by filling voids, lifting with hydrophobic foam, and restoring proper slope so water sheds away from the pool and coping in St. George’s hot, dry conditions.

02How does polyurethane foam jacking level a pool deck?

We drill small 5/8 in. ports and inject closed‑cell polyurethane beneath the slab. The foam expands, fills voids, and gently lifts panels while we monitor elevations with a laser. Because the foam is hydrophobic, it resists moisture and stabilizes soils. Most decks can be walked on the same day, and joints are sealed after lifting to lock in alignment and keep water out of the subbase.

03Is polyjacking safe around pool plumbing and coping?

Yes - when done with staged lifts and careful monitoring. We lift incrementally to protect the bond beam and tile, and we avoid injection points that could stress skimmers or return lines. If we suspect voids near plumbing, we can add GPR scanning or coordinate pressure testing before injection for extra peace of mind.

04How long does pool deck leveling take, and when can I use the deck?

Most residential jobs take 2-5 hours, depending on the number of panels and drop. The foam reaches working strength in minutes, so foot traffic is typically same day. Heavier loads, like furniture, are usually fine after 24 hours. We’ll provide site‑specific guidance at your walk‑through so you can plan with confidence.

05Polyjacking vs. mudjacking: which is better for St. George soils?

In our climate, closed‑cell polyurethane is preferred for pool decks because it’s lightweight, moisture‑resistant, and requires smaller holes. Mudjacking uses a heavier cement slurry that can add load to weak soils and may wash out over time. Foam also allows finer control near coping and tile. We’re happy to explain both options and recommend what’s best for your site.

06Will leveling fix standing water and drainage issues?

Yes - our goal is to restore a consistent 1-2% slope away from the pool. We combine precise lifts with joint restoration and, when needed, minor grinding to feather high edges. If resurfacing is planned, we coordinate elevations so coatings and textures maintain proper drainage and safe traction in wet, barefoot areas.

07How much does it cost to level a pool deck in St. George, UT?

Pricing depends on the number of affected panels, depth of settlement, and void conditions. Many projects are quoted per section, with typical single‑section lifts costing less than partial replacement. Your free on‑site estimate will detail section counts, joint/crack repairs, and any resurfacing options so you can choose the right scope for your budget.

08Do you offer warranties for pool deck leveling in Southern Utah?

Yes. We’ll review coverage, maintenance tips (like joint resealing intervals), and resurfacing compatibility during your estimate so expectations are clear.

09Do you service Washington, Santa Clara, Ivins, and Hurricane?

Absolutely. We serve St. George and surrounding communities across Washington County, including Washington, Santa Clara, Ivins, Hurricane, Little Valley, SunRiver, Washington Fields, and more. Ask about expedited or same‑day scheduling during peak season - we’ll do our best to meet your timeline.

10Can you resurface or coat the deck after leveling?

Yes. After lifting, we can recommend compatible finishes such as spray textures (cool deck style), cementitious microtoppings, or polyaspartic/epoxy systems. We’ll verify moisture conditions, recommend anti‑slip additives that meet DCOF guidelines, and coordinate elevations so the final surface drains correctly and looks uniform.
