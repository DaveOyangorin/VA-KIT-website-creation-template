Common questions

01How do I know if my concrete needs lifting or void filling?

Common signs include hollow sounds when tapped, visible gaps at slab edges, rocking or “see-saw” movement, new or widening cracks, and pooling water where panels settled. Driveways, garage floors, sidewalks, and pool decks in St. George often show these symptoms after irrigation leaks or storms. We inspect the slab, check drainage, and can use GPR to verify voids. If the slab is structurally sound, void filling and leveling usually restore support and alignment without replacement.

02How long does polyurethane lifting/void fill take and when can I use the area?

Most residential projects take a few hours. Polyurethane foam cures fast, so areas are typically ready for foot traffic in 15-30 minutes and vehicle traffic the same day, depending on temperature and load. We monitor lift with a laser and stage injections to protect the slab. Commercial sites may require phased work or brief closures. You’ll get a clear plan and return-to-service timeline before we start.

03Is polyurethane foam safe and environmentally friendly?

We use high-density, closed-cell geotechnical foam designed for soil stabilization. It is inert after cure, does not wash out, and resists water absorption. Our process uses small injection ports and minimal material to achieve lift. We follow OSHA/UOSH safety standards and control dust when drilling. For large or sensitive areas, we can discuss alternative fills like CLSM or cellular concrete to match site needs in Southern Utah.

04Polyjacking vs. mudjacking: which is better for my slab?

Polyjacking uses lightweight, high-density foam that expands to fill voids and lift slabs through small 5/8-inch holes. It cures fast and adds minimal weight to weak soils. Mudjacking uses a sand-cement slurry (heavier and larger holes) and can be effective for certain conditions. We recommend the method based on soil, load (cars, RVs, forklifts), access, and budget. We’ll explain pros/cons and provide pricing for both when appropriate.

05How much does concrete void filling cost in St. George?

Costs depend on void size, access, lift height, and material choice (polyurethane foam, grout, CLSM, or cellular concrete). Small residential void fills can be a few hundred dollars; larger or commercial projects vary. We can GPR-scan to better estimate material volume.

06What Blue Stakes 811 steps are required before drilling injection ports?

In Utah, call Blue Stakes 811 at least 3 business days before any covered drilling or excavation so utilities can be marked. We coordinate this for projects where required, especially along sidewalks, driveways near rights-of-way, and commercial sites. After utility clearance, we mark injection points, set traffic control if needed, and proceed safely. This step protects underground lines and keeps your project compliant.

07Can a GPR scan quantify the void volume so I get an accurate material estimate?

Yes. Ground Penetrating Radar can map the extent and depth of voids, helping us estimate material needs and refine pricing. We combine GPR data with test injections and elevation checks to avoid over- or under-injecting. This is especially helpful for warehouse floors, approach slabs, and suspected washouts from plumbing leaks. Better data up front leads to a more accurate proposal and fewer surprises.

08When should I choose cellular concrete or CLSM instead of polyurethane foam?

For large voids, long runs, or annular spaces where lightweight uniform fill is preferred, cellular concrete (LDCC) or CLSM can be ideal. We use foam or grout for targeted lifts, then switch to LDCC/CLSM when volumes are high or slab loads require broader support. We’ll evaluate soil conditions, drainage, and access to select the most efficient, durable option for your St. George site.

09Can void filling fix trip hazards to meet ADA guidelines?

Yes. We level panels and reduce vertical offsets that exceed ADA §303 thresholds (1/4 inch vertical; 1/2 inch beveled). We aim for smooth transitions and restore slope for drainage. After lifting, we seal joints to limit water entry that causes future settlement. For public sidewalks, we set up traffic control and schedule work to minimize impact while improving accessibility and safety.
