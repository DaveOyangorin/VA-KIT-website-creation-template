Concrete Lifting & Leveling Services for Sunset Homes and Businesses

We specialize in precision concrete lifting and stabilization that’s fast, clean, and built to last. Our team uses proven methods - polyurethane foam injection, careful void filling, and joint sealing - to deliver reliable results with minimal disruption.

Polyurethane Foam Injection

Sunken slabs don’t need replacement. We lift them with high-density polyurethane foam (2-4 lb/ft³) injected through 5/8-inch ports. Heated hoses and a static mixer ensure consistent expansion, while laser levels and pressure gauges guide a controlled, even lift. This method is lightweight, won’t add load to weak soils, and cures fast - often ready for traffic in 30-60 minutes. It’s ideal for residential and commercial projects where downtime matters.

Driveway Leveling

Settlement at driveway panels and aprons creates bumps, pooling, and tire-catching lips. We lift and align sections near joints and at the curb using foam injection and laser-guided leveling. Hydrophobic foam tackles moisture-prone areas, while our team seals joints with semi-rigid polyurea to keep water out after the lift. You’ll get a smoother approach, better drainage, and a safer surface - without tearing out concrete or blocking access for days.

Sidewalk Leveling

Trip hazards as small as 1/4 inch can violate ADA guidelines. We correct sidewalk lips and misaligned panels along the Sunset Blvd corridor using targeted lifts, trip-lip beveling where needed, and post-lift crack/joint sealing. Our crew sets MUTCD-compliant cones and pedestrian detours when working near public walkways and coordinates Blue Stakes 811 utility locates before drilling in right-of-way zones.

Patio Leveling

Patios and pool decks often settle along edges over blue clay or poorly compacted fill. We fill voids, re-level the slab, and stabilize load points with deep foam injections when necessary. Industrial vacuums control silica dust, and color-matched patch mortar blends 5/8-inch ports. The result: a safer, flatter space that sheds water away from foundations and coping - ready for use the same day in most cases.

Garage Floor Leveling

Sunken garage slabs create door clearance problems, puddles, and uneven transitions. We lift interior panels with minimal mess using a slab lifting gun and foam rig, then seal control joints with semi-rigid filler to resist future raveling. Laser checks verify level at door thresholds, and our clean drill-pump-patch process keeps dust and disruption to a minimum - so you can park again fast.

Common questions

01How do I know if my concrete needs lifting?

Look for uneven panels, 1/4-1/2 inch lips, pooled water near joints, doors that rub, or gaps under steps and stoops. In Sunset’s 84770 area, sidewalks along Sunset Blvd commonly settle at tree roots or utility trenches. We inspect with laser levels, check soils for voids, and recommend either polyurethane lifting, void filling, or targeted trip-lip beveling to bring panels back into alignment and improve drainage.

02How long does polyurethane lifting take and when can I use the slab?

Most residential projects take 2-4 hours. Polyurethane cures fast; foot traffic is usually allowed within 30 minutes, and vehicles can return to driveways in about 1-2 hours depending on temperature and slab thickness. Because we avoid heavy, wet slurry, there’s no long cure time. We’ll confirm safe re-entry before we leave and provide aftercare tips to protect the lift.

03Is the foam safe for soil and groundwater?

Yes. We use closed-cell, high-density polyurethane designed for geotechnical lifting. Once cured, it’s inert, does not leach, and resists water intrusion. In wetter spots or near irrigation, we can use hydrophobic lifting foam to maintain performance. Our process minimizes spoil and runoff, and we patch ports with durable mortar, then seal joints to keep water out of the subgrade.

04What’s the difference between polyjacking and mudjacking?

Polyjacking uses lightweight foam injected through small 5/8-inch ports. It expands, lifts, and cures quickly with minimal added load to weak soils. Mudjacking uses a cementitious slurry pumped through larger holes and adds significant weight, which can lead to re-settlement in blue clay. Foam is cleaner, faster, and better for tight access or interior slabs. We offer both methods and recommend what fits your slab and budget.

05How big are the drill holes and will patches be noticeable?

Foam injection ports are typically 5/8 inch. We use color-matched patch compounds over each port and finish them flush. On older or stained concrete, patches may be faintly visible up close. If desired, we can pressure wash and apply a penetrating sealer after leveling to blend tones and protect the slab from moisture and UV.

06Do you seal cracks and joints after leveling?

Yes. After lifting, we seal working joints and control joints with semi-rigid polyurea or self-leveling polyurethane. We install backer rod where needed for proper depth and add bond-breaker tape to protect edges. This reduces future water intrusion, helps maintain the lift, and improves appearance. We can also perform crack stitching and caulking as part of the same visit.

07Do you contact Blue Stakes 811 before drilling?

Absolutely. We submit Blue Stakes of Utah 811 tickets and follow the required notification timelines - typically at least two business days before excavation or drilling in public right-of-way or near marked utilities. Our crews pre-mark panels, use utility locate paint/flags, and hand-check sensitive areas. We can share ticket numbers and permit details on request.

08Can you fix ADA trip hazards on sidewalks to code?

Yes. We correct changes in level per the 2010 ADA Standards Section 303 and verify cross-slope (1:48) where applicable. For sidewalks along Sunset Blvd, we can set MUTCD-compliant pedestrian detours during work and coordinate any city right-of-way permits. After repair, we can document before/after elevations for property managers, insurers, or inspections.

09How much does concrete leveling cost in St. George UT?

Typical residential foam lifting ranges from about $6-$15 per square foot, depending on slab thickness, number of holes, access, and whether void filling or soil stabilization is needed. Small sidewalk panels near Sunset Corner may be a few hundred dollars; large driveways or interior floors cost more. We provide free on-site estimates and written pricing before work begins.

10How do you address St. George’s blue clay and collapsible soils to reduce future settling?

We identify weak zones, inject foam to fill voids and underseal slabs, and use deep foam injection at load points where needed. Post-lift, we seal joints and recommend grading or drainage fixes to keep water out of blue clay. In some cases, we add resin-based soil stabilization to increase bearing capacity under problematic edges and approaches.
