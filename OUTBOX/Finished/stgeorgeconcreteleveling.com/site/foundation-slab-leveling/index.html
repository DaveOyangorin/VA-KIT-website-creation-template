Safety & Risk Elimination

Your home’s structure is not the place for guesswork. We use a laser level/ZipLevel to measure settlement to the tenth of an inch, then lift in controlled stages using manifolds and gauges. This protects plumbing, conduits, and finishes while eliminating trip hazards and liability.

Every project follows OSHA 29 CFR 1926 safety practices. We’re fully insured and Utah-licensed, so you’re covered from the first drill hole to final sealant. The result: a safe site, protected utilities, and a stable slab.

We match techniques to your slab and soils. Polyurethane foam injection (polyjacking) through 3/8-5/8 inch ports delivers precise lift and hydrophobic, closed-cell support. Mudjacking with cementitious grout through 1-2 inch holes is an option for some exterior slabs.

We seal joints with polyurea, repair cracks with epoxy or urethane injection, and finish with non-shrink grout for a clean look. Our methods align with ACI 562 guidance for repair of existing concrete, so you get durable, code-aware work.

Common questions

01How much does slab leveling cost in St. George?

Most residential slab leveling ranges from $8-$18 per square foot locally, depending on access, lift height, void size, and finish work (joint sealing, crack injection). Small trip hazards can be a few hundred dollars; larger interior lifts or garages can run $1,500-$4,500. We provide written, line-item estimates after an on-site assessment, so you know exactly what’s included before work begins.

02Is foam jacking better than mudjacking?

Polyurethane foam excels for precision, small injection ports, quick cure (about 15 minutes), and water resistance - great for driveways, garages, and interiors. Mudjacking uses a cement-sand slurry and larger holes, and can be cost-effective for some exterior slabs. We recommend the best method for your slab, soils, and budget after evaluating drainage, utilities, and lift requirements.

03How long do polyurethane leveling repairs last in our desert climate?

High-density, closed-cell foams are engineered to resist moisture and seasonal swings. When paired with good drainage and soil preparation, results can last many years. We include soil densification or joint sealing where needed to extend longevity.

04Do I need a permit for foundation or slab leveling in St. George?

For non-structural slab lifting and void filling, a permit is often not required. If underpinning, structural repairs, or grade beam work are involved, permitting and engineering under the 2021 IBC typically apply. We’ll advise during your assessment and, if needed, coordinate with the city or Washington County to keep your project compliant.

05What are the signs my slab is settling?

Look for uneven or bouncy floors, doors rubbing or not latching, gaps at baseboards, sloping cabinets, and cracks that open wider at the top than the bottom. Outside, watch for pooling water, sunken steps, or trip hazards. Early evaluation helps avoid secondary damage to tile, drywall, and plumbing.

06Can you level interior floors without major demolition?

Yes. We drill small, strategically placed ports - often through grout lines or hidden areas - then inject foam to lift and stabilize. We contain dust, protect finishes, and patch ports after the lift. Most projects take a few hours with minimal disruption, and you can typically walk the area the same day.

07Will foam injection damage plumbing or electrical lines?

We map utilities, inspect for leaks, and lift in controlled stages while monitoring pressure and elevations. Polyurethane seeks the path of least resistance, and we use manifold control to prevent over-lift. In areas with known utilities, we adjust port placement and inject at lower pressures to keep lines safe.

08What’s the difference between leveling and underpinning?

Leveling raises and supports the slab by filling voids and densifying soils - ideal for many residential and flatwork issues. Underpinning adds deep support with helical or push piers when soils near the surface can’t carry the load. We’ll recommend leveling, piers, or a hybrid approach based on settlement depth and soil capacity.

09How quickly can I use my driveway or garage after leveling?

With polyurethane, most driveways and garages are ready within an hour or two of completion. Foam reaches significant strength within minutes and fully cures quickly. We’ll confirm timing on-site based on temperature and load. For mudjacking, cure times are longer - often 24-48 hours before heavy loads.
