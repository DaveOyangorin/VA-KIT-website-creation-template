Polyurethane Foam Injection

Sunken slabs create pooled water, uneven steps, and dangerous trip hazards. We solve this with high‑density polyurethane injected through 5/8" ports to lift and support concrete. Our proportioner and heated hoses ensure consistent foam expansion, while a laser level guides each incremental lift. It’s cleaner and lighter than mudjacking, won’t add heavy load to weak soils, and is traffic‑ready in hours. Learn more about our Polyurethane Foam Injection services.

Driveway Leveling

Sidewalk Leveling

Uneven sidewalks risk injuries and ADA non‑compliance. We remove trip hazards by lifting panels back to grade, checking slopes, and beveling minor edges with a diamond grinder when needed. Common questions

01How does the drill‑pump‑patch concrete lifting process work?

We mark injection points, then drill small 5/8" holes (polyjacking) or 1"-1.5" (mudjacking). Next, we inject expanding polyurethane foam or a cement‑sand slurry to fill voids and lift the slab. A laser level monitors every incremental lift for accuracy. Finally, we patch holes with non‑shrink grout, seal key joints, and clean the site. Most Pine Valley projects are completed in hours with minimal disruption.

02Is it cheaper to level concrete or replace it in Utah?

Leveling is usually 50-70% less than tear‑out and replacement, with far less mess and downtime. Typical Utah leveling runs about $6-$18 per sq ft depending on access, thickness, and void size; replacement can exceed $12-$25 per sq ft plus disposal and curing time. For most settled driveways, sidewalks, and patios in Washington County, leveling is the faster, more economical fix.

03How much does concrete leveling cost in Pine Valley (ZIP 84781)?

Small sidewalk panels may start a few hundred dollars; multi‑panel driveways often range $800-$2,500. Factors include elevation access, number of ports, foam volume, and whether deep foam injection or joint/crack sealing is needed. Rural properties near Dixie National Forest can require additional setup. We provide on‑site evaluations and a written estimate before work begins.

04Polyjacking vs. mudjacking - which is better for my slab?

Polyjacking uses lightweight, high‑density foam through 5/8" ports. It cures fast, adds minimal weight, and resists water intrusion. Mudjacking uses a cement‑sand slurry through larger holes and can be cost‑effective for thick, exterior slabs. In Pine Valley’s freeze‑thaw climate, polyurethane often performs better by limiting added load and sealing voids. We’ll recommend the best method after inspection.

05Can you level concrete in winter in Pine Valley?

Yes, in many cases. We monitor temps, ground moisture, and freeze‑thaw risk, and can use low‑temp curing polyurethane to complete cold‑weather lifts. We also schedule during favorable windows and protect the work area with heaters as needed. If conditions won’t support a quality result, we’ll propose a targeted plan and timeline to ensure durability.

06How long does polyurethane foam lifting last?

High‑density foam is inert, water‑resistant, and designed to last for decades. Longevity depends on subgrade conditions and drainage. We stabilize soils where needed (deep foam injection or compaction grouting) and reseal joints to keep water out. With proper drainage maintenance, leveled slabs in Pine Valley and the UT‑18 corridor perform long‑term.

07How soon can I use my driveway after lifting?

Polyurethane cures quickly - many driveways are ready for light vehicle traffic within 30-60 minutes after the final injection and patch. Heavier vehicles or RVs may require a longer wait, which we’ll confirm on site. Mudjacking cure times can be longer. We’ll provide clear, written guidance before we leave.

08Do you handle Blue Stakes of Utah 811 utility locates?

Yes. We submit 811 notifications at least 3 business days before drilling for public utilities and review owner‑provided maps for private lines. Our crew marks safe zones, adjusts port locations, and uses OSHA‑compliant PPE and dust control. This process keeps projects in Pine Valley safe and on schedule.

09Will leveling fix frost heave or heaved slabs?

Leveling corrects settled or tilted panels caused by voids and weak soils. True heave from expanding frozen soils is different. We assess whether the slab is up or down, then recommend frost‑heave remediation: drainage improvements, soil stabilization, and joint sealing. In rare cases of severe heave or structural damage, selective replacement may be the smarter option.

10Will concrete leveling fix cracks and joints?

Lifting re‑aligns panels but doesn’t “glue” cracks together. After leveling, we often seal joints with polyurethane and fill cracks with epoxy or flexible sealants to block water and reduce movement. Larger structural cracks may need stitching or additional repair. We’ll document options and costs during your on‑site estimate in Pine Valley.
