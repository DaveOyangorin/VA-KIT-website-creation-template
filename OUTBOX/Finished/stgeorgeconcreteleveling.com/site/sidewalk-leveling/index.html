Common questions

01What causes uneven sidewalks in Southern Utah’s soils and climate?

In St. George, settlement is often driven by moisture changes in clay seams, erosion around edges, and washout from irrigation. Caliche layers can create hollow spots beneath panels, while tree roots may heave adjacent slabs. Freeze/thaw cycles are milder here, but water intrusion through open joints accelerates movement. Our inspections look for voids, drainage patterns, and joint failures so we can plan lifting, void filling, and sealing to stop the cause - not just the symptom.

02Is foam jacking better than mudjacking for sidewalks?

For most sidewalks, polyurethane foam offers smaller 5/8-inch holes, precise control, and fast cure - often ready to walk on in 15-30 minutes. Mudjacking (cement/stone slurry) can be cost-effective for larger areas but uses bigger holes and adds more weight to weak soils. We provide both options and recommend the best fit based on slab size, soil condition, access, and budget, so you get lasting results without guesswork.

03How quickly can I walk or drive on leveled concrete?

With polyurethane foam injection, foot traffic is typically allowed within 15-30 minutes after lift. Light carts can follow shortly. For driveways adjoining sidewalks, we’ll advise timing based on temperature and load - often a few hours. Mudjacking cure times are longer. We confirm elevations with a laser, seal joints, and do a final check before opening the area so you can use the surface confidently the same day.

04Can grinding alone remove my sidewalk trip hazard?

Sometimes. ADA Section 303 allows up to 1/4 inch vertical without bevel; 1/4-1/2 inch needs a 1:2 bevel. Precision cutting/grinding can remove small lips, but larger differentials usually require lifting to correct grade. We frequently combine lifting with taper grinding for perfect transitions where slabs meet. Our goal is a smooth, compliant surface without over-grinding or weakening edges.

05Do I need a city permit to repair or construct sidewalks in St. George?

Work in the public right-of-way may require a permit and inspections under St. George Municipal Code. On private property, permits are not always needed for leveling, but utility locates and traffic control rules still apply. We’ll review your address, determine if the slab is city-adjacent, coordinate Blue Stakes 811, and help with any required right-of-way or UDOT encroachment permits before work begins.

06Will leveling address voids and prevent future settling?

Yes - when we lift with foam, we also fill voids and densify weak soils beneath the slab. This reduces water movement, supports edges, and helps prevent re-settlement. We then seal joints and cracks to block water intrusion. Addressing drainage - downspouts, sprinklers, or slope - is part of our plan. These steps work together to keep your sidewalk stable longer and protect your investment.

07How much does sidewalk leveling cost near St. George, UT?

Pricing depends on drop height, number of panels, access, and whether grinding or sealing is included. In 84770-84790, many projects land within practical ranges per panel, with foam typically higher than mudjacking but faster and lighter. During your free estimate, we provide a written scope, itemized options, and expected totals - so you can compare leveling vs. replacement with clear numbers for your property.

08Do I need to call 811 before sidewalk leveling?

Yes. Utah law requires a utility locate before drilling or injection. We submit the Blue Stakes of Utah 811 ticket at least three business days prior and do not start until utilities are marked. This protects underground lines for irrigation, lighting, and communications, and it keeps the project on schedule without costly interruptions or hazards.

09What height difference is considered an ADA trip hazard?

Under ADA 2010 Standards, a vertical change up to 1/4 inch is allowed. From 1/4 to 1/2 inch, the edge must be beveled at 1:2. Anything greater typically requires a ramp or regrading. We measure offsets, lift where needed, and bevel minor transitions so your sidewalks align with these thresholds and feel smooth underfoot for pedestrians, strollers, and mobility devices.

10Can you provide ADA-compliance documentation after service?

Absolutely. We can document before/after offsets, show reductions to ≤1/4 inch or beveled transitions where applicable, and include photos and location maps - ideal for HOAs, commercial sites, and campuses. If your project requires city coordination, we’ll also provide permit references and inspection notes. This record helps demonstrate due diligence and supports risk management for your property.
