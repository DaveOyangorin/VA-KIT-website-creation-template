Common questions

01How does polyurethane concrete lifting work?

We drill small 5/8"-3/4" ports, inject high‑density polyurethane beneath the slab, and the expanding foam fills voids and raises the concrete. Laser levels guide controlled lifts for proper slope and alignment. Because the foam is closed‑cell and lightweight, it won’t add significant load to Red Hills soils. Holes are patched after injection, leaving a clean, long‑lasting repair that avoids demolition and days of downtime.

02How long before I can walk or drive on my slab?

Foam cures fast. Most sidewalks and patios are ready for foot traffic in 15-30 minutes. Driveways typically support cars within 30-60 minutes, depending on slab thickness and temperature. We verify lift and cure with laser levels and temperature checks, then advise on drive‑on timing before we leave. This quick turnaround is why many St. George homeowners choose foam jacking over replacement.

03Is foam safe and environmentally friendly?

Yes. We use inert, closed‑cell polyurethane designed for geotechnical lifting. It’s hydro‑insensitive, so it resists water intrusion and will not leach. The process requires fewer, smaller holes than mudjacking and creates minimal spoil. We also control dust with HEPA vacs and follow OSHA silica rules. It’s a clean, low‑impact option for neighborhoods near Pioneer Park and the Bluff Street corridor.

04What areas around St. George do you serve?

We cover St. George and surrounding communities, including the Pioneer Park area (84770), Downtown, Utah Tech University, Bloomington, Little Valley, Tonaquint, Middleton, Washington City, Santa Clara, Ivins, and Hurricane. If you’re within about 25-30 miles of St. George, we likely serve your address. Ask about same‑day estimates based on crew availability.

05What types of slabs can be lifted (driveways, sidewalks, pool decks)?

We lift residential and commercial slabs: driveways, sidewalks, patios, porches and steps, garage floors, pool decks, curbs and gutters, storefront plazas, and warehouse or loading‑dock slabs. We also level municipal sidewalks and park paths to address ADA trip hazards. After lifting, we can seal joints with polyurea and inject epoxy in cracks to help keep water out.

06Do you offer void filling and soil stabilization?

Yes. We perform undersealing to fill gaps and deep foam injection to densify weak soils. This helps counter washouts from irrigation and monsoon storms common around Red Hills Parkway. We select hydrophobic vs. hydrophilic foams based on moisture and can use a grid pattern to stabilize large panels. These steps reduce the chance of future settlement.

07How much does concrete leveling cost vs. replacement?

Leveling is typically 30-70% less than replacement. Many projects fall between $6-$15 per sq ft for lifting, while replacement can run $10-$25+ per sq ft plus demolition and downtime. Every site is different - soil conditions, access, and slab size matter - so we provide a free on‑site inspection and a written estimate before work begins.

08Will you seal cracks and joints after lifting?

We can route and fill control joints with polyurea and inject epoxy/urethane resins into structural cracks as an add‑on. Sealing helps keep water out of the base, improving long‑term stability. We also recommend concrete sealers (silane‑siloxane) in exposed areas and can provide a maintenance plan for high‑traffic sidewalks and driveways.

09Do I need a city permit to repair ADA trip hazards on public sidewalks near Pioneer Park?

For private walkways on your property, permits typically aren’t required. Work within the public right‑of‑way may need a City of St. George encroachment permit and pedestrian control per MUTCD. We handle Blue Stakes of Utah 811 utility locates (requested at least 3 business days in advance) and can assist with permit coordination when your slab borders the ROW.

10Which is better: mudjacking or polyjacking?

Both work, but foam has key advantages in St. George: smaller holes, lighter weight, quicker cure, and reduced added load to soils. Mudjacking can be cost‑effective on large, thick slabs or where heavy fill is desired. We offer both and will explain polyjacking vs. mudjacking for your site so you can choose the best value.
