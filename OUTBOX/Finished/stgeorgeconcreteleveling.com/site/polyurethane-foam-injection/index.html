Common questions

01How do I know if my concrete needs lifting?

Look for trip edges over 1/2 inch, puddling after irrigation or storms, doors that stick or rub, and gaps under steps or slabs. Sunken panels next to intact ones, visible voids along slab edges, and cracked caulk lines also point to settlement. We’ll measure differential elevations, check drainage, and probe soils to confirm if lifting, void filling, or soil stabilization is the right fix for your St. George property.

02How long does the process take, and when can I use the surface?

Most residential lifts finish in a few hours, and surfaces are typically ready for light use within 15-30 minutes after injection. Driveways and garage floors can often handle vehicles the same day. Commercial aisles and docks are scheduled to minimize downtime. We’ll confirm cure time on site, factoring in slab thickness, temperature, and the specific foam formulation used.

03Polyjacking vs. mudjacking - which is better?

Polyjacking uses lightweight, closed‑cell foam (about 2-6 pcf) versus heavy mudjacking slurry (100-130 pcf), reducing the chance of re‑settlement in weak soils. Foam requires smaller holes (~5/8"), cures fast for same‑day use, and resists water. Mudjacking can be effective in some cases, but most St. George projects benefit from the precision, speed, and moisture resistance of polyurethane.

04How big are the drill holes, and will they be visible?

Access holes are typically about 5/8 inch. We lay out a clean pattern, keep holes to the minimum needed, and patch them after lifting. On broom‑finished or exposed aggregate surfaces, patches blend well. On stamped or decorative concrete, we align holes with grout lines or patterns when possible to make them discreet.

05Is polyurethane foam safe and environmentally friendly?

Once cured, closed‑cell lifting foam is inert and does not leach. We follow OSHA guidance for isocyanate handling, ensure ventilation per EPA recommendations, and maintain controlled component temperatures for proper reaction. Hydrophobic formulations resist water from storms and irrigation. If your project is near utilities or sensitive areas, we’ll select appropriate materials and document the process.

06How much does polyjacking cost compared to replacement?

Many projects price 50-70% less than tear‑out and replacement, with far less downtime. Exact cost depends on slab size, settlement depth, foam volume, access, and whether deep injection or joint sealing is needed. We provide a written, line‑item estimate after inspection so you can compare options confidently for your St. George or Washington property.

07Can you lift stamped/decorative concrete without damage?

Yes. We use smaller 5/8" holes and position ports along grout lines or hidden areas. Controlled expansion lets us lift evenly to protect patterns and borders. Afterward, we color‑match patches and can reseal the surface if needed. This approach works well for pool decks, patios, and entries across Desert Color, SunRiver, and Entrada neighborhoods.

08Do HOAs in Desert Color or SunRiver require approvals before concrete lifting?

Many HOAs appreciate notification for exterior work. We can assist with a brief scope, safety plan, and certificate of insurance to streamline approval. Because foam lifting is low impact, most communities approve it quickly. Ask your HOA for any forms; we’ll supply documentation and schedule work to minimize disruption.

09What permits are needed for sidewalk lifts in St. George or Washington?

For work within the public right‑of‑way, cities may require encroachment or right‑of‑way permits. We’ll help confirm requirements, coordinate traffic or pedestrian control if necessary, and provide insurance and licensing documentation. Private walks on your property typically don’t require permits, but we’ll verify during the site visit.

10Can deep foam injection stabilize weak or collapsible soils?

Yes. Deep injection places expanding polyurethane at depth to densify loose zones and fill voids, reducing recurring settlement under slabs, driveways, and interior floors. We design injection depths and volumes based on soil response, and we may combine lifting with joint sealing to keep water out. This method is effective in Southern Utah’s collapsible desert soils.
