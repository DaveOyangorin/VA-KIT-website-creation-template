Polyurethane Foam Injection (Polyjacking)

Foam jacking solves sunken slabs caused by voids, irrigation leaks, or collapsible soils near Padre Canyon. We drill 5/8" injection ports, pump high‑density, hydrophobic polyurethane through a mixing manifold, and monitor lift with a laser level. The small‑hole method cures in 15-30 minutes for rapid return to service. Benefits include cleaner patches, lighter material that won’t add load, and superior water resistance compared to mudjacking. Common questions

01Is it cheaper to level concrete or replace it in St. George?

In most cases, leveling costs 50-70% less than replacement and takes hours, not days. You keep the existing slab, avoid demolition and disposal, and fix drainage and trip hazards fast. Polyjacking’s small 5/8" ports and rapid cure minimize disruption, especially near landscaped areas. Replacement may be better if the concrete is severely broken, structurally unsound, or has widespread spalling. We’ll assess your slab and provide side‑by‑side costs so you can choose confidently.

02How much does concrete leveling cost in Utah?

Typical residential projects in Southern Utah range from a few hundred dollars for a single sidewalk panel to several thousand for multi‑panel driveways or large patios. Pricing depends on slab size, lift height, access, and whether deep foam injection or joint/crack sealing is needed. After a site visit with laser measurements and void mapping, we provide a fixed quote and timeline.

03What’s the difference between mudjacking and polyjacking?

Mudjacking uses cementitious slurry through 1‑1/2" holes - good for some applications but heavier and more invasive. Polyjacking injects high‑density polyurethane foam through 5/8" ports, expands to lift, and cures in 15-30 minutes. Foam is lighter, water‑resistant, and ideal near pool decks, entries, and decorative concrete. We also offer slabjacking and deep foam injection when subgrade densification is needed. We’ll recommend the best method for your slab and budget.

04How long does concrete leveling last?

With proper joint sealing and drainage, foam lifting can last decades. Our hydrophobic polyurethane resists water intrusion, and deep foam injection stabilizes weak soils to prevent re‑settlement. Maintenance matters: keep downspouts directed away from slabs, maintain caulking at joints and cracks, and correct irrigation leaks quickly to protect the subgrade and your investment.

05How soon can I drive on a driveway after foam lifting?

Most driveways are ready for light vehicle traffic in about 30-60 minutes after polyjacking. Timing varies with temperature, slab thickness, and lift magnitude. We verify cure and elevation with a laser level before reopening. For safety, we’ll mark the area, provide re‑entry guidance, and complete joint sealing to keep water out. Commercial aprons and heavier loads may require a longer wait - our crew will advise on site.

06Can you level pool decks and patios near Tuacahn?

Yes. We frequently level pool decks, coping, and patios around Tuacahn, Entrada, and Kayenta. Hydrophobic foam displaces water and fills voids caused by washouts or irrigation. We use staged lifts to protect tile and grout, and we seal control joints after lifting. Most areas reopen the same day, with color‑matched patches for a clean finish that blends with your existing surface.

07Will leveling fix drainage and water pooling issues?

Often, yes. By restoring slope and re‑establishing elevations, leveling directs water away from garages, entries, and foundations. We can add deep foam injection to stabilize soft spots, then finish with joint and crack sealing to reduce water intrusion. For chronic issues, we may recommend simple drainage corrections. During your estimate, we’ll laser‑measure elevations and show you projected runoff improvements after lifting.

08What size holes are drilled for foam vs. mudjacking?

Polyjacking uses small 5/8" injection ports spaced strategically; patches are compact and color‑matched. Mudjacking typically requires 1‑1/2" holes for cementitious grout. For sensitive finishes, pool decks, and front entries, foam’s smaller footprint and fast cure are strong advantages. We’ll explain which method fits your slab, expected lift, and aesthetic goals during the site assessment.

09Do I need a permit to level a sidewalk in the public Right‑of‑Way?

Yes, if the sidewalk is within the City of St. George or Ivins public Right‑of‑Way, a permit and traffic/pedestrian control are typically required. We handle permitting, barricades, and ADA bevel requirements. For private sidewalks on your property, permits are usually not needed, but ADA trip‑hazard thresholds still guide how we correct height differences and transitions safely.

10Can foam injection stabilize collapsible or caliche soils near Snow Canyon/Padre Canyon?

It can. Deep foam injection densifies weak or collapsible soils beneath slabs and works well around caliche layers by filling fissures and binding loose zones. We start with probe tests and void mapping, then inject at depth before surface lifting. This two‑step approach reduces future settlement, especially in areas affected by monsoon washouts or irrigation leaks common in Southern Utah.
