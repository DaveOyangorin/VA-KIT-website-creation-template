Concrete Leveling & Slab Lifting Services for Homes and Businesses

We restore safety and slope to sunken concrete with precision leveling methods tailored to Southern Utah soils.

Driveway Leveling

Uneven driveways cause puddling, tire wear, and trip hazards at garage entries. We lift settled panels using polyjacking (high-density polyurethane foam injection) through 5/8" holes and verify slope with a laser level. Foam expands 25-35x to fill voids and stabilize weak subgrade. Most homeowners can drive on leveled panels within hours, not days. We finish with control joint caulking to keep water out. If you’re comparing mudjacking vs. foam in Leeds, we’ll explain pros, cons, and costs for your exact slab thickness and access.

Sidewalk Leveling

Lifted or sunken sidewalks create liability - especially near schools, churches, and HOA paths. We remove trip hazards by lifting slabs to meet ADA thresholds and ensure smooth transitions. Our minimally invasive process uses a polyurethane proportioner rig, heated hoses, and an injection gun to place the right foam at the right depth. We can often complete a typical residential run in one visit with same-day pedestrian access. Need right-of-way work? We handle Blue Stakes 811 and temporary traffic control per MUTCD Part 6 when required.

Concrete Slab Lifting

Garage floors, interior slabs, and patio panels settle from erosion, collapsible soils, or plumbing leaks. We correct settlement with a grid of injection points and deep foam injection when soil densification is needed. Hydrophobic foams are available for wet conditions. After lift, we seal joints with polyurethane sealant and backer rod to prevent water infiltration. Our team follows OSHA silica controls with vacuums and PPE while drilling. Ask about before-and-after photos from projects near Red Cliffs and Quail Creek.

Trip Hazard Removal

Heaves at seams as small as 1/4" can violate ADA guidelines and increase liability on walkways and commercial entries. We evaluate whether lifting, grinding, or a combined approach is best. Where undersealing is needed, we fill voids to stop rocking panels. We use concrete grinders only when lifting won’t resolve a lip, then seal joints to keep the repair lasting longer. Our process restores safe, code-conscious access for residences, HOAs, retail, and municipal paths across Washington County.

Common questions

01What material is used to lift and support concrete - polyurethane foam or mudjacking?

We offer both. Polyjacking uses high-density, closed-cell polyurethane foam that expands to fill voids and lift slabs with minimal weight added. Mudjacking uses a sand/cement slurry through larger holes and can be a lower-cost option. In Leeds and Washington County, we often recommend foam for faster cure, smaller holes, and better long-term water resistance. We’ll compare options on site and explain which best fits your slab thickness, access, moisture, and budget.

02When can the concrete be used after lifting?

Polyurethane foam cures quickly. Most sidewalks and patios are walkable in about 30 minutes, and driveways or garage slabs can typically handle vehicles in 2-4 hours, depending on temperature and slab size. Commercial floors often reopen the same day. Mudjacking may require a longer wait. We’ll confirm timing before we leave so you can return to normal use safely.

03Will concrete lifting create a lot of mess?

No. Polyjacking is minimally invasive. We drill 5/8" holes, vacuum the cuttings, inject foam, and patch the ports with color-matched cementitious material. There’s no heavy demolition, and landscaping usually remains undisturbed. We protect adjacent finishes, manage dust with vacuums per OSHA 1926.1153, and leave the site clean. You’ll see small patched circles where we injected - far less disruptive than replacement.

04How does Deep Foam Injection address unstable soils?

Deep Foam Injection places high-density polyurethane at depth to densify weak or collapsible soils beneath the slab. Using probing rods, we identify voids and soft layers, then inject in stages to build a stable base. This compaction grouting approach reduces future settlement and improves load-bearing capacity - ideal for garage floors, pool decks, warehouse slabs, and areas affected by erosion or irrigation leaks common in Southern Utah.

05Is concrete leveling cheaper than replacement?

Yes, in most cases lifting saves 50% or more versus tear-out and re-pour - and it’s completed far faster. You also avoid mismatched finishes and long cure times. Costs vary based on panel size, thickness, access, void volume, and whether joint/crack sealing is included. We provide a written estimate with options, so you can choose the best value for your Leeds property.

06How long does polyurethane foam concrete lifting last?

High-quality closed-cell foams are engineered to be water-resistant and stable. With proper drainage and sealed joints, lifts can last many years. We target root causes - voids, moisture, and weak soils - with undersealing and optional deep injections.

07How big are the drill holes for polyjacking?

We typically drill 5/8" diameter holes on a grid tailored to the slab’s size and settlement pattern. The small holes allow precise injections and a cleaner finish compared to mudjacking’s larger ports (often 1-2"). After lifting and stabilization, we patch the holes flush and color-blend so they’re discreet while preserving the panel’s integrity.

08Do you contact Blue Stakes of Utah 811 before drilling in Leeds 84746?

Yes. We submit a Blue Stakes 811 locate request at least 3 business days before work when excavation or subsurface drilling could affect utilities. Marks are typically valid for 21 days. We coordinate timing with you to keep the project on schedule and safe. Private utilities (like irrigation or landscape lighting) may require additional locating, which we’ll discuss during the estimate.

09What is the typical price to level a two‑slab driveway panel in Leeds?

Most two-panel lifts in Leeds fall in a general range of $450-$1,100, influenced by void size, access, slab thickness, number of injection points, and whether joint/crack sealing or deep foam injection is needed. After a site visit, we’ll provide a fixed quote and options. We can also discuss mudjacking vs. foam to match your budget and performance goals.

10What permits or traffic control are required for public sidewalks?

If work occurs in public right‑of‑way, temporary traffic control per MUTCD Part 6 may be required, and municipal permissions may apply. We handle plans, signage, and pedestrian routing where needed to keep the site safe and compliant. For private property and HOAs, we coordinate access and closures to minimize disruption and meet ADA trip hazard thresholds.

11Can foam leveling improve drainage during monsoon storms, and do you seal joints?

Yes. We lift to re-establish positive slope away from foundations, improving runoff during Southern Utah’s monsoon season. After leveling, we seal control and expansion joints with polyurethane sealant and backer rod to reduce water infiltration that can reopen voids. We’ll also recommend simple grading or downspout adjustments if needed to protect your repair.

12Do you offer both mudjacking and polyurethane, and help me choose?

We do. Polyurethane excels for smaller holes, faster return to service, and long-term moisture resistance. Mudjacking can be a good fit for thicker, lightly loaded slabs on a tighter budget. We’ll assess the slab’s condition, soil, and usage, then present clear pros and cons so you can choose confidently for your Leeds property.
