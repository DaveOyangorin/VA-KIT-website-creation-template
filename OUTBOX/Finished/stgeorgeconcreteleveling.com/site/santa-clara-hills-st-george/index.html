Common questions

01How do I know if my concrete needs lifting?

Look for trip edges over 1/2 inch, pooling near joints, gaps under slabs, doors rubbing, and uneven garage aprons. In Santa Clara and St. George, irrigation and collapsible soils often cause voids. We assess with a laser level, probe for hollow spots, and check drainage. If the slab is structurally sound, lifting is faster, cleaner, and usually more economical than replacement - often done in hours, not days.

02How long does polyurethane lifting take and when can I use the slab?

Most residential projects finish in 2-4 hours. Polyurethane cures quickly; foot traffic is typically allowed within 30 minutes. Driveways can often handle vehicles in about 30-60 minutes, depending on temperature and load. We verify stability, seal ports, and complete joint/crack work before we leave, so you can get back to normal the same day in St. George and Santa Clara Hills.

03Is polyurethane foam safe and environmentally friendly?

We use high‑density, closed‑cell foam that is inert after curing and resists water infiltration. During installation, our crew follows OSHA and NIOSH guidance for isocyanate handling with proper PPE. Because we drill small ports and avoid heavy demolition, there’s far less dust and debris than replacement - an effective, low‑waste option for Southern Utah properties.

04What causes concrete to sink in Santa Clara/St. George?

Common causes include collapsible desert soils, moisture changes around caliche layers, irrigation leaks, poor downspout routing, and compaction shortfalls during original construction. Along the Snow Canyon corridor, voids can form near lava rock pockets. Our approach combines foam expansion for void fill, soil densification, and drainage corrections to reduce future settlement.

05How much does leveling cost compared to replacement?

Leveling typically runs 30-70% less than tear‑out and repour, with far less disruption. Pricing varies by void size, access, and the number of injection ports. After a free on‑site assessment, we provide a fixed, itemized proposal. For many Santa Clara Hills homeowners, lifting restores use the same day and avoids color/texture mismatches that can occur with new concrete.

06Do you seal cracks and joints after lifting?

Yes. Post‑lift, we offer epoxy crack repair and polyurea joint filling with backer rod where needed. We also perform perimeter saw‑and‑seal and recommend downspout or grading fixes if water is contributing to settlement. This finishing work helps lock out moisture, improve appearance, and extend the life of your lift in St. George’s climate.

07What’s the difference between mudjacking and polyjacking?

Mudjacking uses a cementitious slurry; it can be effective but requires larger holes and adds significant weight. Polyjacking uses lightweight, high‑density foam injected through small 5/8 in. ports, expanding to fill voids and lift slabs with less added load. In desert soils around Santa Clara Hills, polyjacking often provides faster cure times and better long‑term performance.

08Do I need a city permit to level a sidewalk or driveway, and who handles trip‑hazard repairs?

For most private slabs on your property, permits aren’t required for lifting. Public sidewalks may involve city authorization, especially for ADA trip‑hazard remediation. In St. George and Santa Clara, owners adjacent to the sidewalk are typically responsible for hazards. We can guide you on local requirements and provide documentation for HOAs or inspectors.

09Can you level post‑tension or stamped concrete without damage?

Yes. We locate tendons, adjust port spacing, and control lift with laser monitoring to protect post‑tension systems. For stamped surfaces, we place ports at grout lines or discreet locations and patch to blend. The small port size and careful lift sequencing help preserve finishes while restoring elevation.
