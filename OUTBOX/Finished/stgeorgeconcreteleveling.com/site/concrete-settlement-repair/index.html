Common questions

01What causes concrete settlement in St. George, UT?

Common causes include collapsible desert soils, irrigation or plumbing leaks that wash out fines, poor compaction during construction, and drainage that concentrates water along slab edges. Monsoon storms and the area’s red rock/clay soils can accelerate movement. We identify the source, fill voids, densify subgrades, and restore elevation so your driveway, sidewalk, patio, or garage floor remains stable in Southern Utah’s climate.

02Polyjacking vs. Mudjacking: Which is better for my slab?

Polyjacking uses high-density polyurethane foam that expands quickly, cures in minutes, and requires smaller holes. Mudjacking uses a cement-sand slurry that’s heavier and cures slower. We provide both and recommend the best fit based on load, soil moisture, access, and budget. For wet or sandy soils, hydrophobic foam often performs better. For thick, heavily loaded panels, grout can be a cost-effective option.

03How much does concrete leveling cost in St. George?

Costs vary by slab size, required lift, number of injection points, and whether deep foam injection or crack/joint sealing is needed. Many small trip hazards can be corrected for a few hundred dollars, while larger driveways/patios range higher. Leveling typically saves 50-70% versus replacement. We provide a free on-site estimate in 84770, 84790, and surrounding ZIPs, with a clear scope and fixed pricing.

04How long does polyurethane foam lifting last?

High-quality, closed-cell polyurethane foam is durable, water-resistant, and chemically stable. When paired with proper void filling, joint sealing, and drainage improvements, results are long-lasting. Lifespan depends on soil conditions and load patterns. We tailor foam density and injection depth to the site and provide maintenance tips to help your slab stay level for years.

05When can I walk or drive on the slab after lifting?

Most foam-lifted slabs are walkable within minutes and drivable in about 30-60 minutes, depending on slab thickness, temperature, and load. Mudjacking cures slower and may require longer wait times. We confirm reopening timelines before we leave and provide written guidance so you can use your driveway, sidewalk, or patio with confidence.

06Do I need a City of St. George permit to level a sidewalk in the public right-of-way?

If the work is within the public right-of-way, permits and traffic/pedestrian control may be required. We handle the City of St. George ROW process, set safe detours, and meet ADA slope/edge requirements. For private property sidewalks, permits usually aren’t required, but utility locates (Blue Stakes 811) are still recommended for safety.

07Can you level concrete during winter or extreme summer heat in Southern Utah?

Yes. We adjust methods and materials for seasonal temperatures, including hot-weather handling and cure management. Polyurethane injection performs well in a wide range of conditions. We’ll schedule work in optimal windows, protect adjacent finishes, and verify cure before reopening to foot or vehicle traffic.

08Will polyurethane foam withstand flash floods and high groundwater near the Virgin River?

Closed-cell, hydrophobic polyurethane resists water intrusion and maintains strength when exposed to moisture. We frequently use it near wet or flood-prone zones. We may also recommend drainage improvements or deeper injections to stabilize weak layers. Each site is evaluated for soil type, moisture, and expected loads to ensure a durable repair.

09Do you offer HOAs and property managers multi-site trip hazard programs?

Yes. We create maintenance plans for HOAs, apartments, and retail centers, including surveys, prioritized repairs, ADA trip hazard removal, and scheduled resealing. Bundling sites reduces cost and disruption. We can coordinate off-hours work to keep residents and shoppers moving safely during business hours.
