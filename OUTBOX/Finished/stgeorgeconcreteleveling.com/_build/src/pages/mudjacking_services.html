Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my concrete needs lifting?

Signs include pooling water near your foundation, doors or garage thresholds that rub, and trip edges over 1/4 inch. You might also notice gaps under slabs or rocking panels. We measure elevations with a laser level and inspect for voids. If the slab is structurally sound, lifting is usually more cost-effective than replacement in St. George’s climate.

How much does mudjacking cost per square foot?

Pricing varies by slab thickness, access, severity, and void size. In Southern Utah, many projects fall between $6-$12 per square foot, with small minimums often landing between $600-$900. Larger driveways, garage slabs, and commercial entries may be quoted by section or panel. We provide a firm, written estimate after a quick on-site assessment.

Mudjacking vs polyjacking: which lasts longer?

Both can last many years when subgrade issues are addressed. Mudjacking uses a cement-based slurry that’s heavy and excellent for filling large voids. Polyjacking (foam jacking) is lighter, hydrophobic, and cures fast - often ideal for deep voids or wet soils. We recommend the method that best stabilizes your soil conditions in St. George and meets your budget.

How long after mudjacking can I use my driveway?

Foot traffic is typically fine within hours. For vehicle traffic, we generally advise 24-48 hours to let patches set and joints cure, depending on temperature and slab thickness. We’ll confirm timing at completion. Polyurethane foam options can support loads much sooner if you need faster turnaround.

Do I need a permit for mudjacking on city sidewalks in St. George?

For work within the public right-of-way, the City may require an encroachment/right‑of‑way permit and traffic control per MUTCD. We handle permit coordination and set up safe pedestrian detours. On private property, permits are usually not required for leveling, but HOA approvals may apply. We’ll confirm requirements before work begins.

What causes concrete to sink in Utah’s climate?

Common causes include poorly compacted fill, sandy or collapsible soils, irrigation leaks, downspout discharge, and erosion from monsoon storms. Freeze-thaw cycles and heavy vehicle loads can accelerate settlement. Our solution densifies soils, fills voids, and improves drainage to reduce movement in areas like 84770, 84790, and nearby zip codes.

Can you level interior slabs without tearing them out?

Yes. We drill small holes (often 5/8"-1") through the slab, inject slurry or foam, and lift gradually. Dust is controlled per OSHA 1926.1153, and we protect adjacent finishes. This is common for sunken interior garage slabs or rooms converted over slabs in St. George homes and commercial spaces.

Can you work during extreme summer heat in Southern Utah?

We can. We schedule early, shade work zones, and adjust mix water and set times to maintain lift control. For specific conditions, polyurethane foam may be recommended due to its quick cure and moisture resistance. We follow hot-weather best practices to keep results precise even in July and August.

Will leveling correct water drainage away from my foundation?

Often, yes. By restoring pitch, we redirect surface water away from the structure. We also seal joints to limit infiltration and can suggest downspout extensions or minor grading adjustments. If needed, we incorporate stone slurry grout or foam to stabilize weak soils that contribute to recurring pooling near foundations.
