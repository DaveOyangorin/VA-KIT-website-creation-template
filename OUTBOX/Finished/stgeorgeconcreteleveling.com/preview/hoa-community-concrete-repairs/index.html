Common questions

01Who is responsible for HOA sidewalk repairs in St. George?

In most communities, the HOA maintains common-area sidewalks and pathways, while the city handles public right‑of‑way panels along public streets. Many HOAs in St. George have responsibility inside the community, especially near clubhouses, pool decks, and interior streets. We can review your CCRs and site map, identify who owns which sections, and advise when a St. George City sidewalk/right‑of‑way permit is needed for work outside HOA property.

02What is the fastest way to remove sidewalk trip hazards in our community?

For small vertical offsets (about 1/4” to 3/4”), precision grinding removes the lip quickly and cleanly. For larger settlements or rocking slabs, polyurethane lifting (polyjacking) relevels the panel and stabilizes voids. Both methods are fast and low‑disruption. We set up traffic control, use HEPA dust extraction, and can reopen most areas the same day - ideal for occupied neighborhoods across St. George and Washington City.

03Polyjacking vs mudjacking for HOA sidewalks - what’s better?

Polyjacking injects a lightweight, water‑resistant foam that expands to lift and support slabs with small, clean injection ports. Mudjacking uses a heavier cement slurry that can add weight and may wash out. In Southern Utah, polyurethane often performs better for sidewalk leveling due to fast cure times and minimal mess. We still recommend mudjacking in limited cases; we’ll compare options and costs for your site.

04Do we need a St. George permit for sidewalk replacement in our HOA?

Grinding and lifting inside private common areas typically don’t require a city permit. Full panel replacement in the public right‑of‑way usually needs a St. George sidewalk/right‑of‑way permit and traffic control plan. If state routes are affected, UDOT encroachment permits may apply. We handle permitting, inspections, and restoration requirements so your project passes smoothly and reopens on schedule.

05What are ADA/PROWAG requirements for curb ramps and cross‑slopes?

ADA and PROWAG call for curb ramps with a 1:12 running slope (8.33%) and a 2% maximum cross‑slope on pedestrian access routes. Detectable warning surfaces (truncated domes) are typically required at curb ramp entries. We verify slopes with digital inclinometers, install compliant warning tiles in approved colors/sizes, and align transitions per current guidance so your community meets audit expectations.

06How quickly can residents use sidewalks after polyurethane lifting?

Most polyurethane lifting work is walkable within 30-45 minutes and ready for light carts or maintenance vehicles within a few hours. That quick return to service is a major reason HOAs choose polyjacking over replacement. We phase work to keep ADA‑compliant routes open and post clear signage so residents know when areas are safe to use again.

07How much do HOA concrete repairs cost per panel or linear foot?

Costs vary by method and condition. As a planning range, grinding small offsets may start per location, polyjacking per panel is typically mid‑range, and full panel replacement costs more due to demo, base, and concrete. We provide unit pricing by panel and linear foot with options to match your reserve funds. Same‑day estimates are available in St. George and nearby communities.

08Can you work around residents and maintain access during repairs?

Yes. We phase work, post resident notices, and establish pedestrian detours with cones, barricades, and signage. Our crews use OSHA‑compliant dust controls and keep pathways clean. When closing a route, we maintain an ADA‑compliant alternative and schedule work windows around community activities, including pool hours, trash pickup, and deliveries. Communication is built into our process.

09Do you call Blue Stakes 811 before drilling or excavation?

Always. We contact Blue Stakes of Utah 811 to locate utilities before drilling, coring, or excavation. This protects residents, avoids service disruptions, and is required by law. For private utilities within HOAs, we coordinate additional locates as needed. You get documentation of all locates as part of our project file and closeout package.

10What warranties do you provide for HOA concrete repairs?

We back our work with written warranties tailored to the repair type - polyjacking, grinding, or panel replacement - and provide maintenance recommendations to protect your investment.
