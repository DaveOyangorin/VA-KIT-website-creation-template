Outdoors

The yard, the drive and the outside of the house, including the water that is buried under all of it.

34 kinds of work below, across 4 parts of the business. Prices go on each page as they are set, and never before. Read our Plain Terms

Outside is where this county is hardest on a house. The sun takes the coating off a south wall years before the north one, the water bill is mostly the yard, and the pipe that fails most expensively is the one nobody can see. This page is everything from the front door outward.

Sewer and water line work sits here as well as under plumbing, because a sewer line is a trench across your yard before it is anything else. Same page, same quote, either way.

Three numbers that decide outdoor work here

101.9°F. The normal daily high in St. George in July, on NOAA’s 1991 to 2020 record for the city’s own station, with August at 99.9°F. Benjamin Moore caps application of its exterior coating at a surface temperature of 100°F and PPG at 100°F ambient. An average July afternoon here is at the edge of what the manufacturers publish, before you measure the wall, which in sun is hotter than the air. It is why exterior work follows the shade around a house instead of the schedule.

$3 a square foot. What the Washington County Water Conservancy District pays on the first 1,000 square feet of lawn removed, and $2 a square foot after that, capped at $100,000 per property per calendar year. The yard is most of a water bill here and the district will help pay to change it. We handle that paperwork rather than handing you a form.

The most troublesome condition the state maps. That is the Utah Geological Survey’s own phrase for expansive soil and rock — clay that swells when wet and shrinks when dry — and it illustrates the hazard with cracks in a Santa Clara house foundation. Collapsible soil does the opposite and settles when it gets wet. Both mean the same thing outdoors: water in the wrong place, from an irrigation line or a downspout or a failed sewer lateral, is a foundation question and not just a water-bill question.

Every figure above is somebody else’s published number, and the pages below that rest on one cite it.

Lawn to water-wise

The yard is most of a water bill here, and the district pays toward taking the grass out. We handle the rebate paperwork rather than leaving it with you.

  • Turf removal

    Cut, remove and haul the sod, then rough grade.

  • Water-wise conversion

    Turf out, soil prep, drip conversion and desert planting.

  • Designed conversion

    The same work with designed beds, boulder placement and a plant schedule.

  • Rebate application management

    Pre-inspection, measurement, photographs and the paperwork. Nobody else here does this for you.

  • Landscape design and plan

    A measured plan, plant schedule and drip layout.

Part of Water-wise landscape, where this work is written up in full.

Irrigation

A converted yard still needs water, delivered to the root of a plant instead of thrown into the air where most of it leaves before it lands.

  • Drip irrigation conversion

    Spray heads out, drip in, zone by zone.

  • Smart controller install

    A controller that waters to the weather, not to a habit.

  • Backflow test, annual

    The test your water provider requires, filed for you.

Hardscape

The parts of a yard that do not need watering at all: paving, walls, steps and the routes between them.

  • Paver patio

    Excavate, compacted base, bedding sand, pavers and edge restraint.

  • Paver walkway

    The same build, narrower run, more cuts.

  • Paver driveway

    Heavier base and thicker paver for vehicle load.

  • Concrete flatwork

    Form, base, pour and broom finish.

  • Decorative and stamped concrete

    Stamp pattern with integral or release color.

  • Retaining wall

    Block or boulder wall, drained and built to hold.

  • Sealing, pavers or concrete

    Clean, dry and seal. Recurring work, not once.

Painting outside

Outside is where the sun decides everything. The south and west walls of a house fail years before the north one, and the work is scheduled around the shade for that reason.

  • Exterior repaint

    The whole envelope, prepared and coated in the order and the weather the manufacturer publishes.

  • Stucco painting and coating

    The most common exterior in Washington County, and the one with the most ways to go wrong.

  • Trim, fascia and garage doors

    The parts the sun destroys first, repainted without doing the whole house.

Driveways, patios and decks

Exterior concrete gets heat, ultraviolet and freeze-thaw that an interior slab never sees, so it is a different product decision.

  • Driveway and apron coating

    The slab that takes the sun, the tires and the blown sand. Exterior work is scheduled around weather, not around us.

  • Patio and porch coating

    Resurfacing the slab you have, rather than breaking it out.

  • Pool deck coating

    Textured on purpose. A deck has to stay walkable wet and bearable barefoot.

  • Walkways and steps

    The awkward work: edges, risers, nosings and curves, most of it done by hand.

Part of Concrete coatings, where this work is written up in full.

The slab underneath

Almost every coating failure is a preparation failure. This is the work under the finish, and it is most of the labor.

  • Concrete slab assessment

    Moisture tested to the published methods, the surface profile agreed, and you get the readings whether or not you hire us.

  • Concrete grinding and preparation

    Diamond grinding to take the slab back past its surface film. It is most of the labor and all of the difference.

  • Failed coating removal and recoat

    Taking off what is peeling, finding out why it did, and telling you before we quote putting anything back.

  • Concrete cleaning and sealing

    Washing and sealing bare or stained concrete. Recurring work, not once.

Part of Concrete coatings, where this work is written up in full.

Sewer lines, without the trench

The most expensive pipe in a house is the one nobody can see. Lining and bursting replace it without opening the whole yard.

  • Sewer lining, no digging

    Clean, camera, line and cure the existing lateral in place.

  • Pipe bursting

    Burst the old lateral and pull a new one through, two pits instead of a trench.

  • Sewer line replacement

    Open trench replacement where lining will not hold.

  • Sewer spot repair

    One break, excavated and repaired.
