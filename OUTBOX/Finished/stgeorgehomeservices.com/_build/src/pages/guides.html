Guides

Read the document before you sign anything. We did.

Each guide answers one question a homeowner here actually asks, from the statute, the city code, the program rules or the manufacturer’s own warranty. Every source is named. None of it is legal advice.

Plumbing

  • Your backflow test notice, explained

    St. George requires an approved assembly on every outside watering system, protected from freezing, and tested every year. Here is what that device does.

  • A pipe broke in the wall of your condo. Who pays?

    Utah Code § 57-8-7.2 draws the line at the finished surface. Everything behind it is common area, and a pipe gets sorted by how many units it serves.

  • What St. George water does to a water heater

    The city says set your softener to 18 grains. The tankless makers stop at 200 mg/L. The warranty documents, quoted, and what to do about it.

  • A heat pump water heater in a 110 degree garage

    The usual objection to a heat pump water heater is that it chills the room it sits in. Here that is the feature, and the real questions are different.

  • Hydro jetting or a cable? What each one actually does

    A cable punches a hole through the blockage. A jetter scours the pipe wall. They are different jobs, and on some pipe a jetter is the wrong tool entirely.

  • Look up your property's hazards in the county's own maps

    Washington County publishes 24 free hazard map layers built on Utah Geological Survey Special Study 127. How to read them, and what each means for your pipes.

  • Gray pipe, 1978 to the mid-90s: how to tell, and what to do

    Polybutylene is gray, flexible, and stamped PB2110. Washington County's housing stock is not old enough for clay sewers and is exactly the right age for this.

  • Radon in a slab-on-grade house

    Washington County sits in the EPA's middle radon band. That predicts nothing about your house. A test does, and it costs almost nothing.

  • Closing up a St. George second home for the summer

    Everyone writes about winterizing. Here the house sits empty through 100 degree heat and monsoon season, with the irrigation running for nobody. That is the risk worth planning for.

  • Slab leaks in a slab-on-grade house, and where your shutoff is

    A warm spot on the floor, a bill that jumped, water you can hear with everything off. How the leak gets found, and what to settle before anyone cuts.

  • Why your relief valve drips, and then stops

    A relief valve that weeps for a few minutes and then quits is almost never a bad valve. It is thermal expansion in a closed system, and it has a fix.

  • Why a sewer replacement costs more here

    The national trenchless market runs on old pipe. St. George mostly has young pipe in bad ground. That changes which method applies, and it is the reason the number is higher.

  • Why your water heater has to be strapped here

    Utah requires your water heater to be anchored, everywhere in the state. St. George's own design criteria show why it matters more here.

  • It is two in the morning and something is wrong

    What is an emergency, what can wait until the daytime rate, and the three things to do before you call anybody at all.

  • How to check a contractor before you let them in the house

    The license number is two numbers, not one. Utah law says they have to give it to you on request — and a written contract with a licensed contractor is what stops a subcontractor you never met from putting a lien on your house.

  • The gray pipe: how to tell if your house has polybutylene

    Installed from about 1978 to the mid-1990s, it degrades from the inside where nobody can see it, and about one home in three in Washington County was built in the two decades it was on the market. The class-action money closed in 2009. Here is how to identify it yourself in ten minutes.

Accessible living

  • Curbless walk-in showers: the complete guide

    A standard 60-inch tub occupies the same footprint as a 60 by 30 roll-in shower. The numbers, the slab, the hard water, and what the research honestly says.

  • Does changing the house actually help?

    The best evidence gives two answers, and they point in different directions. One of them helps us sell work. The other does not. Both are printed below.

  • Grab bars: where they go, and what holds them

    The standard is 250 pounds of force. The wall behind the bar matters more than the bar. And the objection is almost never that they do not work.

  • Does my HOA have to approve a bathroom remodel?

    Since May 6, 2026, a Utah HOA declaration may not restrict the interior of your home except for the safety of neighbors. What that means for a bathroom.

  • Your HOA cannot make you use their contractor

    Four separate provisions of Utah law say an association may not restrict you to its preferred vendor list. Two bind declarations, two bind rules. Here is where each one sits.

  • The Utah HOA Ombudsman, and how to escalate

    Utah has had a state office for association disputes since 2025. It is narrower than most people expect, and knowing the boundary before you pay the $150 is the whole trick.

  • Walk-in tub or walk-in shower?

    We install both, so we have no reason to steer you. Here is the comparison on the points that actually differ: the fill and the drain, the door seal, the threshold, and what each one does to the house.

  • Who pays for accessibility work in Utah

    Six real funding routes, what each one actually pays, and the order to work them in. Most of them require authorization before the first tile comes off.

Water-wise landscape

  • Artificial turf, measured: 200 degrees on a 98 degree day

    The highest published synthetic turf surface temperature was recorded in Utah. Watering cools it for about five minutes. Here is where turf still makes sense, and what planted conversion does instead.

  • Defensible space, water savings and your wildfire fee

    Utah HB 48 took effect on January 1, 2026. If your property is inside the high risk boundary, a county officer now assesses an annual fee on it. The work that lowers your classification is mostly the same work that lowers your water bill.

  • Getting a Level 2 charger installed here

    The federal credit expired on June 30, 2026 and half the internet has not noticed. Which utility serves you depends on which side of the Virgin River you live on. And you probably do not need the panel upgrade you were quoted.

  • Above the flood zone and still at risk

    In 2005 the Santa Clara River destroyed about 30 homes at roughly half the 100 year flow. The mechanism was bank erosion, not rising water.

  • A board's guide to converting common-area lawn

    The hard part is not the landscape. It is the budget calendar, the reserve study, and a recorded easement that lasts forever. Here is the sequence in the order a board has to run it.

  • Your HOA denied your project. Here is what the letter has to say.

    Utah Code § 57-8a-109(4) requires a written denial to name the provision relied on and the specific part of your plan that fails it. Most letters do not.

  • Can my HOA make me keep my grass?

    Utah Code § 57-8a-231 and St. George City Code § 10-7-7 both limit how much lawn anyone can require. Here is what the documents say, section by section.

  • The turf rebate, and the easement you sign for it

    WCWCD pays $2 per square foot to remove lawn, with no published end date, plus $1 more on the first 1,000 square feet for projects finished between June 9, 2026 and June 1, 2027. The payment buys a perpetual, recorded easement on that ground. Both halves, explained.

  • What actually grows here, and where you are

    Santa Clara is half a zone colder than St. George and it is ten minutes away. Cold air pools in the river bottoms. And there is no approved plant list for the rebate, whatever anyone told you.
