Plumbing · Trenchless and pipe replacement

Pipe bursting

A new continuous line pulled through the path of the old one from two small pits. The pipe is replaced, not patched, and the driveway stays whole.

Call (435) 850-0053Ask for a visit

One flat fee to find out what is wrong. You are told it on the phone before anyone is dispatched, and it comes off the job if you hire us. Read our Plain Terms

What the work is

A bursting head is drawn through the old pipe, splitting it outward, while a new continuous polyethylene line is pulled in behind it. Two access pits, one near the house and one at the connection, instead of a trench the length of the run.

It is the right answer when the old pipe is beyond lining: collapsed sections, badly separated joints, or a material at the end of its life. It replaces the pipe rather than resurfacing it, and the new line has no joints along the run.

The reason to care is what it does not cost you. Driveways, mature planting, paver walks and converted front yards stay where they are.

What is included

  • Camera survey and locating before anything is opened
  • Both access pits, excavated and backfilled
  • The new continuous line, pulled and connected at both ends
  • Surface protection over the work area
  • A camera run afterwards, and you keep the recordings
  • Backfill and compaction

What is not

  • Replacing driveway, paver or concrete surfaces where a pit has to go through them. Quoted once the line is located
  • Landscape replanting beyond restoring the ground
  • Work on the city's side of the connection
  • Bursting where the ground or the utilities alongside will not allow it. We will tell you at the survey

Locate first, always

Other utilities run alongside sewer laterals, and bursting displaces the ground around the pipe as it goes. Locating is not a formality. We locate, and we tell you what is down there before we price the job.

Where the run crosses a converted front yard under the water district's rebate agreement, we plan the pits around it.

What to ask anyone quoting this, us included

  • Where do the two pits go, and what is on top of them?
  • What else is in the ground along this run?
  • What is the new pipe material, and what does the manufacturer warrant it for?

What we can show you in writing

Every line traces to a document we read in full. The number after a sentence points to the research below.

  • Bursting does not fix a belly. The head follows the bore that is already there, so a sag comes back in the same place in new pipe. A sag has to be dug, re-bedded and re-graded.1
  • Where bursting genuinely wins here is a line under mature landscaping, a driveway or hardscape, where putting the surface back costs more than the premium for going trenchless.1

Read before you decide

  • Why a sewer replacement costs more here

    The national trenchless market runs on old pipe. St. George mostly has young pipe in bad ground. That changes which method applies, and it is the reason the number is higher.

  • The turf rebate, and the easement you sign for it

    WCWCD pays $2 per square foot to remove lawn, with no published end date, plus $1 more on the first 1,000 square feet for projects finished between June 9, 2026 and June 1, 2027. The payment buys a perpetual, recorded easement on that ground. Both halves, explained.
