Dammeron Valley, Washington County

In Dammeron Valley, the first question is whose pipe it is.

There is no city water department up here. Your culinary water comes from a private company running three wells, and the sewer main belongs to the water district while the lateral running out to it belongs to you. Where the blockage sits decides who pays, so it is worth finding out before anybody starts digging.

Right now

Three things, in this order.

  1. Find your shutoff and turn it. Up here that is usually at the house — garage wall, the water heater closet, or where the service comes through. Do not go looking for a curb stop at a municipal meter box; this is not a city system and you may not have one.
  2. Notice whether it is supply or drain. Water arriving under pressure is the water company’s side or yours. Water backing up — a tub filling when the washer drains, more than one fixture slow at once — is the sewer side, and that is where the ownership question starts.
  3. Call us. A person answers at any hour. We will tell you honestly whether it can wait until morning, and most things can — the daytime rate is lower and this is a drive.

Dammeron Valley, specifically

A private water company, and a sewer line that is half yours.

Dammeron Valley Water Works is a private utility, not a municipal department. The state lists it as Public Water System 27030, a community system serving 877 people as of 2025, drawing from three wells — North Ridge #3, #3A and #4. Its rates go before the Utah Public Service Commission, the way a private utility’s do.

That matters on a bad night for one practical reason: there is no city utilities desk with a 24-hour line and a truck. If the failure is on the company’s side of your meter, the answer is different from what it would be in St. George, and it is worth knowing which side you are on before you start ringing round.

The sewer is the more useful half. The water district says it plainly on its own page: it “manages this system’s main sewer line; sewer laterals are not part of the district’s system.” The main is theirs. The lateral — the pipe from your house out to that main — is yours, and so is anything wrong with it.

So when a house backs up here, the useful thing is not a plunger and it is not a guess. It is a camera down the cleanout, which tells you how far the blockage is and therefore whose pipe it is in. That is a cheap answer to an expensive question, and we would rather run it than sell you a repair on a pipe that turns out not to be yours.

One more thing worth saying out loud. Dammeron Valley sits at about 4,600 feet, roughly 1,800 feet above St. George. Advice written for town — including some of ours — assumes a municipal meter, a curb stop and a milder winter. Tell us your address and we will work from what is actually under your house.
