What is missing, and why

The figures we took out

The research holds more numbers than the site publishes. Where a figure could not be traced to a document somebody can read, it is flagged in the data and it does not appear on any page. There is a build check whose only job is to catch a number that is held back in the data and asserted in prose somewhere else, because that contradiction is how a site ends up believing its own estimate.

Some specific things you will not find here. There is no hardiness zone or winter temperature for the high communities, because the only figure available came from a third-party zip-code lookup rather than the USDA’s own tool. There are no paver base depths or compaction percentages, because the engineering bodies that publish them refused automated retrieval and nobody has opened the paper copy. There is no thermostat savings percentage, because the widely-quoted federal one could not be found on a federal page.

Thirteen documents were dropped while this page was being built. Two had gone dead. Ten sat behind a filter that refuses anything that is not a browser, so a human has to open them and nobody had. One looked like a county government address and was not: the registrable domain belonged to a private records vendor. It had been backing fourteen facts.
