Handyman work, and the part of it that needs a license.

Most of what people call handyman work, we do — 20 kinds of it, listed below. Utah lets anyone do a lot of it without a contractor license. It does not let anyone do your heating, your gas, or your plumbing system. Here is where that line sits, in the state’s own words, and how to check anyone you call.

What the state actually says

Utah has a handyman exemption. It stops before your furnace.

Under Utah Code § 58-55-305(1)(h)(i), a person can do alteration, repair, remodeling or improvement work on a building without a contractor license as long as the job is worth less than $7,000, including both labor and materials, and including every change and addition to it. That is a real, lawful exemption, and plenty of good people work under it.

Then subsection (1)(h)(ii) carves pieces back out, and these are the pieces most likely to be in your house:

  • Plumbing and electrical. Work on a system has to be done by a licensed plumbing or electrical contractor. Work on a component — the statute names “a faucet, toilet, fixture, device, outlet, or electrical switch” — may be done by a licensed journeyman. Those are two different answers, and which one applies depends on the job, not on the price.
  • Heating, ventilation and air conditioning. Installation, repair or replacement “must be performed by an HVAC contractor licensed by the division.” There is no dollar figure attached to that one. It applies to a $200 repair.
  • Gas appliances and combustion systems. Must be done by someone certified under § 58-55-308(2).
  • Alarm systems, fire sprinklers and radon mitigation. Each has its own licensed trade, named in the same subsection.
  • Over $3,000, whoever is working under the exemption has to file a one-time affirmation with the state that they carry public liability insurance, and workers’ compensation if they have employees.

So the useful question is not “are you licensed.” It is “which license, and does it cover this job.” Ask that of anybody you call. Ask it of us.

Where we stop

What we will tell you to call somebody else about.

  • Roofing. We do not do it and we do not hold the license for it.
  • Electrical. Done by our licensed electrical partner, under their license, named to you in advance. We do not pretend it is ours.
  • Appliance repair, and anything still under a manufacturer warranty that our touching it would void. We will say so rather than take the ticket.
  • A job too small to be worth the trip. We will tell you that on the phone instead of coming out and charging you to hear it.
  • A repair that does not make sense on the unit you have. If it is too far gone, that is what you will be told.

None of this is modesty. A company that says yes to everything is a company that has not told you where its edges are.
