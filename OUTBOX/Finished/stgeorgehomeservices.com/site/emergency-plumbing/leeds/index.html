Emergency plumbing

Leeds is its own town, and your water comes from an association you belong to.

Not a suburb of anywhere. Leeds has its own town government, and its culinary water comes from the Leeds Domestic Waterusers Association rather than a city utility — which means the people who run your water supply are, technically, you and your neighbors. Most property here is on septic. Both of those change what happens when something fails at nine at night.

Right now

Three things, in this order.

  1. Shut the water off at the house — where the service enters, the water heater closet, or the garage wall. Leeds is a town but it is not a big municipal system; do not assume a curb stop you have never seen.
  2. Work out which side it is. Water arriving under pressure is supply. Water backing up with more than one fixture affected is the drain side, and on septic that points at the tank before it points at the pipe.
  3. Call us. Someone answers at any hour and will tell you plainly whether it can wait until morning. Leeds sits right on I-15, so unlike the communities up SR-18 a morning visit is a genuinely small inconvenience and a cheaper one.

Leeds, specifically

An association, not a utility, and septic under most of it.

The Leeds Domestic Waterusers Association supplies culinary water to the large majority of the town. It is a members’ association rather than a municipal department, which is a distinction most people never think about until the day it matters: the questions you would ring a city utilities desk about at midnight go somewhere rather different here.

On the other side, Leeds Town itself is responsible for wastewater within the town, and most property is on septic. Newer plats are held to one septic system per nine acres, or an approved alternative treatment system — a density rule, and a sign of how the town is thinking about the ground it sits on.

On septic, the useful instinct is the opposite of the one town gives you. A single slow drain is usually a drain. Several fixtures slowing together is the tank, and the answer to that is a level check and a pump-out, not a cable through your cleanout and certainly not a jetter before anybody has looked. We will camera it and tell you which one you have.

Leeds is also the only one of the outlying communities we cover that sits east of Interstate 15, which puts it on the Ash Creek side of the county’s wastewater line rather than the water district’s. It matters for whose rules apply to a new system, and it is the kind of thing worth knowing before you plan work rather than during it.

At about 3,500 feet Leeds is the lowest of the outlying communities — roughly 700 feet above St. George rather than the 1,700 to 2,400 of the valleys up SR-18. In practice that means a morning appointment here is genuinely easy to keep, and we will usually suggest one.
