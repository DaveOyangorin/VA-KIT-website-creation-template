Indoors

Everything inside the house: the bathrooms, the kitchen, the floors, the walls and the water that runs through it.

(435) 850-0053Ask for a visit

Most people do not call a contractor by trade. They call because a room is wrong: the shower is not usable any more, the kitchen faucet drips, the wall is scarred where a shelf came down, the water leaves a ring on everything it touches. This page is the inside of the house, whichever trade the answer turns out to belong to.

Some of it appears under plumbing as well, because a tub-to-shower conversion genuinely is both. It is the same page either way, and the same quote.

Two things decide most indoor work in this county

The water. The City of St. George’s own Water Distribution Division tells residents to set a softener to 18 grains per gallon, with a maximum of 22. That is roughly 308 to 376 mg/L, which the USGS scale calls very hard, and it is why the same handful of failures repeat in this county: scale that shortens a water heater’s life, fixtures that stop sealing, glass and cabinet finishes that spot permanently if the mineral is not cleaned off before anything is coated.

The age of the house. Of Washington County’s 88,415 housing units, 7,613 were built before 1980 — about one in eleven, on the Census Bureau’s American Community Survey. That cuts both ways. In most of the county the federal lead rule does not apply and you should not be sold containment you do not need; in the older parts of St. George, Washington City and Hurricane it very much does, and the age of your house is the first question rather than a formality.

Both figures are somebody else’s published numbers, and each page below that leans on one cites it.
