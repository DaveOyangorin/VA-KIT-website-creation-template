Heating and air

Furnace replacement in St. George

The heat only, where the cooling side is sound and worth keeping.

(435) 850-0053Ask for a visit

What the work is

Gas or electric, sized to the house rather than to the old label. Venting, combustion air and the condensate on a condensing unit are part of the job, not an extra found on the day.

If the existing cooling coil sits in the airflow, it comes out and goes back in, and we check it is still the right match for the outdoor unit while it is out.

Gas or electric is usually decided for you by what is already there, but not always, and it is worth asking rather than assuming. Where a heat pump is on the table it changes the answer entirely, and that is on its own page.

Two things get checked that are easy to leave out of a quote and expensive to find on the day: whether the flue is the right size and material for the new unit, and whether the return duct is big enough to feed it. A condensing furnace also produces condensate that has to go somewhere with a trap and a drain, and in a garage or an attic that is a real piece of work rather than a footnote.

What is included

  • The unit, set, connected and started, with the combustion test recorded
  • Venting to the manufacturer's instructions
  • Condensate handling on a condensing furnace
  • The permit, and the old unit taken away
  • The flue sized and specified for the new appliance
  • Return duct capacity checked against the new unit

What is not

  • Gas line alteration beyond the appliance connection, quoted separately
  • Ductwork modification, unless the new cabinet does not fit the old opening and we have quoted it
  • Electrical beyond a like-for-like connection

What we can show you in writing

Every line traces to a document we read in full. The number after a sentence points to the research below.

  • The federal minimum for a new non-weatherized gas furnace is 80.0% AFUE, and it steps to 95.0% for units manufactured on and after December 18, 2028. That is a manufacturing date, not an installation deadline. Nothing requires anyone to act before it.1
  • A 95% furnace is a condensing furnace. It produces condensate that has to be drained, and it usually needs a different flue. That is why the flue size and material and the condensate path get checked before anything is ordered.1
  • The normal daily low in St. George is 27.9°F in December and 28.4°F in January, below freezing on an average winter night. That is why this is a two-system county and not a cooling-only one.1
