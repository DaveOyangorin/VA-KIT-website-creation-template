What the work is

A resin-saturated liner is drawn into the existing pipe and cured in place, leaving a continuous new pipe inside the old one. There are no joints for roots to find, and the only ground opened is the access point.

It suits a line that is structurally intact enough to host a liner: joints separating, roots coming in, corrosion, a long run under something you do not want to dig up. It does not suit a pipe that has collapsed, gone badly out of line, or lost its shape, and it slightly reduces the internal diameter.

Which of those you have is a camera question, not a sales question. We run the camera first and show you.

What is included

  • Camera survey and locating before anything is opened
  • Cleaning the host pipe so the liner bonds
  • The liner, installed and cured
  • Reinstating any branch connections
  • A camera run afterwards, and you keep both recordings

What is not

  • Lining a pipe that has collapsed or lost its shape. We will tell you and quote the alternative
  • Restoring landscape or hardscape beyond the access point, which is quoted once we know where it goes
  • Work on the city's side of the connection

Why the same job costs more here

Sewer work in this county carries costs that the same job would not carry on the Wasatch Front, and the guide below sets out what those are rather than leaving you to assume you are being overcharged. Read it before you compare quotes.

What we can show you in writing

Every line traces to a document we read in full. The number after a sentence points to the research below.

  • Lining does not fix a belly. A liner follows the grade of the pipe it is inside, so if the pipe sags, the new liner sags in the same place. Only digging it up, re-bedding and re-grading fixes a sag.1
  • Lining earns its place here on a narrower set of jobs than the national marketing implies: roots at the joints in 1980s and 1990s stock, cracked pipe that is still on grade, and lines running under mature landscaping or hardscape where digging costs more to put back than the line costs to fix.1

Read before you decide

  • Why a sewer replacement costs more here

    The national trenchless market runs on old pipe. St. George mostly has young pipe in bad ground. That changes which method applies, and it is the reason the number is higher.

  • The turf rebate, and the easement you sign for it

    WCWCD pays $2 per square foot to remove lawn, with no published end date, plus $1 more on the first 1,000 square feet for projects finished between June 9, 2026 and June 1, 2027. The payment buys a perpetual, recorded easement on that ground. Both halves, explained.

  • A pipe broke in the wall of your condo. Who pays?

    Utah Code § 57-8-7.2 draws the line at the finished surface. Everything behind it is common area, and a pipe gets sorted by how many units it serves.
