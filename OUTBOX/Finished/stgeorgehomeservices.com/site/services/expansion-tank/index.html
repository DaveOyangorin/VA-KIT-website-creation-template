Heating and air

Expansion tank install in St. George

A thermal expansion tank, sized and charged to your measured incoming pressure, not to the number on the box.

(435) 850-0053Ask for a visit

What the work is

When a check valve or a pressure reducing valve sits on the water service, the plumbing becomes a closed system. Heated water expands and has nowhere to go, so pressure spikes every time the heater fires. An expansion tank gives it somewhere to go.

The part that gets done wrong is the charge. The tank's air side has to be set to the actual static pressure on your service before the water is turned on. Set it to the factory number and the tank is a decoration.

So we measure your pressure, set the tank to it, and tell you the number in writing.

What is included

  • The tank, correctly sized for the heater
  • Tee, support and mounting so the tank is not hanging on the pipe
  • Air charge set to your measured static pressure
  • The static pressure reported to you in writing

What is not

  • A pressure reducing valve, if your incoming pressure turns out to be too high. That is a separate item and we will show you the reading
  • Replacing a failed relief valve

If your relief valve drips and then stops

That is the classic sign of thermal expansion with nowhere to go: the valve relieves the spike, then reseats. People replace the valve, and it starts again. The guide below explains the sequence and what actually fixes it.

What we can show you in writing

Every line traces to a document we read in full. The number after a sentence points to the research below.

  • Water expands about 2 percent by volume going from 60 to 140 degrees, and it does not compress. A check valve, a backflow preventer or a pressure-reducing valve at the service entrance closes the system, and pressure climbs to the T&P setpoint of 150 psi inside a single heating cycle.1
  • St. George requires backflow assemblies on outside watering systems and pressure-reducing valves are common here, so a lot of local houses are closed systems. If the T&P valve drips for a while and then stops, that is thermal expansion, not a failed valve.1
  • The precharge is set to the measured static pressure of the house before the tank goes into service. A factory 40-psi precharge left on an 80-psi system means the bladder is bottomed out and the tank does nothing from the first day.1
  • Press the Schrader valve on the tank. If water comes out, the bladder has failed. That tank gets replaced, not re-charged.1
