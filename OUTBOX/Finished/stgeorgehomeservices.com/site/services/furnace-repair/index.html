What the work is

Most winter calls here are an igniter, a flame sensor, a pressure switch reading a blocked flue or condensate line, or a limit switch tripping because airflow is restricted. The last one is worth saying plainly: a furnace that keeps shutting off is often not a broken furnace, it is a choked filter or a collapsed duct, and replacing the part does not fix it.

Anything touching combustion gets checked before we leave, whatever we came for.

The parts that fail are a short list and most of them are inexpensive: a hot surface igniter, a flame sensor that has gone dirty enough to stop proving flame, a pressure switch that is doing its job because a flue or a condensate line is blocked, or a limit switch tripping on restricted airflow.

The last two are the ones worth understanding, because the part is not the fault. A pressure switch that will not close is usually telling you something is obstructed. A limit switch that keeps tripping is telling you the furnace cannot move enough air through itself, and the cause is nearly always a filter, a closed-off return, or ductwork that has come apart. Replacing the switch in either case buys you a few weeks.

So the first thing we do on a no-heat call is not order a part. It is find out what the part was reacting to.

What is included

  • The failed part named, with the reading behind it
  • A combustion and venting check on any gas appliance we open
  • The airflow restriction found, if that is what tripped the limit
  • The cause behind the failed part, not just the part
  • A carbon monoxide check in the space around the appliance

What is not

  • Condemning a heat exchanger without showing you what we found
  • Work on the gas line beyond the appliance connection unless it is quoted separately
  • Replacing a switch that was doing its job, without finding what tripped it
