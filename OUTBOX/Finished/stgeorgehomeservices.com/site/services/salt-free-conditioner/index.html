Salt-free conditioner in St. George

A template-assisted crystallisation conditioner. We will install one and we will tell you honestly what it does and does not do first.

(435) 850-0053Ask for a visit

What the work is

A salt-free conditioner does not remove calcium and magnesium. It changes their form so they are less likely to plate onto surfaces. Total hardness at the tap is unchanged, and a hardness test after one will read the same as before.

That matters because manufacturers write their limits as total hardness. On our reading, a conditioner does not bring an 18-grain house under a 200 mg/L limit. That is our reading of the documents and not a manufacturer's ruling, and if you are relying on it for a warranty you should confirm it with the maker of your unit in writing.

What a conditioner does do is add no chloride to the sewer, use no salt, and need no drain. In some situations that is worth a lot. We would rather you chose one knowing all three things.

What is included

  • The unit, installed with a bypass
  • A written statement of what your hardness is before and after
  • Plain advice on whether it suits what you are trying to protect

What is not

  • Any claim that it softens water. It does not, and we will not write that down
  • Any claim that it satisfies a manufacturer's hardness limit
  • Drinking water treatment

When we would still recommend one

Where a drain or a salt supply is genuinely impractical, where the household wants no added sodium, or where the goal is scale on fixtures rather than a warranty condition on a tankless heater. Those are real cases and we install for them.

Where the goal is protecting a tankless heat exchanger and keeping the warranty intact, a softener is the answer and we will say so even though the conditioner is an easier sale.

What we can show you in writing

Every line traces to a document we read in full. The number after a sentence points to the research below.

  • A salt-free conditioner does not remove hardness. Calcium and magnesium stay in the water at full concentration; total hardness measured at your tap is unchanged.1
  • That means no reduction in soap scum, spotting, scale on glass and fixtures, or detergent demand. Those are concentration effects, and the concentration has not changed.1
  • The manufacturer limits above are written as total hardness. On a plain reading of those documents a conditioner does not bring an 18-grain house under them. That is our reading of the warranty text, not a ruling by the manufacturer — ask them in writing before you rely on it.1
  • The City of St. George's Water Distribution Division instructs residents to set water softeners to 18 grains per gallon, with a maximum of 22 grains.2
  • A.O. Smith's gas tankless warranty excludes damage from hard water over 7 grains per gallon in a single-family home. St. George runs 18 to 22.1
  • Noritz voids outright above 12 grains per gallon.1
5 more facts, with sources
  • Rinnai's manual states it flatly, and separately tells you a hard-water area needs treatment and more frequent flushing.1
  • Rheem excludes heat exchanger failures caused by scale, and Navien excludes damage from hard water deposits and mineral build-up.1
  • Rinnai and Rheem also void on hours, not just years: both stop at 12,000 operating hours, whichever comes first. A scaled unit runs longer per gallon delivered, so hard water spends that clock faster too.1
  • These are exclusions on the damage, not a blanket void of the whole warranty. A manufacturer denies a scale-related heat exchanger claim; it does not normally void an unrelated control board failure.1
  • Softening is what clears the limits. Softened output runs about 0 to 1 grain per gallon, under every threshold above. The 18-grain figure is what you tell the softener is coming in, not what comes out of it.1
