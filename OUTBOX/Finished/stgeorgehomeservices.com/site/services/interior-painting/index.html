Interior painting in St. George

Walls and ceilings, one room or the whole house, with the patching and caulking done before anything is rolled.

(435) 850-0053Ask for a visit

What the work is

Interior painting is mostly preparation and masking, and the finished room is decided long before the first coat. Nail pops and settlement cracks get cut out and patched, not painted over. Caulk that has split at the trim line is cut out and replaced, because new paint over a split caulk joint splits again in the same place within a season.

We wash where washing is needed. Kitchens carry a film of cooking oil that no primer is designed to stick to, and bathrooms carry soap and hairspray. Paint applied over either one fails by peeling in sheets, and it does it a year later, which is long enough that nobody connects the failure to the cause.

Then the order of work: ceilings, walls, trim, in that order, because each stage can be cut into the one before it. Everything that is not being painted is masked or moved, floors are covered, and the room is put back the same day it is finished.

We paint one room as willingly as a house. A single room is a small job and we price it as one, without the surcharge that gets attached to small work to discourage it.

What is included

  • Nail pops, cracks and dents patched, sanded and spot-primed
  • Split caulk lines cut out and re-caulked
  • Greasy or soaped surfaces washed before primer
  • Floors and fixtures covered, switch plates off and back on
  • Ceilings, then walls, then trim
  • The leftover paint labeled with the room it belongs to, and left with you

What is not

  • Painting over a wall that is wet, or over a stain whose cause is still active. We find out where the water is coming from first
  • Painting over wallpaper that is lifting. It has to come off, and that is a separate quote
  • Furniture we are not qualified to move: pianos, gun safes, anything on a wall mount

What is different here

Houses in Washington County are dry, and dry air is not neutral for painting. Water-based paint sets faster than the label assumes, which shortens the window you have to keep a wet edge and makes lap marks easier to leave on a long wall. It is a reason to work in smaller sections and to cut and roll together, rather than a reason to thin the paint.

The second thing is dust. Blowing dust settles on freshly coated surfaces through the windows you opened for ventilation, so on a windy day we close the house and ventilate differently. A gritty finish is not a paint defect, it is a scheduling decision somebody made badly.

What we can show you in writing

Every line traces to a document we read in full. The number after a sentence points to the research below.

  • Sherwin-Williams states it on its own product data sheet: most coating failures are due to inadequate surface preparation or application. It also warns that any surface preparation short of total removal of the old coating may compromise how long the system lasts.1
  • Federal law treats homes built before 1978 differently. Under the EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting rule, work that disturbs more than 6 square feet of painted surface in a room inside, or 20 square feet outside, must follow the rule's work practices — and sanding or scraping paint counts as disturbance. Window replacement counts at any size.1
  • The rule requires two separate credentials, and they are both checkable by anyone. The company doing the work must be an EPA-certified firm, and a certified renovator, an individual who has passed an accredited course, must be assigned to each job. EPA publishes a public search for certified firms, so you can look up anyone quoting you.1
  • The USDA Forest Products Laboratory puts it in four words in its own surface preparation guidance: do not sand lead-based paint.1
  • Of Washington County's 88,415 housing units, 7,613 were built before 1980 — about one in eleven, on the Census Bureau's American Community Survey. In most of the county the federal lead rule simply does not apply, which is worth knowing before anyone quotes you for containment you do not need.1
  • The City of St. George's Water Distribution Division instructs residents to set water softeners to 18 grains per gallon, with a maximum of 22 — about 308 to 376 mg/L, which the USGS scale classes as very hard.2
