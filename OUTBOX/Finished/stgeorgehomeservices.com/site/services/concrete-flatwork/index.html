Concrete flatwork in St. George

Concrete slabs, walks and pads, with the joints cut where they belong and the reinforcement in the middle of the slab.

(435) 850-0053Ask for a visit

What the work is

Concrete cracks. The job is to decide where. Control joints cut at the right spacing and depth, at the right time, put the cracks where you planned them instead of across the middle.

The other half is what is in it and under it: compacted subgrade, reinforcement held at the correct height rather than lying on the ground, and a mix suited to the conditions on the day.

What is included

  • Subgrade preparation and compaction
  • Forms, reinforcement held at the correct height
  • Placing, finishing and curing
  • Control joints cut at the right spacing and depth
  • Fall set away from buildings

What is not

  • Sealing
  • Removing existing concrete, unless quoted in
  • Structural slabs or footings, which need an engineer
  • Repairing cracks that appear in adjacent existing work

Heat changes the schedule

Placing and finishing concrete in the summer here is a timing job. Work starts early for a reason, and a crew that turns up at eleven in July is telling you something about the finish you are going to get.

The mix is a soil question, and the soil is mapped

Gypsum-bearing ground attacks concrete. In gypsiferous zones mapped by the Utah Geological Survey, a soil sulfate test is what sets the required mix under ACI 318 exposure classes, and sulfate-resistant cement is required at S2 and above.

We are not going to tell you your site needs a particular cement type before anyone has tested it, because that depends on the test. What we will tell you is that the county publishes a Gypsiferous Rock and Soil layer and an Expansive Rock and Soil layer for free, and you can look up your own parcel before anybody pours anything.

If a rebate is part of the plan, this is the wrong material

Poured concrete is subtracted from the water district's incentive calculation. Ungrouted pavers and dry-set flagstone are counted as mulch and stay inside it.

That is a point against the thing on this page, and it is worth knowing before you spend money rather than after. If the area is going into a rebated conversion, ask us about pavers instead and we will tell you plainly which one your project wants.

What we can show you in writing

Every line traces to a document we read in full. The number after a sentence points to the research below.

  • Ungrouted pavers and dry-set flagstone count as mulch inside the rebated area, poured concrete and mortared work are subtracted, and permeable artificial turf is accepted in lieu of mulch under the WCWCD program.1
  • In gypsiferous zones mapped by UGS Special Study 127, a soil sulfate test determines the required concrete mix under ACI 318 exposure classes S1 to S3. Sulfate-resistant cement is required at S2 and above.2
  • Sulfate-bearing ground is mapped in five categories in the county's GIS, from high to low, and gypsum is soluble, so gypsiferous ground can dissolve and leave voids under what sits on it.2
  • Expansive soils account for 42.87% of Utah's classified problem soils, and the state's hazard plan attributes concrete pavement failure and severe building distress to them.2
  • Washington County publishes a free Gypsiferous Rock and Soil layer along with Expansive Rock and Soil, so the question of what is under a slab is one you can check before anybody pours it.2
