Kitchen faucet replacement in St. George

A kitchen faucet replaced, with new supply lines and the stops under the sink changed while we are down there.

(435) 850-0053Ask for a visit

What the work is

The faucet is the easy part. The reason this job takes longer than people expect is everything under the sink: corroded stops that will not close, supply lines that have been there since the house was built, and a basin wrench in a cabinet that was not designed to be worked in.

We change the stops and the supplies at the same time. Doing it later means doing the awkward part twice.

What is included

  • Removing the old faucet
  • Setting the new one with the correct escutcheon or deck plate
  • New quarter-turn stops and braided supply lines
  • Reconnecting the sprayer, soap dispenser or filtered tap where fitted
  • Testing for leaks under pressure before we leave

What is not

  • Cutting new holes in a stone or composite countertop
  • Repairing a sink or a drain assembly that fails when disturbed. We will show you first
  • Reverse osmosis or filtered water plumbing, which is its own item
  • The faucet, unless you ask us to supply it

Cartridges and hard water

A mixing cartridge on this water furs up with mineral scale, which is why a faucet gets stiff and then drips. If the faucet is otherwise good, a cartridge is sometimes the whole repair and we will tell you that instead of selling you a faucet.

If you are replacing faucets across the house because they keep failing, the water is the thing to fix.

What we can show you in writing

Every line traces to a document we read in full. The number after a sentence points to the research below.

  • In the Battelle testing, faucet strainers were nearly completely plugged after 19 equivalent days on unsoftened water. That is what hard water does to the small openings in a faucet.1
  • Cartridges that fail early are often a pressure problem, not a faucet problem. A waterlogged expansion tank lets pressure spike, and cartridges and fill valves go first.2
  • A dripping faucet or a faucet swap is scheduled work. Nobody needs to pay after-hours rates for it.2
