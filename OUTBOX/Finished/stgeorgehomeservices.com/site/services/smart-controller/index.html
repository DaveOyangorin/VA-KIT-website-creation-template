Heating and air

Smart controller install in St. George

A weather-based controller installed and actually programmed, with the schedule written down so you can check it.

(435) 850-0053Ask for a visit

What the work is

A smart controller adjusts run times to the weather instead of to a calendar. Installed and left on its defaults, it does very little. Programmed properly for your zones, your plants and your soil, it does a lot.

So the install includes the programming, walking the zones with you, and leaving you the schedule on paper as well as in an app.

We also set it to comply with your town's watering rules, which differ from one to the next.

What is included

  • The controller, installed and connected
  • Every zone identified, labeled and programmed
  • A schedule that complies with your town's watering rules
  • Walking the zones with you
  • The schedule written down and left with you

What is not

  • Electrical beyond an existing receptacle, which is our licensed electrical partner's work
  • Repairing valves or wiring found faulty when we test each zone. Shown and quoted
  • The subscription on any product that has one

Programmed to your town, not to a default

Every town here sets its own landscape rules and they are not the same. St. George, Washington City and Ivins each cap residential lawn differently, and Washington City's ceiling applies to every lot regardless of its size. Ivins bans lawn watering year-round and limits slope. Your town page has the figures with the section numbers next to them.

A controller set to a default schedule can put you outside your own city's ordinance. We set it to your town's rules and write down which rule we set it to.

What we can show you in writing

Every line traces to a document we read in full. The number after a sentence points to the research below.

  • A smart controller is not an upgrade here, it is the standard. WCWCD's Water Efficiency Standards require a WaterSense-labeled smart controller, or a comparable weather-based controller where no WaterSense model fits, on any automated irrigation system covered by the standards.1
  • WCWCD pays a rebate of up to $100 on a WaterSense-labeled controller. The rebate is calculated on what you actually spent, with shipping and tax excluded, and every Utah residential property is eligible.1
  • The EPA's figure for a WaterSense-labeled controller replacing a clock timer is up to 15,000 gallons of water a year for one household.1
  • The EPA attaches a condition to its own savings figure: the controller has to be installed, programmed and maintained properly. An unprogrammed smart controller saves nothing, so we set the program and the zones before we leave.1
  • September is the month a controller earns its price. St. George's outdoor watering-hours restriction runs June 1 to September 1, and when it lapses the District's published schedule is stepping watering days down, not up. A weather-based controller makes that adjustment on its own.1
