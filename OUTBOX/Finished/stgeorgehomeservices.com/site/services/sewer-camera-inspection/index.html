Sewer camera inspection in St. George

A camera run of the main line, with a recording you keep. You should not have to take anyone's word for what is under your yard.

(435) 850-0053Ask for a visit

What the work is

A self-leveling camera goes down the line from the cleanout or a roof vent and records the whole run. You get the video. Not a still frame chosen by whoever wants to sell you a replacement, and not a description over the phone.

With a locator we can also mark on the ground exactly where a defect sits and how deep, which is what turns a trench into a single pit.

This is the piece of work we would most like every homeowner here to buy before anything else, including from someone other than us. Everything expensive downstream of a sewer problem gets decided on the strength of what is on that recording.

What is included

  • A full camera run of the main line from the cleanout or vent
  • The recording, handed over in a form you can keep and send to anyone
  • Locating and marking any defect on the surface, with the depth
  • A plain description of what is on it, in writing

What is not

  • Any repair. This is a look, and the look is priced on its own
  • Running a camera through a line so blocked the camera cannot pass. That gets cleared first, and you approve that first
  • A guess at remaining life. Nobody can give you a number for that honestly

Buy this before you buy a sewer replacement from anybody

A sewer replacement is one of the most expensive things that can happen to a house here, and the guide below explains why the same job costs more in this county than it does in Salt Lake. That is exactly the situation in which you want the evidence in your own hands before you decide.

If someone quotes you a full replacement without showing you footage, ask for the footage. If they will not give you a copy, that tells you something.

What we can show you in writing

Every line traces to a document we read in full. The number after a sentence points to the research below.

  • The camera goes in before the jetter on any line of unknown material or condition, and the jetting is done with what the camera found. Plenty of operators do it the other way around.1
  • A belly reads as standing water that does not drain as the camera advances — the picture goes under and comes back out. The footage counter says where it is and the sonde says how deep. No cleaning method fixes it.1
  • Roots read as fine hair-like masses coming in at a joint or a crack, usually at the top or the sides where the seal failed. The camera has to push through them like a curtain.1
