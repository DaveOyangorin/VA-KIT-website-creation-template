Trim, fascia and garage doors in St. George

Fascia, eaves, window trim, shutters and garage doors. The parts the sun destroys first, repainted without doing the whole house.

What the work is

Trim fails before the field of the wall does, every time. It is usually a different material, it is often a different color, and the horizontal surfaces take sunlight for more of the day than any wall does. A house whose body is still sound can have fascia that is chalking and garage doors that have faded two shades.

That makes trim-only repainting one of the better-value jobs available: it is the part that reads as tired from the street, and it is a fraction of a full exterior. We will tell you honestly when a house needs the whole envelope instead.

Garage doors deserve their own note, because they are usually steel, usually face the street, and are frequently the largest single colored surface on the front of the house. They also get hot, which means they get coated early in the day and not at four in the afternoon.

Where we find rot in fascia or a sill, you hear about it before it gets painted. Coating rotten wood is the most common way a small repair becomes a large one two years later.

What is included

  • Loose and chalking coating removed, bare wood and metal primed
  • Failed caulk at trim joints and window perimeters replaced
  • Rot found and shown to you before anything covers it
  • Garage doors and other hot surfaces coated at the right time of day

What is not

  • Replacing rotten fascia, trim or sills, which is carpentry and is quoted separately
  • Roof, gutter and flashing work

Why the trim goes first here

The normal daily high in St. George is 101.9°F in July and 99.9°F in August, on NOAA's 1991 to 2020 normals for the city's own weather station. Fascia, eaves and garage doors take that sun for more hours a day than any wall does, and horizontal surfaces take it longest of all.

The federal wood laboratory measured what that asymmetry does: paint on the north side of a building lasts twice as long as paint on the south. Which is a practical point, not a curiosity. The sides of your house are not on the same schedule, and quoting them as though they are means overcharging for two of them.

It also decides the hour we work. A steel garage door facing west is coated in the morning, because by afternoon its surface is well past the temperature its manufacturer publishes.

What we can show you in writing

Every line traces to a document we read in full. The number after a sentence points to the research below.

  • The USDA Forest Products Laboratory reports that paint on the north side of a building lasts twice as long as paint on the south side, and concludes that different sides of a structure do not need to be painted on the same schedule.1
  • Chalking is the coating failing, not dirt. Ultraviolet light degrades the resin that binds the pigment, and the loose pigment is the powder that comes off on your hand. You can test for it yourself before anyone quotes: rub a black cloth across the wall and see whether the film transfers.1
  • Dunn-Edwards publishes the rule plainly for hot climates: follow the shade, do not paint in the sun, and paint south-facing surfaces in the morning before the full heat of the day. It defines hot weather as above 90°F up to 120°F, and names the failures heat causes — mud cracking, dry spray, and lap marks where the paint loses its open time.1
  • The USDA Forest Products Laboratory reports that if the moisture content of wood exceeds 20% when it is painted, the risk of blistering and peeling increases.1
  • All four manufacturers agree on the cold end: 35°F minimum air and surface temperature, the surface at least 5°F above the dew point, and standard latex primers no lower than 50°F.1
  • The normal daily high in St. George is 101.9°F in July and 99.9°F in August on NOAA's 1991 to 2020 normals for the city's own station — which is at or past the surface temperature ceiling Benjamin Moore and PPG publish for applying their exterior coatings.1
