What the work is

Sometimes the trench is the correct job: the line has to move, the grade has to change, the run is short, or the conditions rule out bursting and lining. In that case the honest thing is to dig it, not to sell a trenchless method that will not work.

An open cut costs you the surface above it. That is the real price, and it is why we survey and price the alternatives first even though this job is often simpler for us.

Where we do open cut, we protect the surfaces we are not digging, we compact the backfill properly, and we tell you before we start exactly what will not go back the way it was.

What is included

  • Camera survey and locating before anything is opened
  • Excavation, new line at correct grade, and connection at both ends
  • Bedding, backfill and compaction
  • Surface protection over everything we are not opening
  • The permit and the inspection
  • A camera run afterwards, and you keep it

What is not

  • Replacing landscape, turf, concrete, pavers or irrigation that the trench goes through. Quoted separately once the route is marked
  • Work on the city's side of the connection
  • Restoring a converted yard to the water district's rebate conditions, unless it is quoted in

What the trench actually costs you

A trench across a front yard here often crosses drip irrigation, decomposed granite, boulders set as part of a conversion, or a paver walk. Those are real costs and they belong in the quote, not in a conversation afterwards.

If your yard was converted under the district's rebate, the agreement has conditions on the converted area and, in some cases, a right of entry. Tell us before we mark the route.

What we can show you in writing

Every line traces to a document we read in full. The number after a sentence points to the research below.

  • A sag is a grade problem, not a pipe problem. Lining follows the existing grade and bursting follows the existing bore, so neither one corrects it. Only excavation with re-bedding and re-grading does.1
  • The Utah Geological Survey defines collapsible soil as soil with real strength while dry that settles when it gets wet. A leaking line is the wetting agent, the ground drops, and the line loses its support.2
  • Caliche is mapped across this area as an adverse construction condition, and the Utah Geological Survey says plainly that it can be very difficult to excavate. That is why trenching prices here do not match national ones.2

Read before you decide

  • Why a sewer replacement costs more here

    The national trenchless market runs on old pipe. St. George mostly has young pipe in bad ground. That changes which method applies, and it is the reason the number is higher.

  • The turf rebate, and the easement you sign for it

    WCWCD pays $2 per square foot to remove lawn, with no published end date, plus $1 more on the first 1,000 square feet for projects finished between June 9, 2026 and June 1, 2027. The payment buys a perpetual, recorded easement on that ground. Both halves, explained.

  • A pipe broke in the wall of your condo. Who pays?

    Utah Code § 57-8-7.2 draws the line at the finished surface. Everything behind it is common area, and a pipe gets sorted by how many units it serves.
