Hydro jetting in St. George

High-pressure water scours the main line back to bare pipe. It is the right tool for grease, scale and root mass, and the wrong tool for a pipe that is already failing.

(435) 850-0053Ask for a visit

What the work is

A cable cuts a hole through a blockage. A jetter washes the pipe wall. For a kitchen line packed with grease, or a main with years of soft scale on it, that difference is the difference between clearing it and fixing it.

It is not always the right call. Jetting an old, thin, or cracked line can make a small problem into an open one. We run a camera first on anything we are not sure of, and if the pipe cannot take the pressure we will tell you so and quote the repair instead. Saying no to the jetting is sometimes the honest sale.

What is included

  • Jetting the main line from the cleanout with the nozzle chosen for what is in it
  • A camera run afterwards on request so you can see the pipe wall, not just a working drain
  • Root cutting where roots are the mass

What is not

  • Jetting a line we believe will not survive it. We will say so and quote the alternative
  • Repairing a line that fails during the work because it had already failed structurally
  • Anything on the city's side of the connection

Why we camera first more often than most

A large share of the older housing stock in the county is on cast iron or clay, and both fail in ways that a jetter finds the hard way. A five-minute camera run before the jetter comes off the truck is cheaper than an emergency excavation, so we do it.

What we can show you in writing

Every line traces to a document we read in full. The number after a sentence points to the research below.

  • A jetting nozzle drives itself up the line on reverse thrust and scours the full circumference of the pipe on the way back to the access point. A cable only cuts a channel through the middle.1
  • Pressure cuts. Flow carries the debris out. A machine with high pressure and low flow will open a grease plug and leave the spoil in the pipe, so the flow rate has to match the diameter of the line.1
  • The camera goes first on any line whose material or condition we do not know, and the jetting is done with the camera's findings in hand.1
