Slab leak repair in St. George

A leak in a line under the slab, either rerouted overhead or opened and repaired. We will tell you which is cheaper for your house, and it is usually not the one that involves a saw.

(435) 850-0053Ask for a visit

What the work is

There are two ways to deal with a failed line under a slab. Reroute it: abandon the buried section and run a new line overhead or through the walls. Or open the slab, repair the pipe and close it back up.

In most single-story houses here a reroute is faster, cheaper and less disruptive, and it removes the section that failed rather than repairing one point on a pipe that is the same age all the way along. Where a reroute is impractical, we open the floor.

You will be shown both options and both prices before anything starts.

What is included

  • Locating the leak precisely before any decision is made
  • The reroute or the repair, whichever you approve
  • Pressure testing before anything is closed up
  • Photographs of the failed section
  • Concrete replacement where we opened it, to a flat and level finish

What is not

  • Finished flooring replacement. We restore the concrete, not your tile or your carpet
  • Repairing a second failure elsewhere on the same aged line. We will tell you the risk honestly
  • Drywall finishing and paint where a reroute goes through a wall, unless you ask us to include it
  • Mold remediation

Post-tensioned slabs

If the slab is post-tensioned, nobody cuts it until the cables are located. A post-tensioned slab has steel tendons under load running through it, and cutting one is a serious and expensive mistake. We scan before any saw touches concrete, and where we cannot get a clean read we reroute overhead instead of guessing.

Why we usually recommend the reroute

A slab leak is rarely the pipe's only weak point. If the line failed at one place after twenty years under a slab, the rest of it is twenty years old too. Repairing one point on the floor and closing it up is a fix with a known expiry date.
