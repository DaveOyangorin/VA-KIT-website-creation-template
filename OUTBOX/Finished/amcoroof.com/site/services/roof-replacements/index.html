Signs It’s Time For a New Roof

Roof repairs can only go so far. At some point, the cost of patching a failing roof outweighs the investment in replacing it. Here are the most common signs homeowners in Utah reach that threshold:

  • Aging Roof: Most asphalt shingle roofs have a functional lifespan of 20–30 years, depending on roof installation quality, materials, and local climate conditions. Utah’s temperature swings accelerate wear.
  • Shingle Curling: Curling, buckling, or losing granules are signs of material breakdown, not isolated damage. When it’s widespread, repair won’t solve the underlying problem.
  • Recurring Leaks: One leak can be repaired. Leaks that keep coming back, especially in multiple areas, point to systemic failure.
  • Visible Damage: If light is coming through the roofline, or you see water stains, streaks, or moss/algae growth, it signals the roof is actively degrading.
  • Storm Damage: Hail and wind damage can compromise your roof’s structural integrity even when it doesn’t look severe from the ground. If you’ve had a major storm, a professional inspection matters.
  • High Energy Bills: A deteriorating roof loses its insulating effectiveness. If your heating and cooling costs have increased without a clear explanation, your roof may be part of the problem.

What New Roof Installation Includes

A new roof isn’t just new shingles on top of old ones. A proper installation is a full system replacement. Here’s what that looks like:

1. Tear Off of Existing Materials

Everything comes off down to the deck, no burying old problems under new roofing.

2. Decking Inspection and Repair

Once the old roof is off, the structural decking gets inspected. Rotted or damaged sections are repaired before anything goes on top.

3. Underlayment and Flashing Installation

A moisture barrier goes down first, followed by flashing around penetrations, valleys, and edges—an area where cheaper installs often cut corners.

4. New Roofing System Installation

Material goes on according to manufacturer specifications, which is required to keep the manufacturer’s warranty valid.

5. Cleanup and Final Walkthrough

The site is cleared and reviewed before the job is called done.

New Roof Material Options

The right material depends on your home, your budget, and how long you want your next roof to last. Here’s a high-level look at what American Roofing installs:

  • Asphalt Shingles: The most common choice for Utah homes. Cost-effective, widely available, and available in a range of styles and performance tiers.
  • Stone-Coated Steel: Durable, lightweight, and built to handle Utah’s freeze-thaw cycles and hail. A strong option for homeowners who want longevity without the look of traditional metal.
  • Standing Seam Metal: A premium option with a long lifespan and excellent performance in extreme weather. Common on modern and mountain-style homes.
  • Flat and Low-Slope Systems: For commercial properties or residential additions with low-pitch rooflines, we install appropriate membranes and modified systems.

Want help choosing the right material for your home? A free inspection and quote is the right starting point.

How Long Does a Roof Replacement Take?

Several factors affect how much time it takes to replace roofs:

  • The size, slope, and complexity of the roof
  • How accessible it is
  • The chosen roof materials
  • The weather during the project schedule
  • Unique situations like solar panel removal

Depending on these elements, new roof installations can take one day to several days or a few weeks at most.

What Affects the Cost of a New Roof

Roof replacement costs vary, and any contractor who quotes you a number without seeing your roof first is guessing. The main factors that affect your final cost include:

  • Roof Size: Measured in squares (100 sq ft). This is the biggest driver of cost.
  • Material Choice: Asphalt shingles are the most affordable; metal systems carry a higher upfront cost with a longer lifespan.
  • Tear-Off Needs: Removing existing layers adds labor time and disposal costs.
  • Roof Complexity: More angles, valleys, and penetrations mean more labor and more material.
  • Decking Repairs: If we find damage beneath your old roof, repairs are necessary before installation.
  • Accessories and Penetrations: Skylights, chimneys, vents, and solar panels all add scope.

What Are the Benefits of Residential Roof Replacement?

A residential roof replacement is one of the higher-return home investments available, improving energy efficiency, appearance, and resale value all at once.

  • Energy Efficiency: Getting a residential roof replacement can make your home more energy efficient. With better insulation and ventilation, your roof replacement will help keep your home comfortable while reducing energy use.

  • Property Value: If you’re thinking of selling your home at some point, investing in a roof upgrade can pay off. A new roof will not only make your home more attractive to potential buyers but also increase your property value.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions

When should I replace my roof?

How often you should replace your roof depends on the climate where you live, how well the roof was installed, and the type of materials used on the roof. However, most roofs need to be replaced every 20-25 years.

What time of year is best to replace a roof?

The busy season for roofing is usually between late summer and early fall, but you can replace your roof during the other months as well. However, most experts recommend avoiding roof replacement when weather conditions are more extreme. This will vary depending on the climate. You’ll also want to avoid replacing your roof when temperatures drop below 40℉ as shingles rely on thermal sealing to adhere to the roof. The process goes much quicker in warmer weather, usually only a matter of hours, but can take days or weeks in colder weather.

Do gutters have to be removed when replacing a roof?

Typically the answer is no, but many homeowners choose to have their gutters replaced as part of their roof replacement. However, if you’ve recently installed new gutters or they’re still in good condition, they won’t need to be removed during your roof replacement. Your contractors should take every precaution to ensure your gutters aren’t damaged during the replacement process. Most will even blow out gutters and clean them after the process is done.

Will my insurance go up if I get a new roof?

The answer is no under most circumstances, but it depends greatly on the insurance. Some carriers may lower your premium as a new roof makes your home safer. Others may increase your costs, especially if you need to file for a new rate. Additionally, if you’re replacing your roof due to weather damage, you can usually expect your rate to remain the same, especially if it’s your first claim.

How do you evaluate a roofing contractor?

When choosing a roofing contractor, be sure to check their insurance and license information. The contractor should also have a detailed project plan and payment terms. All of these help determine whether you’re hiring trusted, certified professionals who can do the job properly and have their own financial protection in case of work-related accidents.

What's the difference between roof tear off vs. roof overlay?

Tear-offs and overlays are two types of roof replacement methods. In a roof tear-off, the contractor removes existing roof materials and replaces them with new ones. Meanwhile, an overlay keeps the current roof as is and simply lays the new materials on top.

  • When tearing off and replacing a roof, you’ll be able to identify and solve any underlying issues that exist under the current roofing layer. However, it is more expensive due to the increased labor.
  • Roof overlays are much cheaper and faster since the contractor doesn’t have to remove the existing roof materials. But the old roof might not be able to handle the extra weight of the new one on top and could become compromised.
