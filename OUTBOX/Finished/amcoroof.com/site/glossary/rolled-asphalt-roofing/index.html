Rolled asphalt roofing is popular due to its simplicity and cost-effectiveness. This type of roofing is widely used in residential, commercial, and temporary buildings. It’s an attractive option for homeowners and property managers looking for an affordable and quick solution to a roof leak repair or total roofing replacement. In this roofing terms glossary page, we’ll provide you with more information about rolled asphalt roofing to help you make an informed decision about your property.

What Is Rolled Asphalt Roofing?

Rolled asphalt roofing is a material composed of the same elements as asphalt shingles, but it comes in large rolls. It is typically used for low-slope roofs and temporary structures and is known for its ease of installation and affordability. This roofing option is also ideal for some urgent situations, including roof leak repair.

Materials Making Up Rolled Asphalt Roofing

Rolled asphalt roofing consists of a base material, usually felt or fiberglass, coated with asphalt and embedded with mineral granules. This combination provides durability and weather resistance.

How Rolled Asphalt Roofing Is Installed

Rolled asphalt roofing is installed using these basic steps:

  1. Preparation: Clean the roof surface thoroughly, including removal of debris and damaged roofing material.
  2. Underlayment: Apply a tar paper or underlayment layer to protect against leaks.
  3. Rolling out: Unroll the roofing material and position it correctly.
  4. Securing: Nail down the roofing at intervals, ensuring proper overlap to prevent leaks.
  5. Sealing: Use roofing cement to seal edges and seams, reducing the chances of needing a roof leak repair.

Types of Rolled Asphalt Roofing

Rolled asphalt roofing comes in a few different types that include different advantages:

  • Smooth finish: Ideal for temporary structures or additional waterproofing
  • Mineral surface: More durable for permanent installations on low-slope roofs
  • Saturated felt: Often used as an underlayment or for minor roof leak repair

Durability in Various Weather Conditions

In Utah, where rain, snow, and hail are common, rolled asphalt roofing offers limited to moderate durability and protection. While it handles mild weather well, extreme conditions can lead to damage, necessitating a roof leak repair. Therefore, rolled asphalt roofing is generally only used as a temporary measure or for temporary structures. Regular maintenance and inspections are crucial to ensure its performance and longevity.

Lifespan of Rolled Asphalt Roofing

The lifespan of rolled asphalt roofing can vary depending on weather conditions, proper installation, and whether or not regular inspections and maintenance are performed. Generally, rolled asphalt roofing has a shorter lifespan than other common roofing materials, such as asphalt shingles.

Pros and Cons of Rolled Asphalt Roofing

Advantages

  • Affordable: One of the most cost-effective roofing options
  • Easy to install: Suitable for professional installations and DIY projects
  • Quick fix: Ideal for immediate roof leak repair needs
  • Lightweight: Less strain on building structures

Drawbacks

  • Shorter lifespan: Does not last as long as other roofing materials
  • Weather sensitivity: More susceptible to damage in extreme weather conditions
  • Maintenance needs: Requires regular inspections to prevent the need for roof leak repairs
  • Aesthetic Limitations: Less visually appealing compared to other options

Installation Over Existing Roofing

Rolled roofing can be installed over existing shingles if the roof is in good condition and has no more than two layers of rolling. This method can save time and labor costs but may not be the best long-term solution.

Types of Roofs Best Suited for Rolled Asphalt Roofing

Rolled asphalt roofing is best suited for low-slope roofs such as those on garages, sheds, and smaller homes. It’s not recommended for steep-sloped roofs due to its potential for infiltration, which can lead to a future roof leak repair.

Schedule a Professional Repair or Installation

Rolled asphalt roofing is a cost-effective and straightforward solution for low-slope roofs and temporary structures. While it may not offer the durability or aesthetic appeal of other materials, its affordability makes it a popular choice.
