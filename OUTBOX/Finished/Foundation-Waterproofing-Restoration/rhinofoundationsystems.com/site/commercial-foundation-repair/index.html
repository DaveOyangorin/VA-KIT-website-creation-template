Industrial Methods We Use for Commercial Foundation Repair

Our extensive experience in foundation repair allows us to tackle even the most challenging foundation problems for commercial properties. We use a range of cutting-edge methods, including:

Piers and Piles

Piers and piles are essential in stabilizing deep foundations that have been compromised by shifting soil or settling. These support systems transfer the weight of the building to more stable soil layers, ensuring long-term stability.

Drilled-in Displacement Micropiles

Micropiles are ideal for stabilizing structures in difficult-to-reach areas or in locations with unstable or shrinking soils. These high-strength, small-diameter piles are drilled deep into the ground to anchor the structure and provide reliable support.

Helical Piers

These spiral-shaped piers are installed by rotating them into the ground, creating a strong, stable foundation. Helical piers are highly effective for repairing foundations that have settled or are at risk of settling due to poor soil conditions.

Push Piers

Push piers are hydraulically driven deep into the soil to lift and stabilize foundations. This solution is highly effective for commercial buildings with substantial weight, offering reliable support and long-term foundation repair.

Soil Nails & Tiebacks

Soil nails and tiebacks are used for deep foundation stabilization, particularly in excavation projects or areas where soil movement has created instability. These techniques reinforce the foundation by securing the surrounding soil and preventing further shifting.

Micropiles: The Powerhouse of Foundation Stabilization

What Are Micropiles?

Micropiles are one of the most powerful tools in foundation stabilization. These steel rods, driven deep into the ground, provide solid support for large commercial buildings. At Rhino Foundation Systems, we use micropiles with load capacities exceeding 1,000 tons, making them ideal for both small and large-scale foundation repair projects.

Versatility

Micropiles can be installed in tight, confined spaces where traditional methods may not be feasible. They’re also adaptable to a variety of soil conditions, making them suitable for nearly any commercial foundation problem.

Stability

Micropiles offer exceptional strength and stability in shifting or weak soil. They act as a reliable anchor for your foundation, preventing future issues from arising.

Low Environmental Impact

The installation of micropiles is a minimally invasive process that generates very little noise and disturbance, reducing the environmental impact of the repair work.

Customizable Solutions

Micropiles are highly customizable. We adjust the installation method, angle, and depth to meet the specific needs of your project, ensuring optimal performance for your commercial property.

How Micropiles Work

Micropiles are installed by drilling small-diameter holes into the ground and then driving steel rods into place. The micropiles transfer the weight of the structure to stable layers of soil below, distributing the load evenly to avoid future settlement or shifting. After installation, the micropiles are grouted into place to create a solid bond, ensuring long-lasting support.

Polyurethane Foam: A Lightweight and Efficient Solution

Polyurethane foam is an effective method for filling voids, stabilizing soil, and providing quick fixes for foundation issues. This lightweight, environmentally friendly solution is ideal for commercial properties in need of immediate stabilization.

Quick Installation

The polyurethane foam injection process is fast, allowing for minimal disruption to your business operations. The foam expands quickly, filling voids and stabilizing foundations in a fraction of the time compared to traditional methods.

Long-Lasting Stability

Once injected, the foam cures into a durable, rigid material that prevents future shifting and settlement, providing long-term support for your commercial property.

Environmentally Friendly

Polyurethane foam is a non-toxic, environmentally safe option that doesn’t contribute to pollution or soil contamination.
