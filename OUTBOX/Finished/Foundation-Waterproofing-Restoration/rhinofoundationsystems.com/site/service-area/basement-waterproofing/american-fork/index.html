American Fork, several environmental and structural factors increase the likelihood of basement water problems:

Clay-Heavy Soils

Much of Utah Valley, including American Fork, sits on expansive clay soil. This soil swells when wet and shrinks when dry. That constant expansion and contraction places stress on your foundation, eventually leading to cracks and water entry points.

Freeze-Thaw Cycles

During the winter, water in the soil freezes and expands, then thaws again as temperatures rise. This freeze-thaw cycle creates pressure against foundation walls—also known as frost heave—which can crack walls and open paths for moisture.

Spring Snowmelt and Heavy Rainfall

American Fork’s climate brings snow in winter and rain in spring, often within weeks of each other. This leads to a sudden increase in groundwater and hydrostatic pressure—the force exerted by water against your basement walls and floor.

Poor Drainage or Grading

If your home’s exterior grading slopes toward your house, or if gutters and downspouts aren’t functioning properly, water collects near the foundation. Over time, that leads to saturation, leaks, and long-term water damage.
