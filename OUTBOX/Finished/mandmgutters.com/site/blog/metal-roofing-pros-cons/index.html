Pros and Cons of Metal Roofing for Your Home

Metal roofs have become popular for homes because of their unique advantages, such as sustainability, durability, and lifespan. However, when it comes to choosing a roofing material for your home, it’s also important to consider the disadvantages, such as dents and temperature fluctuations.

Below, we’ll discuss the pros and cons of metal roofs to help you decide if it’s the best roofing material for your home.

What Is Metal Roofing?

Metal roofing is a durable and environmentally friendly roofing material for homes. It’s made from various metals, each offering similar benefits and drawbacks.

Benefits of a Metal Roof

Below is a list of the advantages of metal roofs to consider when evaluating roofing options for your home.

Durability

One of the key advantages of metal roofing is its extraordinary durability. Metal roofs can withstand intense weather, like rainstorms, snow, and hail.

Eco-Friendly

Metal roofs are often made from recycled materials, making them an environmentally friendly choice. They can also be recycled at the end of their life. Choosing a metal roof contributes to sustainable building practices.

Life Span

Metal roofs typically last longer than other roofing materials. When you invest in a metal roof, you won’t have to replace it as often or worry about maintenance costs.

Fire-Resistant

Metal roofs are fire-resistant, reducing the risk of damage during a fire. If you install a metal roof, you’ll have another layer of protection for your home.

Aesthetics

Metal roofs are available in several styles and finishes. Their versatility allows homeowners to choose from many different colors and designs to fit their aesthetic preferences.

Energy Efficiency

Metal roofs reflect the sun’s rays, preventing heat absorption and keeping the inside of your home cooler. This energy-efficient feature may reduce utility bills over time and provide a cooler, more comfortable living space.

What Are the Disadvantages of a Metal Roof?

With such a long list of pros, are metal roofs better? That depends on your personal preferences. Here are some disadvantages of a metal roof to consider when determining whether metal roofing is right for you.

Noise

A big complaint of metal roofing is the noise that can occur during rain and hailstorms. However, you can prevent noise with proper insulation.

Up-Front Costs

Metal roofs may provide long-term cost savings, but the initial cost of installation can be more expensive than other roofing materials. Homeowners should consider the upfront cost versus the long-term benefits.

Dents

Metal roofs may dent easily in areas prone to hail. Choosing a thicker metal can enhance the roof’s resistance to dents.

Limited to HOA

Some HOAs may restrict the types of roofing materials allowed in the neighborhood. Check with your HOA before installing a metal roof.

Difficult Installation

An experienced professional should install metal roofing to ensure the installation is done correctly to prevent leaks. However, hiring a professional may cost more than installing the roof yourself.

Contraction and Expansion

Metal roofs can contract and expand with temperature fluctuations, leading to potential issues such as leaking, sealant deterioration, and structural damage. Properly installing the expansion joints helps prevent these problems.

Color Fading

Over time, exposure to sunlight, weather, and debris may cause the color to fade on metal roofs. Applying high-quality finishes can reduce fading.

Corrosion

Metal roofs can corrode, especially in areas with high pollution levels.

Color Matching

Finding a metal roof to match the siding and the trim of your house can be challenging, but it’s not impossible.

What Does a Metal Roof Sound Like When it Rains?

A metal roof sounds different depending on how well your roof is insulated. If your roof is properly insulated, you’ll hear a soft, steady patter. If the sound is more like loud drumming, then you may need more insulation.

How Much Does a Metal Roof Cost?

If you want to install a metal roof on your home, you can expect to spend around $11,000. The price may vary depending on what metal you choose, installation costs, and square footage.

How Much Does It Cost to Put a Metal Roof on a 2,000-Square-Foot House?

A metal roof on a 2,000-square-foot house can cost $5-$ 30 per square foot, depending on the type of metal, the complexity of your roof, and the labor rates in your area. With the varied climates in Utah, many homeowners opt for mid-range options, such as standing seam steel, for long-term durability and cost savings.

Are Metal Roofs Worth the Money?

Metal roofing is often considered a worthwhile investment for many homeowners compared to other roofing materials. While you may pay more upfront for a metal roof, it can last up to 70 years and reduce cooling costs in hot climates like St. George.

Types of Metal Roofs

Several types of metal can be used for metal home roofs, including:

Zinc Roofing

Tin Roofing

Steel Roofing

Aluminum Roofing

Copper Roofing

Does a Metal Roof Devalue a House?

Metal roofs don’t devalue your house; they actually do the opposite, thanks to their energy efficiency, durability, and curb appeal. They’re also fire-resistant and can handle extreme weather better than traditional shingles.

Do Metal Roofs Leak More Than Shingles?

Metal roofs do not leak more than shingles since they have fewer seams and better insulation. With the right insulation, a metal roof can outperform shingles in preventing water from seeping in from the roof, especially during heavy storms and snowmelt.

Conclusion: Should I Get a Metal Roof?

Whether or not you decide on a metal roof should depend on several factors, like your budget, location, and preferences. Their long-term benefits make metal roofs a compelling option for many homeowners — one that’s well worth the investment.
