When winter shows up in Utah, it brings beautiful snow-covered mountains. However, it also
brings the risk of roof damage from ice. Some homes even experience roof collapse from snow.
So how do you keep your roof safe from heavy snow and ice? Below are ten helpful tips to
prepare your roof for Utah’s harsh winters.

1. How Do Heavy Snow Loads Affect My Roof?

Before diving into roof ice removal techniques, it’s essential to understand the potential damage
snow can cause. Ice dams and heavy snowfall can cause water to seep under shingles, causing
leaks and roof damage. But how much snow is too much for a roof? Ultimately, it depends on
the specific roof. However, the average roof can hold about 20 inches of packed snow or 40-45
inches of fresh snow.

2. Trim Your Tree Branches

One of the first things you can do to prevent roof damage from ice is to cut any overhanging tree
branches. Falling branches covered in snow can damage your shingles and gutters. Before
winter rolls in, trim back any tree branches close to your roof.

3. Clean Your Gutters

Clogged gutters can cause a lot of damage during winter. When leaves and debris build up in
your gutters, they obstruct water flow and create ice dams that cause water to back up under
your roof.

4. Remove Debris

Besides cleaning your gutters, removing debris from your roof is also a good idea. Leaves,
twigs, and other materials can trap moisture and cause roof decay. You can use a roof rake or
opt for a professional Salt Lake City roof cleaning company.

5. Insulate Your Attic

A well-insulated attic helps maintain a consistent temperature and prevents ice dams. Install
high-quality insulation to get your attic ready for the cold Utah winters.

6. Remove Snow Often as Possible

Remove snow as often as possible to prevent roof damage from ice and heavy snow loads.
However, be careful when removing snow because improper snow removal can damage
shingles and roofing materials. Consider hiring a professional for snow and ice removal from
your roof.

7. Repair Broken or Missing Shingles

Damaged or missing shingles can let water seep in and cause leaks. Before winter, inspect your
roof for any signs of damage and then repair them immediately.

8. Reseal Around Vents and Other Fixtures

The seals around your roof’s vents, skylights, and other fixtures can deteriorate over time.
Replacing or resealing these areas will protect your home during snowfalls.

9. Check Your Siding

Your siding is another area of your home that can be vulnerable during winter. To prevent water
from causing damage in your home, check your siding for any cracks, gaps, or signs of damage.

10. Install a Roof Ice Melt System

For additional help with ice removal from your roof, you can install a roof ice melt system. At
M&M Gutters, we offer an innovative system that prevents ice dams and keeps your roof safe
throughout the winter.

Potential Winter Problems if Your Roof Goes Unattended

Leaving your roof unattended can be a costly mistake. Here is some of the damage you could
face if you don’t properly prepare for winter.

Ice Dams

Ice dams are common during winter, especially in places like Utah that get a lot of snow. When
heat leaves your home, it warms the roof and melts the snow. The melted snow can refreeze
around the roof’s edges, forming ice dams. Without removal, ice dams prevent drainage, which
can cause water damage.

Heavy Snow Loads

Heavy snowfalls can put a lot of weight on your roof. Roofs are designed to hold a certain
amount of snow, but too much snow can cause structural damage.

Roof Leaks

Roof leaks are common if you don’t care for your roof. Leaks aren’t just an inconvenience —
they can also lead to extensive damage to your walls, ceilings, and insulation.

Shingle Damage

Cold temperatures and freezing can make shingles weak and crack. Heavy snow loads can also
put too much pressure on shingles and cause damage.

Condensation and Moisture

The difference in temperature between your home’s cold exterior and warm interior can cause
condensation in the attic. Condensation can lead to mold growth and wood rot, affecting your
home’s structural integrity and air quality.

Poorly Ventilated Attic

Improper ventilation in an attic can trap moisture, causing ice dams. Proper ventilation in your
attic is crucial in preventing moisture-related problems.

Roof Collapse

Roof collapse from snow is an issue some Utah homeowners face during the winter. Twenty
pounds of snow per square foot is usually how much weight a house roof can hold. Anything
heavier may cause a roof to collapse.

Damage to Gutters and Downspouts

Ice and debris can clog gutters and downspouts, leading to overflowing gutters and potential
water damage to your home’s foundation.
