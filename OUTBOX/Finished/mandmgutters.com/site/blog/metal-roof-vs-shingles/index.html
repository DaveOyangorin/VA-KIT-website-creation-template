Metal Roof vs. Shingles: What’s the Difference?

When it comes to roofing options, the choice between a metal vs. asphalt roof is crucial and can significantly impact your home’s aesthetics, durability, and energy efficiency. Read on to learn the differences between these two popular roofing materials to make an informed decision for your roofing needs.

What Are Asphalt Shingles?

Asphalt shingles are the most common type of roofing currently available. They are made of fiberglass or organic base mat covered with asphalt and ceramic granules, and you can buy them as sheets, stacks, or rolls for easier installation.

What Is Metal Roofing?

Metal roofs are typically made from steel, aluminum, or copper and come in large sheets. These sheets can be folded into various patterns, including flat-seam and corrugated metals. Many people prefer metal roofs vs. asphalt because they are more durable.

Metal Roofing Advantages and Disadvantages

When deciding between a metal or shingle roof, it’s essential to know the advantages and disadvantages of each. Here are the pros and cons of a metal roof.

Pros

  • Exceptionally durable
  • Fire-resistant
  • Eco-friendly
  • Sleek and popular design
  • Potential energy savings
  • Longevity
  • Minimal maintenance

Cons

  • Higher installation costs
  • Takes longer to install
  • Installation is more challenging
  • Heavier than other roof options

Asphalt Shingles Advantages and Disadvantages

Here are the pros and cons of shingles vs. a metal roof.

Advantages

  • Cost-effective and widely available
  • Aesthetically pleasing and versatile designs
  • Resistant to fire and storms
  • Low-maintenance
  • Easy repairs/installations
  • Commonly used by contractors
  • User-friendly and durable

Disadvantages

  • Vulnerable to extreme weather
  • No energy savings
  • Shorter lifespan
  • Few environmental benefits

Which Roofing Material Is Better Overall?

Now that we know the pros and cons of metal roofs and shingle roofs, it’s time to decide which material is the best option overall. Here’s a direct comparison of metal vs. asphalt roofs.

Maintenance and Care

Is a metal roof better than shingles when it comes to care and maintenance? Yes! Metal roofs need very little maintenance or attention over their lifespan. Shingles are also relatively low-maintenance but need to be replaced more often.

Longevity & Durability

Metal roofs can last almost twice as long as shingle roofs, making them a better investment for homeowners planning to stay in their homes long-term.

Installation

When comparing a metal vs. shingle roof installation, asphalt shingles are quicker and easier to install than metal roofs.

Cost

Looking at the cost of metal roofs vs. shingles, metal will have a higher initial cost due to the materials used and the longer, more intensive installation process

Water, Heat, and Environment

Metal roofs can reflect heat, reducing energy bills, and are known for their eco-friendly benefits since the material is recyclable. Asphalt shingles, however, have the upper hand when it comes to water flow. Rain quickly slides off metal roofs while moving slower on asphalt shingles, making shingles better suited for areas with lots of rain.

Resale Value

Metal roofs can increase the resale value of a home due to their durability and energy efficiency. Asphalt shingles, while attractive, may not have the same impact on resale value.

Aesthetics

Deciding between the aesthetics of a metal roof vs. shingles comes down to personal preference. Metal roofing is an excellent choice if you want a sleeker, more modern look. For a more traditional appearance, you can’t go wrong with asphalt shingles.

Cost of Metal Roof vs. Shingles?

Metal roofs are more expensive than asphalt shingles and range from $7,000 to over $100,000, depending on the size of your home and where you live. Asphalt shingles, on the other hand, can range from $5,000 to $18,000.

Conclusion: Which Roofing Material Is Better?

So, which is better, a metal or shingle roof? Ultimately, it depends on your priorities, aesthetics, and budget. Metal roofing has a higher upfront cost but is more durable and long-lasting, while asphalt shingles are more cost-effective and user-friendly.
