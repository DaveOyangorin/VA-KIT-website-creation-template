How Much Does It Cost to Have Rain Gutters Installed?

The cost to have rain gutters installed typically falls between $2,624 and $4,723 for a typical single-family home in 2026, or roughly $10 to $16 per linear foot installed.

Here’s a quick-reference breakdown:

Cost TypeTypical Range (2026)
Per linear foot (installed)$10.41 – $15.77
Average home (full system)$2,624 – $4,723
Budget range$628 – $1,732
Premium or complex installs$5,000 – $12,500+

Your final number depends on a few key variables:

  • Material — vinyl and aluminum are the most affordable; copper costs the most
  • Linear footage — most homes need 150 to 250 linear feet
  • Home height — two-story homes add $1 to $3 per foot in labor
  • Roof complexity — steep pitches, multiple gables, and valleys increase costs
  • Add-ons — downspouts, gutter guards, and fascia repairs all affect the total

Read on and we’ll walk through every factor in detail so you can build a realistic budget before calling anyone.

Let’s break it all down clearly so you can make a confident, informed decision.

Breaking Down the Average Cost to Have Rain Gutters Installed

When we talk about the national average for gutter projects, we often see a range of $2,623.82 to $4,722.87. However, averages can be a bit like weather forecasts in Salt Lake City—they give you a general idea, but the reality on your specific street might be different. Most homeowners across the country pay around $4,000 to replace gutters on a typical single-family home, usually falling within the $2,800 to $5,200 range.

The biggest driver of this number is linear footage. Most homes require between 150 and 250 linear feet of gutters. If you have a small ranch-style home, you might only need 100 feet; a sprawling two-story estate in Summit County could easily exceed 300 feet.

Calculating the Cost to Have Rain Gutters Installed per Linear Foot

As of January 2026, the benchmark cost to have rain gutters installed per linear foot sits between $10.41 and $15.77. This figure isn’t just for the metal or plastic hanging off your roof; it covers a blend of local material delivery, worksite preparation, protection of your landscaping, and the skilled labor required for a precise installation.

Labor generally accounts for 10% to 50% of the total project cost. For a basic installation, you are paying for the layout, fabrication (especially with seamless systems), securing the gutters to rafters, and ensuring a proper drainage slope. If the pitch is off by even a fraction of an inch, you’ll end up with pooling water and a very expensive mosquito nursery. You can find more detail on these breakdowns in this guide on Gutter Installation Cost: How Much Homeowners Typically Spend.

Regional Variations in the Cost to Have Rain Gutters Installed

In our neck of the woods—specifically Salt Lake City and the surrounding Northern Utah areas—pricing can vary from the national baseline. Utah’s average often trends slightly higher due to the demand for durable materials that can handle heavy snow loads and freeze-thaw cycles. Recent data suggests Utah averages can range from $18.56 to $22.55 per linear foot for high-quality professional installs.

Factors like labor availability in Davis or Weber County and the cost of shipping specialized materials to the Mountain West play a role. Furthermore, homes in high-rainfall or heavy-snow locations may require wider 6-inch gutters instead of the standard 5-inch, which can increase the system capacity and the price by up to 50%. Material Selection: Balancing Durability and Price

Choosing the right material is where you decide between a short-term fix and a long-term investment. In our Rain Gutter Materials Guide, we emphasize that the “cheapest” option isn’t always the best value over 20 years.

Aluminum and Vinyl Options

Aluminum is the heavyweight champion of the gutter world—not in actual weight, but in popularity. It is rust-resistant, lightweight, and comes in over 20 prefinished colors. You can expect to pay between $6 and $14 per linear foot installed for aluminum. It lasts a solid 20 to 30 years and handles the Utah climate quite well.

Vinyl (PVC) is the most budget-friendly choice, often starting as low as $3 to $7 per linear foot. While it won’t rust, it has a shorter lifespan of 10 to 15 years. In extreme cold, like we see in Summit County, vinyl can become brittle and crack. If you’re debating between the two, check out our comparison: Vinyl vs Aluminum Gutters: Which One is Best?.

Premium Steel and Copper Systems

For those looking for maximum strength or high-end aesthetics, steel and copper are the go-to choices.

  • Galvanized Steel: Costing $8 to $26 per foot, these are incredibly strong but require maintenance to prevent rust.
  • Copper: This is the “luxury” option, ranging from $25 to $50+ per foot. Copper gutters can last 50 to 100 years and develop a beautiful green patina over time. They are often required for historic homes in Salt Lake City to maintain architectural integrity.
  • Zinc: Similar to copper, zinc is self-sealing and can last over 80 years, though it carries a premium price tag of $40 to $50 per foot.

Factors That Shift Your Installation Estimate

Every home is unique, and a “simple” 2,000-square-foot house might cost more than a 3,000-square-foot one if the roof looks like a game of Tetris. If you notice 5 Signs Your Home Needs a New Gutter System, you’ll want to consider these cost-shifting factors:

  1. Home Height: Working on a two-story home is more dangerous and time-consuming. Expect a premium of $1 to $3 extra per linear foot for any work above the first floor.
  2. Roof Pitch and Complexity: Steep roofs require specialized “spike and ferrule” hangers or extra brackets to ensure the gutters stay put. Multiple gables, valleys, and “bump-outs” increase the number of mitered corners and joints, which adds to labor time.
  3. Accessibility: If our crew has to navigate tight spaces, overgrown landscaping, or complex architectural features, the labor hours will climb.

Additional Components and Hidden Fees

The gutters themselves are only part of the system. To get the water safely away from your foundation, you’ll need these essentials:

  • Downspouts: These cost between $5 and $40 per linear foot depending on material. We typically recommend one downspout for every 20 to 30 feet of gutter.
  • Gutter Guards: These are a popular add-on in 2026, costing between $7 and $20 per foot. They significantly reduce maintenance by keeping leaves and debris out.
  • Fascia Repair: If your old gutters were leaking, the wood behind them (the fascia board) might be rotted. Replacing this costs $5 to $12 per linear foot.
  • Removal and Disposal: Taking down your old, heavy gutters and hauling them to the dump usually adds $1 to $3 per linear foot to the bill.

Seamless vs. Sectional: Comparing Long-Term Value

If you’ve spent any time researching the cost to have rain gutters installed, you’ve likely seen the term “seamless.”

Performance and ROI

Sectional gutters are sold in pieces at big-box stores. They are cheaper upfront but have seams every 10 to 20 feet. Every seam is a potential leak point. Over time, the sealant in these joints fails, leading to drips that can rot your siding or flood your basement.

Seamless gutters are custom-fabricated on-site using a specialized machine that “spits out” a single continuous piece of metal tailored to the exact length of your roofline. Because they only have joints at the corners and downspouts, the risk of leaks is drastically reduced. While they cost more initially ($6 to $28 per foot depending on material), the maintenance savings and increased home value make them a far better investment. Frequently Asked Questions about Gutter Installation

Is it cheaper to install gutters yourself?

Technically, yes, you save on labor. However, we’ve spent 30 years fixing DIY “saves” that ended up costing double. DIY risks include:

  • Safety: Ladder falls are one of the leading causes of home improvement injuries.
  • Pitch Issues: If the gutters aren’t sloped perfectly toward the downspouts, water will pool, leading to rust and clogs.
  • Warranty: Most professional materials come with warranties that are voided if not installed by a licensed pro. Avoid these Common Gutter Installation Mistakes by hiring a team with the right equipment.

How long does a typical gutter installation take?

For most homes in Salt Lake or Utah County, we can complete a full installation in a single day. If there is extensive fascia repair needed or if the home is particularly large and complex, it might stretch into a second day. Weather is the only real wild card—we can’t install in high winds or heavy snow for safety reasons.

How often should new gutters be replaced?

This depends entirely on the material and how well you maintain them.

  • Vinyl: 10–15 years
  • Aluminum: 20–30 years
  • Galvanized Steel: 20 years
  • Copper/Zinc: 50–100 years Regular cleaning (twice a year) can add years to these estimates. For a deeper dive, read How Long Do Rain Gutters Last?.

Conclusion
