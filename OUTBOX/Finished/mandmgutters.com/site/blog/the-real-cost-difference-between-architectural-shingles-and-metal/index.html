What’s the Real Cost Difference Between Architectural Shingles and Metal Roofs?

Architectural shingles vs metal roof cost is one of the most common questions Utah homeowners face when it’s time to replace a roof. Here’s the short answer:

Roofing TypeCost per Sq Ft (Installed)Lifespan
Architectural shingles$5 – $1025 – 30 years
Exposed fastener metal$4.50 – $6.5040 – 60 years
Standing seam metal$12 – $1650 – 70+ years
Premium copper metal$20 – $5070 – 100+ years

For a typical 2,000 sq ft home, that means roughly $10,000 – $20,000 for architectural shingles versus $18,000 – $80,000+ for metal, depending on the metal type you choose.

The gap looks steep upfront. But the real story is what happens over 30 to 50 years — because with shingles, you may be paying for that roof twice.

A lifetime cost analysis of a 2,500 sq ft home puts shingles at around $46,000 over 30 years (accounting for a second replacement and inflation), while a metal roof comes in closer to $32,000 over 40+ years. That’s a meaningful difference.

I’m Nathan Nuttall from M&M Gutters & Exteriors, and after more than 30 years helping Utah homeowners navigate roofing decisions — including weighing architectural shingles vs metal roof cost in our specific climate — I’ve seen how the right choice today can save thousands down the road. Let’s break down exactly what drives these numbers so you can make a confident decision.

Upfront Installation Costs: Architectural Shingles vs Metal Roof Cost

When you start looking at quotes, the first thing you’ll notice is the “sticker shock” associated with metal. In Northern Utah, we find that many homeowners initially lean toward architectural shingles because the entry price is much more accessible for a standard household budget.

However, the term “metal roof” is broad. The architectural shingles vs metal roof cost comparison changes significantly depending on whether you are looking at a budget-friendly exposed fastener system or a high-end standing seam roof.

Material TypeEstimated Cost Per Square Foot (Installed)Average Total for 2,000 Sq. Ft. Roof
Architectural Shingles$5.00 – $10.00$10,000 – $20,000
Exposed Fastener Metal$4.50 – $8.50$9,000 – $17,000
Standing Seam Metal$12.00 – $18.00$24,000 – $36,000
Premium Metal (Copper/Zinc)$20.00 – $50.00$40,000 – $100,000+

Breaking Down Architectural Shingles vs Metal Roof Cost per Square Foot

Architectural shingles, also known as dimensional or laminate shingles, are the most popular choice in Salt Lake City. They are composed of a fiberglass mat base coated in asphalt and topped with ceramic granules. Unlike old-fashioned 3-tab shingles, architectural versions are thicker and create a multi-dimensional look that mimics wood shakes or slate. You can learn more about these variations in our guide to roofing materials and types.

On the metal side, the price is heavily dictated by the “profile.”

  • Exposed Fastener: These are the corrugated panels you often see on barns or sheds. They are the most affordable metal option, sometimes even competing directly with high-end shingles. However, because the screws are exposed to the elements, they require more maintenance.
  • Standing Seam: This is the “gold standard” for residential metal roofing. The fasteners are hidden beneath interlocking seams, creating a sleek, watertight surface. This complexity is why the cost jumps to double or triple that of shingles.
  • Premium Metals: Materials like copper or zinc are lifetime investments that can cost upwards of $50 per square foot. These are usually reserved for historic restorations or high-end custom homes in areas like Summit County.

Material vs Labor: Why Metal Costs More Upfront

It isn’t just the price of the steel or aluminum that drives up the bill. The labor requirements for metal are significantly more intensive. According to a Forbes Home analysis on metal vs asphalt, the labor-to-material ratio flips between these two options.

For a shingle roof, labor often accounts for about 60% of the cost, while materials are 40%. For a standing seam metal roof, the materials themselves are much more expensive (often 60% of the total), and the labor requires specialized skills, heavy-duty fabrication tools, and more time. Shingles can be “racked” or installed quickly by a large crew. Metal panels must be precisely measured, cut, and seamed, often on-site, which slows down the process and increases the daily labor rate.

Long-Term Value and Lifetime Cost Scenarios

To truly understand architectural shingles vs metal roof cost, you have to look past the day you write the check. You have to look 30, 40, or even 50 years into the future.

Lifespan and Replacement Cycles

In the Intermountain West, our roofs take a beating. Between the intense UV rays in the summer and the heavy snow loads in the winter, materials wear down faster than they might in milder climates.

Architectural shingles typically last 25 to 30 years in Utah. While they are durable, the asphalt eventually dries out, and the granules wash away, leaving the mat exposed to leaks. A metal roof, however, is often a “once-in-a-lifetime” purchase. A well-installed standing seam roof can easily last 50 to 70 years, and some copper roofs have been known to last over a century. We discuss this further in our article on how long does a roof last in utah.

Consider this scenario for a 2,500 sq. ft. roof:

  • Shingles: You pay $15,000 today. In 25 years, you pay for a full tear-off and replacement. Factoring in a modest 3% annual inflation, that second roof could cost you over $31,000. Total 50-year cost: $46,000+.
  • Metal: You pay $32,000 today. Aside from minor maintenance, you never replace it. Total 50-year cost: $32,000.

Resale Value and ROI

Does a metal roof help you sell your house? The data is a bit mixed. According to the 2024 Cost versus Value Report, asphalt shingle replacements often show a higher immediate “recoup” percentage (around 57%) compared to metal (around 48%).

This is largely because many buyers aren’t willing to pay a $20,000 premium just for the roof when they are already stretching their budget to buy the home. However, in high-end markets like Park City, a metal roof is often viewed as a “luxury” feature that can increase curb appeal and provide peace of mind to a buyer who doesn’t want to deal with roof maintenance for the next several decades.

Factors That Influence Your Total Roofing Investment

No two roofs are the same, and your neighbor’s quote for architectural shingles vs metal roof cost might look nothing like yours. Several physical factors play a role in the final tally.

Impact of Roof Complexity and Pitch on Architectural Shingles vs Metal Roof Cost

Complexity is the enemy of a cheap roof. Every time the roof changes direction (valleys and hips) or has a penetration (chimneys, skylights, vents), the price goes up.

  • Pitch: A “walkable” roof is cheaper to install. Once the slope gets steep enough that installers need harnesses and specialized scaffolding, labor costs rise. Metal is particularly difficult to install on steep slopes because it is slippery and harder to maneuver than shingles.
  • Waste: Shingles are small and easy to trim around corners with minimal waste. Metal panels are large. If you have a roof with many small facets, you may end up paying for 10-15% more material than you actually use because of the way panels must be cut.
  • Accessibility: If our trucks can’t get close to your home or if you have delicate landscaping that requires extra protection, expect to see a “difficulty” surcharge on your estimate.

For more details on these variables, check out our breakdown of the cost of a roof replacement roof care in utah.

Regional Considerations for Utah Homeowners

Living in Salt Lake City or Davis County means preparing for “The Greatest Snow on Earth.”

  • Snow Loads: Metal roofs are excellent for heavy snow areas because the snow tends to slide off once the sun hits the panels. This prevents ice damming, which is a common (and expensive) problem for shingle roofs.
  • UV Exposure: Our high altitude means more intense UV rays. This “cooks” asphalt shingles over time, causing them to become brittle. Metal is largely unaffected by UV.
  • Wind: Both materials perform well in wind if installed correctly, but standing seam metal often carries higher wind ratings, making it a favorite for homes on the “benches” of the Wasatch Front. If you notice shingles in your yard after a storm, it’s one of the 12 signs you need a new roof.

Performance Comparison: Durability, Energy, and Maintenance

Beyond the price tag, you’re paying for performance. How will each roof handle a July heatwave or a March hail storm?

Maintenance and Repair Costs

Maintenance is where the two materials differ most.

  • Shingles: You’ll need to keep an eye on granule loss and ensure no shingles have blown loose. Repairs are generally cheap ($300–$1,000) because a pro can swap out individual shingles easily.
  • Metal: While incredibly durable, metal isn’t “maintenance-free.” For exposed fastener roofs, the rubber washers on the screws will eventually dry out and need to be replaced every 15-20 years. For standing seam, you mostly just need to ensure the gutters are clear and the flashing around chimneys remains tight. Repairs on metal are more expensive ($800–$2,500) because panels are harder to replace than single shingles. You can read more in our metal roofing pros cons guide.

Energy Efficiency and Weather Resistance

Metal is the clear winner for energy efficiency. According to insights from the Metal Roofing Alliance, metal roofs can be 15% to 25% cooler in the summer than dark asphalt shingles. This is because metal reflects solar radiant heat rather than absorbing it. In a Salt Lake City summer, this can lead to significant savings on your cooling bill.

Furthermore, metal is highly fire-resistant (Class A rating) and sheds rain and snow much more efficiently than shingles. If you are considering solar panels, standing seam metal is the ideal “host.” Solar installers can use specialized clamps that attach to the seams without ever piercing the roof surface, virtually eliminating the risk of leaks.

Frequently Asked Questions about Architectural Shingles vs Metal Roof Cost

Is a metal roof worth the higher initial cost compared to architectural shingles?

For many, yes. If you plan to stay in your home for more than 15-20 years, the “lifetime cost” of a metal roof is actually lower than shingles. You save money by avoiding a second replacement, reducing your energy bills, and potentially qualifying for lower homeowners insurance premiums due to the roof’s fire and wind resistance. However, if you plan to move in five years, you likely won’t see a full return on that investment. You can find more comparison points in our post on metal roof vs shingles.

How do warranties differ between architectural shingles and metal roofs?

Shingle warranties are often “limited lifetime” but are heavily prorated. This means as the roof gets older, the manufacturer pays out less. Many shingle warranties also “reset” or expire when the home is sold.

Metal warranties, specifically those for Kynar 500-coated steel, often cover the paint finish for 30-40 years and the substrate for even longer. These warranties are frequently transferable to the next homeowner, which can be a significant selling point.

Can a metal roof be installed over existing architectural shingles to save money?

In some cases, yes. This is often called a “re-roof.” It saves you the cost of tear-off and disposal (usually $1,000–$5,000). However, it’s not always recommended. Adding a second layer can trap heat and moisture, and some local building codes in Utah limit the number of roofing layers you can have. At M&M Gutters & Exteriors, we usually recommend a full tear-off to inspect the roof deck for rot before installing a heavy, long-term investment like metal.

Conclusion

The choice between architectural shingles vs metal roof cost ultimately comes down to your “time horizon” and your budget. If you want a beautiful, reliable roof at a price that fits today’s budget, architectural shingles are a fantastic choice that we’ve installed on thousands of homes across Northern Utah. If you want a “forever roof” that lowers your energy bills and ends your roofing worries for good, metal is the way to go.
