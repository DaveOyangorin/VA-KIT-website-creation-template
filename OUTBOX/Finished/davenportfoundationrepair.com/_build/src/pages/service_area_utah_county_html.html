The Warning Signs: Cracks, Separation, and Sinking

The first visible signs of trouble were cracks in the drywall, a classic early warning signal of foundation movement. However, these weren't limited to cosmetic hairline fractures — they extended across walls and widened over time, indicating significant structural movement below the surface.

Upon inspection, it was clear the concrete slab floor was also affected. Sections of the slab had visibly sunk and cracked, as captured in the images above. In some areas, you could even see daylight beneath the baseboards, showing just how far the structure had shifted away from its original placement. These symptoms suggested a major loss of soil support under the foundation — a dangerous scenario for any home.

Understanding the Cause: Unique Utah Soil Conditions

Foundation issues in Utah often stem from the region’s soil composition and climate. Lindon, like many parts of Utah County, sits on a mix of expansive clay soils and loamy substrates that swell when wet and shrink during dry periods. Add in freeze-thaw cycles and potential poor drainage around the home, and you’ve got a recipe for serious foundation instability.

Over time, these soil shifts can cause uneven support beneath a home’s foundation. If not corrected, this leads to settling, floor sinking, and structural cracking — just like what happened in this Lindon home.

The Solution: PolyLevel Injection and 80 Steel Foundation Piers

Given the magnitude of the damage, a typical foundation fix simply wouldn’t suffice. Our team designed a multi-tiered approach using two proven technologies:

  1. PolyLevel Concrete Lifting:
    We began by stabilizing and lifting the sunken concrete slab using PolyLevel — a high-density polyurethane foam injected beneath the concrete. As the foam expands, it fills voids in the soil and raises the slab back to its original level. Unlike traditional mudjacking, PolyLevel is lightweight, long-lasting, and minimally invasive.

  2. Installation of 80 Foundation Piers:
    To address the underlying structural failure, we installed approximately 80 steel push piers around the home’s foundation. These piers were driven deep into load-bearing soil or bedrock, bypassing the unstable upper layers entirely. Once the piers were in place, they were used to lift and permanently stabilize the home.

This combination of technologies not only stopped further movement but also corrected the settling that had already occurred.

Total Cost and Scope: A Major Investment in Stability

At a total project cost of $144,000, this was one of the largest residential foundation repairs we’ve completed in Utah. It serves as a powerful reminder that while preventative maintenance and early intervention may come with a price, ignoring warning signs can lead to far costlier repairs in the future.

Key Takeaways for Utah Homeowners

  • Cracks Aren’t Just Cosmetic:
    Cracks in drywall, concrete slabs, or along baseboards can be signs of a deeper issue. Don't ignore them.

  • Soil Movement Is Common in Utah:
    Expansive clay soils, freeze-thaw cycles, and inconsistent moisture levels can wreak havoc on foundations over time.

  • Early Inspections Save Money:
    If caught early, foundation issues can often be corrected with fewer piers, less disruption, and lower cost.

  • Permanent Fixes Require Engineering:
    DIY fixes or cosmetic patches won't solve a settling foundation. Steel piers and structural solutions like PolyLevel offer long-term protection.
