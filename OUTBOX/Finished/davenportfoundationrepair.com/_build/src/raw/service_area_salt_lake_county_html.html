                                  Waterproofing an Unfinished Basement in Herriman, Utah

                                  In the quiet, fast-growing suburb of Herriman, Utah, one homeowner faced a frustrating and potentially costly problem: water was leaking into their unfinished basement. With exposed framing and bare concrete floors, the space was vulnerable to moisture damage—and without a solution, that dampness could lead to mold, structural issues, and unusable living space.

                                  Fortunately, the homeowner reached out to us before the problem worsened. Our expert crew, led by Tony Johnston, was dispatched to evaluate and repair the issue. After a thorough inspection on June 27th, 2025, the team determined that groundwater was intruding at the base of the foundation walls—a common issue in basements throughout Utah, particularly during the wet season or after heavy irrigation.

                                  Identifying the Problem

                                  The initial photos taken by the homeowner showed clear signs of moisture along the base of the wall—insulation showing signs of dampness and personal items pushed aside in an effort to protect them from water. With the space unfinished, there was a clear opportunity to waterproof properly before drywall or flooring was ever added.

                                  Tony and the team identified the entry points along the perimeter of the foundation, particularly where hydrostatic pressure was pushing water through cold joints and potential wall cracks. This type of issue is often worsened by poor exterior drainage, making it essential to address both interior and exterior factors.

                                  Custom Waterproofing Solution

                                  To protect the basement from future water intrusion, Tony implemented a comprehensive waterproofing system using industry-leading products from Supportworks. Here's what the solution included:

                                  1. CleanSpace Wall System

                                  A durable, vapor-barrier liner was installed along the basement walls. This white liner, known as CleanSpace, seals out water vapor and helps direct any wall seepage down into the drainage system below. It also improves the overall appearance of the basement, providing a clean, bright surface ready for future finishing.

                                  2. WaterGuard Interior Drain System

                                  Beneath the perimeter of the concrete slab, our crew installed the WaterGuard drainage channel—a patented system designed to capture any water that seeps through walls or the cove joint and redirect it to a sump pump. Unlike traditional French drains, WaterGuard sits atop the footing to prevent clogging and ensures a long-lasting, effective barrier against basement flooding.

                                  3. SuperSump Pump System

                                  Collected water is then expelled by the SuperSump, a powerful and reliable sump pump system built to handle large volumes of water with efficiency. The unit includes an airtight lid (preventing odors and evaporation), a cast-iron pump, and a built-in alarm to alert the homeowner of high water levels.

                                  4. Buried Downspout Extensions

                                  To protect the foundation from exterior water sources, we also installed buried downspout extensions. These pipes channel roof runoff far away from the home, preventing re-saturation of the soil next to the basement walls. It’s a crucial step many homeowners overlook, but it significantly reduces the chance of recurring leaks.

                                  Before & After: A Big Transformation

                                  The transformation of the space was immediate and visible. The “before” photo shows an exposed wall with hanging insulation, cluttered storage, and visible moisture risk. The “after” photo reveals a clean, sealed, and protected wall lined with CleanSpace and a freshly poured concrete trench covering the newly installed WaterGuard system.

                                  This basement is now fully prepared for finishing—or for safe, dry storage. Most importantly, the homeowner no longer needs to worry about water intrusion.

                                  The Problem: Sinking & Cracking in a High-Traffic Area

                                  Concrete driveways are exposed to some of the harshest conditions, especially in Utah, where freeze-thaw cycles, dry spells, and clay-rich soil can wreak havoc on slabs. In this case, the right side of the driveway, adjacent to a covered parking area, had visibly dropped. The settling created misalignment along the joints, making it difficult to walk, drive, or park comfortably.

                                  Complicating matters was a significant amount of caulking and sealant that had deteriorated over time. Cracks and joints no longer had proper protection, which meant moisture intrusion was likely contributing to voids forming under the slab. If left unaddressed, the damage could quickly escalate, leading to larger cracks, broken edges, or full slab failure.

                                  The Solution: A 3-Part Concrete Protection System

                                  After a thorough inspection, our team recommended a complete Concrete Protection System tailored for long-term results:

                                  1. Precision Lifting with PolyLevel®

                                  We used 175 pounds of PolyLevel, a high-density polyurethane foam, to lift and level the settled slab. This product is injected through small, dime-sized holes drilled into the concrete surface. Once injected, it expands rapidly, filling voids and lifting the slab back into its proper position — often within minutes.

                                  This approach is perfect for Utah’s varied soils because it doesn’t wash out, shrink, or degrade over time like mudjacking. Even better, it’s lightweight, meaning it won’t contribute to additional soil compression beneath the slab.

                                  2. Caulk Removal & Crack Repair

                                  Before applying new sealant, we removed approximately 39 feet of old caulking that had failed and started to separate from the concrete. These gaps often let water seep below the surface, worsening soil instability.

                                  We then applied 207 feet of NexusPro®, a professional-grade silicone sealant designed for concrete joints and cracks. Unlike store-bought caulks, NexusPro remains flexible and UV-resistant, so it won’t dry out, crack, or peel under the hot West Jordan sun.

                                  3. Full Surface Sealing

                                  To complete the project, we treated 1,724 square feet of concrete with our 3-part Concrete Protection System, which involves surface cleaning, crack filling, and the application of a penetrating sealant. This coating enhances the appearance of the slab and acts as a shield against future stains, moisture, and surface erosion.

                                  Results: Safe, Level, and Protected Concrete

                                  By the end of the day, the driveway looked dramatically better — and more importantly, it performed like new. The lifted slab was level with adjacent sections, eliminating trip hazards and improving water drainage. The new NexusPro sealant created clean, uniform joints that will resist weathering for years to come. And with the entire surface sealed, the homeowners can feel confident their concrete investment is protected from further damage.

                                  The crew, led by Tristan, worked efficiently and left the job site clean and professional — just as you’d expect from a team that cares about quality workmanship and customer satisfaction.

                                  Why This Matters for West Jordan Homeowners

                                  In Utah, where shifting soils and seasonal changes are common, concrete damage is not just cosmetic, it’s structural. Sinking slabs, cracked joints, and unsealed surfaces open the door to more serious problems, from foundation heaving to frost damage.

                                  By choosing modern products like PolyLevel® and NexusPro®, this West Jordan homeowner avoided the mess and cost of full slab replacement and added years of durability to their driveway.                                   Managing water at the base of the foundation was an important part of this Draper basement waterproofing project.

                                  The crew installed WaterGuard, an interior perimeter drainage system designed specifically for basement waterproofing. WaterGuard is installed along the perimeter of the basement where the foundation wall meets the floor.

                                  Rather than allowing water entering along the foundation to spread across the basement floor, the drainage system provides a controlled path for that water.

                                  The system then directs collected water toward the home's sump pump.

                                  Because WaterGuard is installed along the basement perimeter and is largely concealed once installation is complete, homeowners can regain usable basement space while still having a drainage system working beneath the surface.

                                  With the concrete foundation exposed, Davenport installed an 8-foot CleanSpace wall system.

                                  The white wall material visible in the project photos isn't simply there to improve the appearance of the exposed concrete. CleanSpace serves as an important part of the basement water-management system.

                                  If moisture or water enters through the concrete foundation wall, the CleanSpace system helps direct it downward toward the perimeter drainage system instead of allowing it to enter the finished basement.

                                  This creates a coordinated approach to waterproofing:

                                  Foundation wall → CleanSpace → WaterGuard → sump pump → exterior discharge

                                  Instead of treating individual signs of water intrusion independently, the components work together as one system.

                                  Managing Water Around the Basement Window Well

                                  Basement windows introduce another potential pathway for water.

                                  For this project, the waterproofing scope included a WellDuct system at the basement window well. WellDuct provides drainage for water that accumulates in the window well and directs it into the basement's waterproofing system.

                                  This is particularly important because window wells sit below the surrounding grade. Rain, snowmelt, irrigation, and groundwater can potentially collect in these areas if drainage isn't adequate.

                                  Connecting the window well to the basement drainage system helps provide another controlled route for unwanted water.

                                  Installing a SuperSump

                                  Collecting water is only part of a complete basement waterproofing solution. That water also needs somewhere to go.

                                  A SuperSump sump pump system was installed as part of this Draper project.

                                  Water collected by the WaterGuard and related drainage components is directed toward the sump system. The pump can then remove that water from the basement before it has an opportunity to spread across the floor or affect the finished space.

                                  The photos from the project show how the sump location was incorporated into the waterproofing system while working around the home's existing layout.

                                  Carrying Water Away From the Foundation

                                  The waterproofing system doesn't end at the sump pump.

                                  Davenport also extended the exterior discharge line so water pumped from the basement could be carried farther away from the home.

                                  This final step is important. Discharging water immediately beside a foundation can allow it to soak back into the soil surrounding the home, potentially putting that same water back near the basement walls.

                                  The exterior portion of this project helps move discharged water away from the foundation and completes the water-management path that begins inside the basement.
