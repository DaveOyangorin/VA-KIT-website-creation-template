What is the Average Cost to Install a Metal Roof?

The Real Question: How Much Will a Metal Roof Set You Back?

If you’ve been watching shingles blow off in a Utah storm or worrying about leaks every time it snows, you’re probably wondering if a metal roof is worth the investment. The truth is, metal roofing isn’t cheap upfront, but it can save you thousands over its lifetime.

This guide breaks down exactly what homeowners in the Salt Lake Valley should expect to pay, why prices vary so much, and how to tell if it’s the right choice for your home. If you’re in Utah and considering a replacement roof, you’ll walk away knowing whether metal makes financial sense for you.

Average Cost of a Metal Roof in Utah

On average, homeowners in the Salt Lake Valley can expect to spend $18,000 to $40,000 for a full metal roof installation. The wide range comes down to roof size, style, and material choice.

  1. Cost per square foot: $9-$17 installed

  2. 2,000 sq ft home: $18,000-$34,000

  3. High-end standing seam: $25,000-$40,000+

Here’s the thing: while asphalt shingles might run about half this cost upfront, metal can last two to three times longer. Over decades, that usually makes it the smarter investment.

What Impacts the Price of a Metal Roof?

Not all metal roofs cost the same. These are the biggest factors that swing the final price:

  1. Roof size and complexity: More angles, dormers, and steep slopes mean more labor.

  2. Material type: Aluminum, steel, and copper each come with different price tags.

  3. Tear-off vs new construction: Removing old shingles adds labor and disposal costs.

  4. Underlayment and insulation needs: Utah’s climate often calls for extra protection.

  5. Warranties and certifications: Master Roofer Certified crews may cost more, but the workmanship and warranty protection are worth it.

Think of it like buying a car. The base model is one thing. Add premium materials, complex design, and long-term warranties, and you’re investing in a higher-performing roof.

Metal Roof vs Asphalt Shingles: Which Pays Off?

Many homeowners start with the same question: “Why spend double when shingles are cheaper?” Let’s break it down:

  1. Lifespan: Shingles last 15-20 years. Metal lasts 40-70 years.

  2. Durability: Metal stands up to wind, snow, and hail better than asphalt.

  3. Energy savings: Reflective coatings can cut cooling bills by 10-25%.

  4. Home value: A new metal roof can boost resale value, especially in Utah’s competitive housing market.

So yes, you’ll pay more upfront. But you’ll likely avoid two full roof replacements down the line.

Benefits and Drawbacks You Should Know

No roof is perfect. Here’s the balanced view:

✔️ Benefits

  1. Long lifespan with minimal maintenance

  2. Excellent weather protection in Utah’s storms and snow

  3. Fire-resistant and energy-efficient

  4. Adds value and curb appeal

❌ Drawbacks

  1. Higher upfront investment

  2. Some homeowners worry about noise in heavy rain (though insulation helps)

  3. Fewer style options compared to shingles

If you plan to stay in your home for 15 years or more, metal usually wins on value.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions

How long does a metal roof last?

Depending on the material, 40-70 years is typical. Compare that to shingles, which usually last 15-20 years.

Is it cheaper to get a metal roof or shingles?

Shingles are cheaper upfront. Metal wins on lifetime value because it lasts two to three times longer and requires fewer replacements.

Does a metal roof increase home value?

Yes. National reports show homeowners can recover up to 60-70% of the cost in resale value. Buyers like knowing they won’t need a new roof anytime soon.

Are metal roofs noisy when it rains?

Not if installed correctly. Modern underlayments and insulation reduce noise significantly. Most homeowners don’t notice any difference from shingles.

What’s the biggest problem with metal roofs?

The upfront investment. But if you plan to stay in your home, the long-term savings and protection outweigh that concern.
