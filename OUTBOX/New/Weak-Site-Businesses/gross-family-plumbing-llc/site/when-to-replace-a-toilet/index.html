When to replace a toilet: what WaterSense says about old, thirsty models

From the blog · Oct 23, 2026

Contact us 385-331-1988

If your toilet was made before the current federal standard, it may use as much as 6 gallons per flush. EPA WaterSense says replacing old toilets with WaterSense-labeled models, which use 1.28 gallons or less, can cut toilet water use by 20 to 60 percent: nearly 13,000 gallons and more than $170 a year for a home. Before replacing anything, run the 10-minute dye test to see whether your toilet is simply leaking.

Toilets don’t get much attention until they run, clog or crack. But according to the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency’s WaterSense program, toilets are the largest single user of water inside a typical home, accounting for nearly 30 percent of indoor water use. That makes an old toilet one of the easiest places to save water. Here’s how to tell what you have and when an upgrade makes sense.

How much water toilets use

WaterSense lays out three numbers worth knowing:

  • Older toilets: can use as much as 6 gallons per flush, which WaterSense calls a major source of wasted water.
  • The federal standard: 1.6 gallons per flush for toilets sold today.
  • WaterSense-labeled toilets: 1.28 gallons per flush or less, 20 percent below the federal standard.

The difference compounds with every person in the house and every flush, every day. Our toilet water calculator shows what it adds up to over a year for your household.

Finding your toilet’s flush volume

Many toilets have the gallons per flush (gpf) or liters per flush printed on the porcelain, often just behind the seat hinge or inside the tank on the back wall. If you see 3.5 gpf or higher, or no marking at all on an older fixture, you likely have one of the thirstier models WaterSense describes. A toilet marked 1.6 gpf meets today’s federal standard; one marked 1.28 or lower matches the WaterSense level.

What replacing an old toilet saves

WaterSense estimates that replacing old, inefficient toilets with WaterSense-labeled models could reduce the water used for toilets by 20 to 60 percent. For a typical home, it puts that at nearly 13,000 gallons of water a year, and says households could save more than $170 a year in water costs.

Your own savings depend on how old your current toilets are, how many people live in the home and how often the toilets are used, which is exactly what the calculator lets you adjust.

Leaks: the other half of the story

An efficient toilet that leaks isn’t efficient. WaterSense’s Fix a Leak Week materials note that the average household’s leaks can account for more than 9,300 gallons of water wasted every year, and that nine percent of homes have leaks wasting 50 gallons or more per day. Nationwide, it estimates household leaks waste nearly 1 trillion gallons a year.

A running or silently leaking toilet is a common culprit, and it’s easy to check.

The 10-minute dye test

  1. Put a few drops of food coloring in the toilet tank.
  2. Wait 10 minutes without flushing.
  3. Look in the bowl. If any color shows up, WaterSense says you have a leak.
  4. Flush right away afterward so the coloring doesn’t stain the tank.

A dye test result isn’t automatically a reason to replace the fixture. It is a reason to have it looked at.

Check the whole house

WaterSense suggests two more checks. Read your water meter before and after a two-hour period when no water is being used; if the reading changes, you probably have a leak somewhere. And look at your water use in a cold month such as January or February: if a family of four uses more than 12,000 gallons a month, there could be serious leaks.

Signs it may be time to replace

  • The toilet is an older model using 3.5 gallons or more per flush.
  • It keeps leaking or running after repairs.
  • The porcelain is cracked.
  • You’re remodeling the bathroom anyway.

When you do replace, look for the WaterSense label, which tells you the toilet uses 1.28 gallons per flush or less.
