120°F · CPSC’s recommendation

The setting CPSC urges for every home.

Figures: U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission, “Avoiding Tap Water Scalds” (Publication 5098): most adults suffer third-degree burns from 150°F water in 2 seconds, 140°F in 6 seconds, 130°F in 30 seconds, and 120°F in 5 minutes. Dial markings aren’t precise; CPSC suggests checking at the tap with a thermometer.

Set it right

Four steps from CPSC and DOE

Why 120°F is the number
01

Measure at the tap

CPSC suggests holding a candy or meat thermometer under the faucet, first thing in the morning or at least two hours after hot water use.

02

Aim for 120°F

CPSC urges lowering water heaters to 120°F; the Department of Energy’s checklist calls it the warm setting and says it saves energy and prevents scalding.

03

Electric: two thermostats

CPSC notes most electric water heaters have two thermostats, both set to the same temperature, behind access panels. Shut off the power at the breaker first.

04

Gas: follow the maker

Gas thermostats differ, so CPSC says to follow the manufacturer’s instructions or call your gas company, then re-check with the thermometer.

Sources: CPSC Publication 5098; U.S. Department of Energy, FEMP Home Energy Checklist.
