Full home coverage

Seven places pests live. Tap a letter.

Every space, indoors and out, from the eaves to the basement. Pick a zone to see which Utah pests turn up there and how they get in.

AZone A of G

Eaves

  • WaspsPaper wasps, yellow jackets and hornets build nests under eaves, in trees and even inside wall voids.
  • BeetlesElm seed and boxelder bugs gather on the sunny sides of buildings, especially in the fall.

BZone B of G

Bedrooms

  • MothsClothes moths hide in closets and storage areas and damage fabrics.
  • SpidersSpiders turn up in corners of rooms; regular dusting and vacuuming discourages new webs.

CZone C of G

Garage

  • SpidersGarages are one of the places Utah spiders are most often found.
  • RodentsSeal cracks and gaps around doors to keep rodents from getting in.

DZone D of G

Kitchen & Living Room

  • AntsAnts come in for food and moisture, forming long trails along counters and floors.
  • MothsPantry moths target flour and grains in kitchen cabinets.
  • FliesHouseflies gather around garbage, fruit flies near overripe produce, drain flies in drains.
  • RodentsRodent activity runs along walls, in pantries and behind appliances.

EZone E of G

Yard

  • MosquitoMosquitoes breed in standing water: ponds, ditches, birdbaths, clogged gutters.
  • Snails and SlugsSnails and slugs feed on plants in damp, shaded areas.
  • GrasshopperGrasshoppers strip gardens and landscapes in hot, dry summers.

FZone F of G

Foundation

  • AntsAnts get in through cracks in the foundation; sealing them is the first prevention step.
  • RodentsRodents use cracks and gaps around foundations to get inside.

GZone G of G

Basement

  • SpidersBasements are one of the places Utah spiders are most often found.
  • RodentsSigns of rodents: droppings, gnawed materials and scratching in walls or ceilings.

Common Utah pests

The field guide.

How to identify Utah’s most common pests, how to get rid of them, and how to keep them from coming back. Ten species, one page each.

No. 01 MosquitoCulicidae

Swarm near water from late spring to fall; some species spread West Nile virus.

Field notes No. 02 AntsFormicidae

Tiny but persistent, they come inside looking for food and water.

Field notes No. 03 SpidersAraneae

Most are harmless; black widows and hobo spiders call for caution.

Field notes No. 04 RodentsRodentia

Gnaw wiring and insulation, contaminate food and pose health risks.

Field notes No. 05 WaspsVespidae

Paper wasps, yellow jackets and hornets defend their nests hard.

Field notes No. 06 BeetlesSeasonal invaders

Boxelder and elm seed bugs gather on homes in large numbers.

Field notes No. 07 FliesDiptera

Houseflies, fruit flies and drain flies can spread bacteria.

Field notes No. 08 MothsLepidoptera

Pantry moths go for dry goods; clothes moths go for fabrics.

Field notes No. 09 Snails and SlugsGastropoda

Slow-moving but hard on Utah gardens and landscapes.

Field notes No. 10 GrasshopperOrthoptera

Multiply fast and strip gardens in hot, dry summers.

Everything you need to know

Questions, answered.

01Is pest control safe for pets?

It depends on what’s used. Many commercial pesticides contain chemicals like pyrethroids and organophosphates, which can be toxic to pets if ingested or inhaled. Pestx uses eco-friendly practices that remove the dangers pests pose to your animals, and treated areas should stay off-limits to pets until sprays or powders have dried. Read the full post.

02Does one spray work for most bugs?

No. Different pests react to different treatments, some bugs build resistance, and eggs and larvae hidden away often need follow-up treatments. A combination of targeted solutions works better than one spray. Read the full post.

03How do bugs get inside my home?

Through gaps around windows and doors, cracks in the foundation and walls, vents and chimneys, and gaps around pipes and wiring. Uncovered food and open trash bins give them a reason to look. Read the full post.

04What does year-round protection mean?

All-season pest control coverage, month to month, that adapts to seasonal changes, because peace of mind should never have an off-season.

05What if the pests come back?
