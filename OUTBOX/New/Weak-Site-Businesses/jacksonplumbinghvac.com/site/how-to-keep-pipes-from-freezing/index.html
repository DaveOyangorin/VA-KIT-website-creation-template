How to keep pipes from freezing (and what to do if they do)

From the blog · Oct 23, 2026

Contact us 385-595-5700

Keep the heat at 55°F or above, even when you’re away. Insulate pipes in attics, crawl spaces and along exterior walls, shut off and insulate outdoor faucets that aren’t frost-proof, and open sink cabinets on the coldest nights. Know where your main water shutoff is. If a pipe freezes, shut off the water, open the faucet and warm the pipe gently from the faucet end. Never use an open flame.

Utah winters bring nights well below freezing, and a frozen pipe is one of the most expensive surprises a house can deliver. As Utah State University Extension puts it, as water freezes it expands, and that can crack or split pipes. The damage often isn’t obvious until the ice thaws and water starts running where it shouldn’t. Here’s what the Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety (IBHS), USU Extension and the National Weather Service recommend.

Which pipes freeze first

IBHS points to pipes in hard-to-reach places: attics, crawl spaces and along exterior-facing walls. USU Extension adds that any water line in an area where temperatures commonly drop below freezing should be protected, and reminds homeowners that hot water lines freeze too.

Outdoor faucets are the other weak point. IBHS advises shutting off and insulating the valves if you don’t have frost-proof outdoor faucets, and notes that homes built before 2010 typically don’t.

Before the cold arrives

Find your main water shutoff

Every source agrees on this one. IBHS: know where your home’s water shut-off valve is located and how to operate it. USU Extension goes further: teach adults and older children where it is and how to shut the water off, because doing so quickly in the event of a leak can greatly reduce the damage. In many homes it’s where the water line enters the house, often in a basement, utility room or near the water heater.

Insulate exposed pipes

IBHS recommends wrapping pipes with pipe insulation tubes, wrap or even tape in unheated or minimally heated areas. USU Extension describes pre-formed insulation sleeves and strips of foil-faced fiberglass, with two cautions: the insulation must completely cover the exposed pipes, fittings and fixtures, and you should make sure there are no leaks that will soak it first.

Insulation slows heat loss; it doesn’t create heat. USU Extension notes that insulation will not protect pipes from prolonged freezing temperatures unless there is some source of heat.

Heat cable as a last resort

Electrical heat cables designed for pipes can be installed, according to USU Extension, but it says this shouldn’t be your first choice, and points out that if the electricity is off for several hours, unheated pipes can freeze anyway.

Outdoor faucets and hoses

Disconnect garden hoses, then shut off and insulate outdoor faucets that aren’t frost-proof, as IBHS advises.

During a cold snap

  • Keep the heat on. IBHS advises setting the thermostat to at least 55°F, even when no one is home. The National Weather Service says not to turn the heat down too much when you go away, and to leave it at 55 degrees or more.
  • Open sink cabinets. The National Weather Service suggests opening kitchen and bathroom sink cabinets so warmer air can circulate around the plumbing, especially on outside walls.
  • Let a faucet drip. The National Weather Service says a slow drip keeps unheated pipes from freezing, and keeping the faucet open relieves pressure that builds between the faucet and an ice blockage. USU Extension notes this wastes water and should be used when more permanent methods aren’t possible.

Going away for winter

If you’ll be away from home for longer than a week and subfreezing temperatures are ongoing or forecast, IBHS suggests shutting off the water by closing the main valve slowly. USU Extension’s advice for an extended absence is to turn off the water, shut down the water heater and drain water from the plumbing system.

If a pipe freezes

Signs include a faucet that won’t run or only trickles on a very cold morning. USU Extension’s steps:

  1. Shut off the main water supply first, then open the faucet on the frozen line.
  2. Start at the faucet and work back toward the frozen section, raising the pipe’s temperature gradually so the ice melts. Heated gradually, the trapped water won’t boil and plastic or rubber faucet parts won’t melt.
  3. Use gentle heat: a space heater, heat lamp, heat tape, hair dryer or towels soaked in hot water.
  4. Be ready for water. If a leak is possible, open other faucets to drain the system, and keep buckets nearby.
  5. If a hot water line or the main line freezes, it’s often necessary to turn off the water heater.

What not to do: don’t use an open flame to thaw frozen lines, because of the fire hazard, and don’t use boiling water, which can cause burns. Be cautious using electrical appliances in wet locations.

And if the pipe is hidden in a wall, you can’t find the frozen section, or it has already split, USU Extension’s advice is simple: a plumber may be your best contact.

Common questions

01What temperature should I keep my house at to stop pipes freezing?

IBHS and the National Weather Service both advise at least 55°F, even when no one is home. A Utah State University Extension article suggests leaving the furnace on near 50 degrees; 55 is the more protective number.

02Should I let faucets drip?

The National Weather Service says a slow drip keeps unheated pipes from freezing and relieves pressure that builds between the faucet and an ice blockage. USU Extension notes it wastes water, so treat it as a cold-snap measure, not a permanent fix.

03How do I thaw a frozen pipe?

USU Extension: shut off the main supply first, open the faucet on the frozen line, and warm the pipe gradually starting at the faucet, using a hair dryer, heat lamp, space heater or towels soaked in hot water. Never use an open flame, and don’t use boiling water.

04Should I turn off the water when I travel?

IBHS says that if you’ll be away longer than a week with subfreezing temperatures ongoing or forecast, consider shutting off the water by closing the main valve slowly. USU Extension adds draining the plumbing and shutting down the water heater.
