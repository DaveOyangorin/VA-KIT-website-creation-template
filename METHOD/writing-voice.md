# Novarte AI — Writing Voice

Adapted from Novarte's content-writing rules. Use it for every word a customer, a client, or a
third party will read: page copy, meta descriptions, GBP posts, listing descriptions, outreach
emails, and GHL campaigns.

---

## The voice in one line

A smart local expert explaining something to a busy business owner over coffee.
Direct. Specific. Warm but not cheesy. Never salesy hype.

---

## Rule 0 — Truth first (this beats every rule below)

Only use facts from the source file: real numbers, real services, real places, real people.
**Never invent** a story, a customer conversation, a statistic, a review, an award, or a
"we've helped 500 clients". If the copy feels flat without a fact you don't have, write
`[[NEEDS: a real example of …]]` and ask.

---

## How it sounds

1. **Mix sentence lengths.** Short. Then a longer one that explains why it matters and
   lands on something concrete. Then short again. Flat, same-length sentences sound like a
   robot.
2. **Short paragraphs.** 1–3 sentences. Never more than 4.
3. **Contractions always.** "don't", "you're", "it's", "we'll".
4. **Be specific.** Not "fast service" → "same-week appointments". Not "many areas" → name
   the cities. Not "years of experience" → the real number, if the source has it.
5. **Start strong.** The first sentence answers the reader's question or names their
   problem. Never warm up.
6. **One idea, one ask.** Each post, email, or section has one point and one call to action.
7. **Specific headings.** "What a First Visit Actually Looks Like" — not "Our Process".
   Never "Introduction" or "Conclusion".
8. **Vary how sentences start.** Never 3 in a row with the same word. Never 2 in a row with
   "The" or "This".
9. **Lists only when they help.** Max one list per section, max 5–6 items, with a lead-in
   sentence.
10. **End clean.** Don't summarize what they just read. Give the one next step.
11. **Local and dated.** Name the city, the neighborhood, the season, the month when it's real.

## Banned phrases (never use)

**Openers:** In today's digital landscape · In the ever-evolving world of · In today's
fast-paced world · In an era where · As we navigate · It goes without saying · Needless to
say · Without further ado · Let's dive in · Let's explore

**Transitions:** Furthermore · Moreover · Additionally · In conclusion · To summarize ·
In summary · Lastly · First and foremost · Last but not least · Having said that · With that
being said · That being said · It is worth noting that · It is important to note that

**Buzzwords:** deep dive · delve into · unlock the power of · journey (as a metaphor) ·
game-changer · cutting-edge · at the end of the day · leverage (as a verb) · holistic ·
robust · seamless · streamline · empower · elevate · transform · revolutionize · innovative ·
dynamic · synergy · best practices (without saying which) · multifaceted · paramount ·
pivotal · crucial · comprehensive (without specifics) · utilize (say "use") · facilitate
(say "help") · in order to (say "to") · due to the fact that (say "because")

**Vague claims:** studies show · experts say · many businesses · some companies ·
top-notch · world-class · second to none · #1 (unless the source proves it)

## Patterns that scream "AI wrote this" (avoid)

- Always exactly three examples. Use two, or four, or one good one.
- Every bullet the same length and shape.
- Every section ending with a summary sentence.
- "Here are 5 ways to…" followed by exactly five bullets.
- Perfectly balanced "on one hand / on the other hand" with no opinion.

## Per format

| Format | Length | Must have |
|---|---|---|
| Title tag | 50–60 chars | main keyword first, brand last if it fits |
| Meta description | 150–160 chars | real reason to click + action close |
| Page intro | 2–3 sentences | answers the page's main question directly |
| FAQ answer | 40–60 words | direct answer in the first sentence |
| GBP post | 150–300 chars (max 1,500) | local hook + one useful point + one CTA |
| GBP description | ≤ 750 chars | who, what, where; no URLs, no promo words |
| Listing description | 50 / 150 / 250 / 750 chars | same facts in every length |
| Outreach email | < 150 words | the real reason they'd link + one clear ask |
| Marketing email | 80–180 words | one idea, one CTA, sounds like a person |

## Healthcare, legal, and financial clients

Never promise results ("cure", "guaranteed", "pain-free", "win your case"). Use "may help",
"many patients", only when the source supports it. Name the real licensed professional when
the source gives it.
