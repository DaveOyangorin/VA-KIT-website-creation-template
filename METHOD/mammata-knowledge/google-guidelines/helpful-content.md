# Creating Helpful, Reliable, People-First Content

- **Source:** https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content
- **Type:** Official Google documentation
- **Published / Last updated:** 2025-12-10 UTC
- **Scraped by FRAVATA:** 2026-06-01

## What it says
Google asks creators to self-assess against several question sets.

**Content and quality self-assessment — does the content:**
- Provide original information, reporting, research, or analysis?
- Offer substantial, complete, or comprehensive coverage of the topic?
- Provide insightful analysis beyond the obvious?
- Add substantial value vs. merely repackaging other sources?
- Have descriptive, helpful titles/headings that don't exaggerate or shock (no clickbait)?
- Read like something you'd bookmark, share, or recommend?
- Be something you'd expect in / referenced by a printed publication?
- Provide substantial value vs. other pages in search results?
- Be free of spelling/stylistic errors?
- Be produced well, not sloppy or hastily?
- Avoid mass production / outsourcing across many sites with little attention to detail?

**Expertise self-assessment — does the content:**
- Present information trustworthily, with clear sourcing, evidence of expertise, author/site background?
- Come from a site/author broadly recognized as authoritative on the topic?
- Be created by an expert or enthusiast who demonstrably knows the topic well?
- Contain no easily-verified factual errors?

**The "Who, How, and Why" framework:**
- **Who** created the content — make authorship clear; provide bylines and author background where readers expect it.
- **How** the content was created — disclose use of automation/AI and explain why it was useful (transparency, not a blanket ban on AI).
- **Why** the content exists — it should be primarily to help people, not to attract search traffic via manipulation.

**E-E-A-T:** Google's systems prioritize Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness — **Trust is the most important member of the family.** Especially critical for **YMYL (Your Money or Your Life)** topics affecting health, finances, safety, or societal welfare.

**Avoid search-engine-first content (red flags):** content made mainly for engines; chasing many topics hoping some rank; heavy automation across many topics; summaries with no added value; writing about trending topics with no real audience need; content that makes readers search again for a better answer; hitting an arbitrary word count; entering a niche with no real expertise just for traffic; promising answers about unconfirmed dates/events; faking freshness by changing dates without substantive updates; aggressive add/remove churn for perceived freshness.
