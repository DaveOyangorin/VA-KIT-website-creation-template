## What Makes a Great Agency?

There are many small things that make up a great agency. Results, follow through, follow up, personal attention, birthday cards, and so on.

Ironically, if the three foundational bases are covered and dialed in, you have a good agency 100% of the time. Without the need for tweaks and hacks.

What I want to teach you in this module, is how to get the foundation seamless, so that delivery becomes effortless and the tweaks become superfluous.

Imagine how satisfying it would be if your agency delivered a solid 8 to all clients without exception?

How fulfilling would it be if you wouldn't have to worry about churn, because churn was naturally low, because your clients feel like they are served by a system that produces results like clockwork?

Imagine how easy it would feel if you didn't feel like having to overdeliver by 200%, overpromising in client meetings, keeping it all in your head, because you know you are providing solid results consistently no matter what.

I can only imagine. Even with these systems in place, we are still struggling to run the perfect agency, but it sure does help a lot.

## Why Listen To Me?

🎥 [Loom](https://www.loom.com/share/f039efadc6f04c94ad3fd85f6592dca6) · 13:38

Before founding Rank Expand with Jesse, I was Head of SEO of a 20-person SEO firm (one of the biggest in The Netherlands).

My assignment was to lower churn to less than 7% annually and to scale revenue beyond $2M ARR. I was responsible for roughly 100 clients, with some of the biggest brands in The Netherlands and the US too. I actively trained and coached the consultants there, as well as improving daily operations through SOPs, software, and continuous feedback.

During my 16 months there, I've tried almost everything. Most things don't work. Not because they are not effective, but because they are effectively solving a problem that doesn't exist in the real world.

As Elon Musk says: the worst case is to optimize something that shouldn't exist in the first place.

So we need to separate signal from noise.

## 

The basic mechanics of an agency are pretty simple (but not easy).

Doing the basics well is actually very hard.

One of my initiatives was the Operational Excellence Task Force. My aim was to find a small group of consultants that were willing to implement my recommendations 1-on-1, and compare their results to the agency's baseline. Unfortunately, the Task Force never saw the light of day. But this foundational question of 'how to run the perfect agency?' has consumed me ever since, and provided a lot of insight.

After that, I had the opportunity to start from scratch and build something from the ground up with Jesse.

Those two iterations have left me with a short list of pretty simple problems, that proved very hard to solve.

**The most common bottlenecks are: **

- relying on hard work and good intentions instead of repeatable process
- having people figure it out without a containing structure
- not operationalizing critical parts of the delivery
- optimizing for the wrong metrics (not client retention)
- not operationalizing comms and expectation management
- not being able to reward risk-taking and experimentation
- not operationalizing prioritization
- not being able to measure client satisfaction
- not being able to show the client the value you provide

Basically, not doing good stuff and showing the client the good stuff you did on a consistent basis and of a consistent quality.

If you are bumping into any of these, I think you will find this module very valuable.

### Ship Deliverables

🎥 [Loom](https://www.loom.com/share/513b971e4e9544c79ee50436dbe600e4) · 10:42

I want to make shipping deliverables a separate, consistent step so the work is visible to the client and we build trust momentum.

I believe the sweet spot is about 21 days, and in practice we can do two parallel deliverables per month depending on how much the client pays, plus one or two check-ins.

Deliverables should be clearly packaged and substantive, like a published page or a conclusion with next steps, not just audits or analysis.

Work that is finished but not shipped loses impact, so I encourage you to send a real delivery email when something is done.

### Leads vs. Prospects

🎥 [Loom](https://www.loom.com/share/12914d3aaf3f4ddbbdb937c05e6c4829) · 5:46

I’m breaking down the difference between leads and prospects so our pipeline reflects reality.

Leads are attention, not intent, and even qualified leads are only about 3 percent ready to buy, so we need the right timing to cross paths with that window.

Prospects are qualified opportunities with intent to buy, a real problem, and buying potential. If you count leads as prospects, you get false confidence, bad forecasting, and wasted follow-up. 

I then walked through our CRM statuses and how accounts become prospects, then active or churned.
