## 1. ▶️ Start Here

Example resource: floridacustompoolbuilders.com

**This is the build-first path to a directory that earns three ways.**

A microsite is one service in one town. A directory is every business in one service across one
state. Same idea, larger scale: "pool builders near me," "pool builders in Florida," "pool builders
in Orlando." One site, a page for the state, a page for every city, a page for every real business.

You build it from a Google Maps listings pull and a Blueprint you hand to Claude Code. There is no
WordPress, no plugin, no database. It is a folder of files on free hosting, and when something
changes you rebuild the folder. Every directory built this way has the same structure and page set,
taken from one master template tested on a real state. The look comes from a website you name.

### Why build one
- **It gives you a reason to call every business in the niche.** "We're connected because you have
  a listing on my directory. Is your listing correct?" That call is the warmest cold call in local
  SEO, and for most members it is worth more than anything the site earns on its own.
- **It boosts a vertical you already own.** Microsites in three cities plus one directory for the
  state means you hold the results page three ways. Your own sites go in the directory like any
  other business. No links between them.
- **It rents its top spots.** Premium listings, best-of placements, featured slots. Small numbers
  each, and they add up because there are a lot of businesses.

### The whole path, in order
1. Pick your vertical and location
2. Buy your domain
3. Pull your listings
4. Enrich your listings
5. Build your directory
6. Put it online
7. Get it indexed
8. Get paid

Your directory is live at Step 6. Everything after that is making it earn.

### What you need before you start
- Claude Code, on the Claude subscription you already have (Setup Microsite Blueprint lesson in
  Microsite Empire covers install).
- DataForSEO connected to Claude Code, for the cheap listings pull (Gear Up lesson: Connect
  DataForSEO to Claude).
- Node.js 20+ on your machine. The Blueprint builds the site with it in Step 5.

### What it costs
About $10 a year for the domain. Listings pull: a few dollars on DataForSEO, or $25–$120 on
Outscraper depending on state size. Hosting free. Indexing about $60 of credits. Under $100 the
first time with DataForSEO, under $200 with Outscraper, then $10 a year.

The heavy part is Claude, not money. Reading every business's website and writing every city page
runs on your Claude plan; a big state can use most of it for a day or two. Claude tells you the
size before it starts; a small state is a fraction of that.

For the same money you could build ten microsites. So build a directory when you already own the
vertical, or when you want the call list. **Not as your first site.**

### How long it takes
The build is a weekend, mostly Claude working. Ranking is the same curve as a microsite: indexed
in month one, Google decides by month three, trusted from month six. One member rebuilt a directory
this way and went from 166 indexed pages to 887 in about 30 days, with the first leads and the
first business asking to be listed in the same window. That is the shape, not a promise.

## 2. Pick Your Vertical and Location

You need two things: a service, and a state.

If you already have microsites, this step is done. Build the directory around the vertical you own,
in the state those sites are in. Pool builders in Orlando, Naples and Miami means a Florida pool
builders directory.

Starting from scratch? Pick the way you would pick a microsite, then widen it to a state.

- **Step 1. Niche Finder GPT** (same as the Microsite Playbook). Look for a boring, high-ticket
  local service where nobody has a favorite company.
- **Step 2. Niche Validator** — tool.rankexpand.com/members, access code `NICHECHECK`. Put in the
  service plus the five biggest cities in the state. You want a service winnable in *most* of them.
- **Step 3. Look at the state search.** Search "[service] in [state]" and "[service] [state]". A mix
  of national directories and a few local businesses = room. A strong state-wide directory already
  owning it = pick another service or state.
- **Step 4. The seven checks.** Passes all seven = a directory. Fails two = a microsite.

### The seven checks for a directory vertical
1. **It is an exact Google Maps category.** The pull is by category, word for word. "Swimming pool
   contractor" is a category; "luxury pool designer" is not. The label under business names in
   Maps is the category.
2. **The category name says the trade.** Broad categories let other trades in. "Fence contractor"
   in one state came back one-third landscapers, handymen and roofers. "Swimming pool contractor,"
   "roofing contractor," "septic system service" come back clean.
3. **People compare before they buy.** A three-quote job: pool, roof, fence, septic, solar,
   remodel. Not a call-the-first-result job (nobody browses locksmiths at midnight).
4. **Four-figure ticket or more, and nobody has a favorite company.** A $2,000 job makes a $97
   listing an easy sale. A state full of franchise names = businesses that don't need you.
5. **Most businesses have a website.** The Blueprint reads each site to fill in services and
   licensing. Phone-only trades (handymen, mobile services) enrich poorly → thin pages.
   Contractors, installers, builders are good.
6. **The state has the right number of them.** A few hundred to a few thousand reviewed businesses.
   Under 200 = few cities clear the three-business floor. Over 5,000 = huge enrichment run.
   Florida pool builders ≈ 2,000. Colorado fence contractors ≈ 400. Texas roofing > 8,000 (too
   big for a first). Ask Claude to size the category with DataForSEO: about a cent, one minute.
7. **There is a real "how to choose" to write.** Licensed trades, permits, warranties, materials,
   seasons. The buyer's guide earns trust, and the calls come from it ("your listing doesn't show
   your license yet").

### Three things to know
- One service, one state, one directory. A second state is a second directory, later.
- Low search volume is fine. City terms add up, and one listing sale pays for the site.
- A wrong pick costs an afternoon. If the clean report shows other trades or half the businesses
  have no website, pick again **before** enrichment — the last cheap moment.

## 3. Buy Your Domain

Cloudflare Registrar: $10.44/yr for .com, at cost, same renewal, WHOIS privacy included. Porkbun
also fine.

- .com only.
- State plus service, exact match: `floridapoolbuilders.com` (or `poolbuildersflorida.com`). Pick
  one, don't buy both.
- State in the name, not a city (a city makes it a microsite).
- Never reuse a microsite domain.
- One directory per domain. A second state is a second domain.

## 4. Pull Your Listings

Resources: DataForSEO, Outscraper, "Connecting the remote DataForSEO MCP server using OAuth".

Two ways; the Blueprint takes either; both end in the same file with the same columns.

- **DataForSEO** — if connected to Claude Code, the Blueprint pulls in its Gate 1. One category,
  one state, a few dollars, under a minute.
- **Outscraper** — no API; run the scrape on outscraper.com and download a CSV (Jesse's video path).

Head-to-head (swimming pool contractors, Florida, same day): DataForSEO had 19 of every 20
businesses Outscraper found (95% of open businesses with reviews, 96% of those with 5+). Same
records: phone identical 99%, website 99.9%, review counts within a few percent. Outscraper's
extras were mostly hotels with pools, state parks and plumbers (thrown out anyway) plus about one
real pool company in twenty. **Cost: $0.89 vs about $24.**

Take: DataForSEO when connected. Outscraper when not, or when you want every last business day one.

### Way 1: DataForSEO
1. Connect DataForSEO to **Claude Code** (not only the claude.ai connector). The Blueprint reads
   the login from that connection, never from a file or chat. New account needs a $50 top-up.
2. Nothing to run. In Gate 1 Claude looks up the category, quotes the exact cost, pulls on yes.
3. The catch: DataForSEO is a database, not a live scrape — it doesn't know closures. The Blueprint
   drops any listing not updated in the last year. Florida: 193 rows dropped (72 closed, 109 gone
   from Maps, 12 still open). About 1 in 100 survivors is still closed; the claim page and the
   correction line catch those.

### Way 2: Outscraper
1. Account at outscraper.com (first 500 records free).
2. Dashboard → Services → Google Maps.
3. **Categories** field: type the exact Maps category and *pick it from the list*. Free text pulls
   anything mentioning the words. Jesse: a typical scrape ≈ $100; the one time he forgot exact
   match it was $317 for 119,000 rows, mostly junk.
4. **Locations**: country, then state. Cities blank.
5. Fields on default (name, address, phone, website, rating, review count, review tags, about,
   hours, category, coordinates, business status, location link = Maps URL used by enrichment).
   **No email or social add-ons** on this pull.
6. Run, download as **CSV** (not Excel). Minutes to a couple of hours (6,245 US dog parks = 2 hrs).
7. Rename to `listings-raw.csv`.

### Emails
No listing page shows an email; they matter only for Step 8 (claim invitation, badge offer,
follow-up). DataForSEO includes web-sourced emails free (about 1 row in 4 in Florida). On
Outscraper, get them **after cleaning**: the Blueprint writes every kept business's domain to one
file; Outscraper's Emails & Contacts scraper takes it at $3/1,000 domains after 500 free (~$5 a
state). Every address gets a domain check and a bounce test before anything goes out.

### Costs
DataForSEO: Florida pools $0.89, Texas roofing ~$3 (quoted before pulling). Outscraper: $3/1,000
rows after 500 free; Florida pools 8,535 rows ≈ $24; big state/common category 20–40k rows =
$60–$120; you keep 5–6% as real businesses.

### When something is off
- Tiny count → wrong category, or location set to a city.
- Huge count (Outscraper) → broad category ("Contractor") or typed words instead of picking.
- Neighboring-state rows (Outscraper) → normal, filtered.
- A known business missing (DataForSEO) → ~1 in 20; add by hand or let the owner claim.
- Claude can't find the category (DataForSEO) → it isn't a Maps category; back to Step 1.

Don't clean the file by hand. That is the next step's job.

## 5. 📍 Get the Directory Blueprint

Resource: **`Directory Blueprint.zip`** (Skool attachment, file_id `3224569c8e6140d087384063e9fdec82`).

A raw scrape is a phone book; this step turns it into a tool. Junk comes out, then every real
business gets attributes and a description, so "pool builders in Tampa" can say which do
fiberglass, which offer financing, which say they're licensed. That is what makes it rank and be
worth using. All inside Claude Code; you approve each step.

1. **Download and unzip.** Folder has `CLAUDE.md`, `README`, `wiki/`, `templates/`, `scripts/`,
   `skills/`, `input/`. Claude creates `output/`.
2. **Give it listings.** Outscraper: `input/listings-raw.csv`. DataForSEO: nothing to add.
3. **Open the folder in Claude Code**, run `claude`, type: `Read CLAUDE.md and run Gate 1.`
4. **Gate 1 — Scope.** Eight questions, one at a time: service; state; domain; phone numbers of
   any microsites you own in this vertical; the publisher name that fronts the directory; publisher
   details (website, short bio, photo, contact email); one or two "inspired" websites whose look to
   borrow (any industry; if none, Claude shows seven directories to pick from); listings source.
   On DataForSEO it quotes and pulls on yes. The publisher and inspired site stop it looking like
   every other AI-built site.
5. **Gate 2 — Clean.** Report: rows in, rows out, why, kept rows by Google's label. Fixed rules:
   only your state, only open, at least one review, name or category matches your service, no
   duplicates, and (DataForSEO) nothing not updated in a year. Outscraper keeps 5–6%; DataForSEO
   keeps most. Outscraper 1% or DataForSEO half = service terms wrong; rerun.
   Watch: businesses carrying your category as a *second* label come through (fence pull kept a
   third as landscapers/handymen/roofers). You decide in Build Gate 4, listing by listing or as a
   group; decisions are recorded in a file so reruns keep them.
6. **Gate 3 — Enrich.** Three parts; Claude stops before the expensive one.
   - Attribute list with you: 8–15 things a page can say (pools: inground, fiberglass, gunite, spa,
     remodel, screen enclosure, financing, licensed…), each with the website words that make it
     true. If the Maps category is broader than the service, one attribute records whether the
     business does the core work at all.
   - Reads each business's own website (Maps description is empty on nearly every row). Free, a few
     minutes per state.
   - Enrichment: every attribute = true / false / not enough info, plus a plain 50–200-word
     description. **Never guesses**: true needs words on the site; a checker rejects any answer it
     can't trace. "Not enough info" shows on the page as exactly that. Size quoted first (~4,000
     tokens a listing; 400 businesses < 2M tokens; 2,000 ≈ 8M). One small batch to read first,
     then the rest, city by city, saved as it goes (resumable).

End state: `output/` holds the clean list, the attribute list, the enriched list, and a report per
step. The whole directory in three files; everything else is generated from them.

When something is off: CSV not found (name/location); "no DataForSEO login" (connected in
claude.ai but not Claude Code); clean drops almost everything (use the words businesses actually
use); half the websites unreadable (some is normal; more than half = thin vertical); mostly "not
enough info" (normal for thin businesses — they get a page but stay out of the launch sitemap).

## 6. Build Your Directory

Every Blueprint directory has the same bones: page set, URLs, navigation, section order — the
master template from a real reviewed state build. What changes: the look (colors, type, imagery, a
pattern or two, taken from the inspired site) and the facts (from your data). Claude does not
invent a layout.

- **Gate 4 — Plan** (`Run Gate 4.`): which cities get a page (any city with **at least three real
  businesses**; small far-away towns get a short page of their own), total pages, which go in the
  sitemap at launch vs wait, featured-slot positions, colors and type from the inspired site with
  contrast measured. Ends with a numbered list of your decisions with Claude's recommendation
  (other-trade businesses, sort confirmed first, etc.). Every drop/change goes in one file kept
  across reruns. Say so if a city you know is missing. Approve.
- **Gate 5 — Build** (`Run Gate 5.`): copies the template, fills it, writes non-data copy: first
  a **fact sheet** (every claim about law, licensing, permits, weather with its source, plus what
  the copy may not say), then **500–800 words and 4–6 FAQs per city page**, written about the place
  in relation to the trade — never a list of what businesses say. A checker rejects any number not
  in the data and any sentence repeated across cities; a linter runs on every block. Home-page
  buyer's guide written with the seo-content-engine skill when DataForSEO is connected. Runs the
  build, reports pass. ~18,000 tokens a city, quoted first. Needs Node.
- **Look at it**: `preview-site.command` (Mac) / `preview-site.bat` (Windows) in the site folder.
  Don't open files directly. Check home, one city, one business, the claim page:
  - City page reads as written about that city, not swapped in.
  - Businesses show real numbers: rating, review count, attributes.
  - Nothing says "true" where data said "not enough info."
  - The look came from the named site; the structure is the template on purpose. Tells of the
    generic version: same label above every heading, stock icon rows with empty sentences,
    unreadable buttons.
  - Your own microsites look exactly like every other listing.
- **Fix in the data, not the page.** Wrong business → fix the row, rerun Gate 5. Look off → tell
  Claude, rerun Gate 5. Never hand-edit a generated page.

Why static: a directory changes constantly (claims, sales, closures). On WordPress that's a login,
a plugin and a VA. Here it's one row and one command.

## 7. Put It Online

Resource: Formspree.

1. **Form handler first.** formspree.io free account → New form (named after the directory) →
   copy endpoint. In Claude Code: `Set the claim form endpoint to <endpoint> and run Gate 5.`
   Send a test claim once live (Formspree confirms your email on the first one).
2. **Gate 6** (`Run Gate 6.`): Claude asks you to run `npx wrangler login` (you log in yourself).
3. **Deploy** to Cloudflare Pages via command line (1,000+ files is over the drag-and-drop limit).
   Claude checks live: home, a city, a business, sitemap, 404, no preview-only content shipped.
4. **Custom domain** in Cloudflare project → Custom domains. HTTPS automatic.
5. **Every rebuild** = change data → Gate 5 → Gate 6. Cloudflare keeps old versions.

Cost $0; only the domain bills. Check: open domain, click a city and business, submit the test
claim, open `/sitemap-index.xml`.

Troubleshooting: no styling = deployed project folder not `dist`; city "not found" = build didn't
finish; claim form goes nowhere = endpoint not set before build; domain doesn't load = wait an
hour; live check fails = fix, rebuild, redeploy, don't move on.

## 8. Get It Indexed

Resource: Omega Indexer.

**Launch lean.** Pushing thousands of pages at once gets a few indexed and the rest ignored. The
sitemap at launch = home, state page, every city/town page, the strong business pages. Thin
business pages (**under five reviews, or no description**) are built but `noindex` — they still
show, link and say "claim this listing," and join the index once they earn it. Tested state:
~1,100 in sitemap out of 1,700 built.

1. URL list from `06-launch.md` (strong first) or the sitemap. Only after the live check passed.
   Each URL = one credit. Ask Claude to tier it: home/state/city → strongest listings → rest.
   Tested state: first two tiers 584 URLs ≈ $44 vs ≈ $81 for all.
2. Omega: Dashboard → Campaigns → + Create Campaign → Copy & Paste Links.
3. **Dripfeed 7–10 days.** Never in one day.
4. Launch; leave it alone.
   Pricing: buy a subscription, cancel, credits stay (months). 800 credits = $60 (1,000 URLs ≈ $75).
5. **30-day check.** Two numbers: pages Omega reports indexed, Cloudflare analytics visitors. Both
   moving → `Widen the sitemap.` (next business pages to index: claimed first, then by review
   count) → Gate 5 → upload → new Omega campaign with the new URLs. Repeat monthly while growing.

**No Search Console** by default — a directory next to your microsites should not share a Google
account with them. Omega's report + Cloudflare analytics are enough. (GSC is allowed if you want.)

## 9. Get Paid

Three ways; run all three. Start with the first: works day one, needs nothing built.

### 1. Make the calls
"We're connected because you have a listing on my directory, the largest directory of [service]
in [state]. Is your listing correct?" Then: "It isn't optimized yet. No photos, no links. Want me
to fix that?" Then the listing, the website, the SEO. One member: directories are his #1 source of
SEO clients — the call does it, not the site.

### 2. Handle claims
Every business page: "Is this your business? Claim it." Free. When a claim comes in:
- Verify: real local business, not lead-gen; email domain matches their website; phone matches.
- Call them the same day ("Saw you claimed your listing, welcome") — the warmest lead you'll get.
- `Mark <business> as claimed.` → Gate 5 → upload.
No logins; nobody edits their own listing. Every change goes through you (keeps spam out).

### 3. Sell the spots (all by hand: Stripe Payment Link → they pay → flip the row → rebuild)
- **Premium listing, $97/yr** — their photos, all links, map, hours, and a "Listed on [directory]"
  badge for their own site that links back to their page. Easiest sale.
- **Best-of placement, one time** — every city page is already a "best [service] in [city]" list.
  Pin a business to a top spot, package with a press release. Members sold it at $750–$1,200. Call
  the businesses on the list first to congratulate them; six months later call the ones not on it.
- **Featured slots, monthly** — top card on a city page / a group of cities / the whole state:
  **$29 / $99 / $499 a month** starting points. One business per slot per city.
Setup: Stripe Payment Links (free account) → paste into the Blueprint's featured tiers file →
Gate 5. After a sale, tell Claude who bought what → Gate 5 → upload.

**Later:** sell the leads — once city pages rank, a tracked number or lead form turns a city page
into rank-and-rent (Microsite Playbook Step 4). Not a first-month move.

**Your own microsites** sit in the directory like any other business: same card, same page, no link
between the sites, no badge. The boost is owning the results page three ways, not linking.

Do this: ten calls off the biggest city's page.

## 10. ❓ FAQ

- **My microsites are in it — isn't that a PBN?** No: same card, same page, same **nofollow** link
  as everyone, no link back from them. A directory listing every real business that happens to
  include yours is a directory; one that links only to your sites is a network.
- **Why not WordPress + directory plugin?** Works, but needs hosting, plugin, updates, spam control,
  listing management. One member's launch stalled for months on that. Keep WP if you already run it
  with a team; otherwise start static.
- **$100 is ten microsites — why one directory?** Not as a first site. Build it when you own a
  vertical and want the SERP three ways, or want the call list.
- **DataForSEO instead of Outscraper?** Yes; ~8× cheaper per row; database not fresh scrape (lag);
  API call, not a download button.
- **Stripe, Supabase, logins?** Stripe Payment Links only, only when selling. No accounts on the site.
- **Photos?** None until a business claims or buys and sends its own. Google's photos can't be
  reused; copying from social pages is a copyright problem. A listing with no photo is the pitch.
- **Businesses per city page?** Three. Under that they sit on the nearest city's page.
- **Two states / whole country?** One state per directory. Second state = second domain + build.
- **Is scraping Maps allowed?** Public business info; Outscraper is a paid tool built for it. Two
  rules: publish only what a business publishes about itself, and honor any correction/removal
  request within a week. The contact page says how.
