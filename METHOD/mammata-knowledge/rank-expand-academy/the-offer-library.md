Every lesson in this module is a single sellable deliverable: a specific thing you can pitch to a client today and deliver in a few hours using the AI workflows we've built. They're the building blocks the Upwork Playbook and Microsite Playbook both draw from.

If you're working through one of those paths and you've reached the point where you need to pick what to actually pitch or ship - this is where you come.

## **How this module is organized**

The deliverables are grouped into four sections: **SEO Services, Leads, Web Builds, AI Workflows.** Each section has its own intro lesson that maps the offers inside and suggests where to start.

Each deliverable has its own short lesson with four things:

- **What it is** - plain description of the deliverable
- **Who buys it** - the kind of client most likely to pay for this
- **What to charge** - typical price range, based on what members are actually selling it for
- **How to deliver it** - direct link to the AI workflow inside AI Tools & Workflows that produces it

That's the whole format. No filler.

## **How to use it**

You don't read this module front-to-back. You scan, pick the deliverables you can confidently offer right now, and use them as your pitch material.

Run each workflow once on a real example: your own site, a friend's business, a competitor you've been curious about. Fake examples produce fake portfolios. Real ones give you something to point at. That's your portfolio. Now you can pitch from a position of *"I've done this"* instead of *"I think I can do this."*

### Quick SEO Audit

🎥 [Loom](https://www.loom.com/share/9ac73df5cd134f56a220281c502cd2a8) · 18:07

#### **What it is**

A focused, fast-turnaround SEO audit of a client's website. Surfaces the highest-impact issues: technical, on-page, content gaps, ranking opportunities. Delivers a prioritized action list. Not a 60-page report. A tight executive document with the 5-10 things that actually matter.

The fast turnaround is the value. Most agencies take 2-3 weeks for an audit. You deliver in 48 hours.

#### **Who buys it**

Anyone whose website matters to their business: local service operators, eCommerce founders, agencies who need a second opinion, marketing managers who suspect their current SEO consultant isn't pulling their weight. The phrase you'll hear is *"I just need to know what's working and what isn't."*

#### **What to charge**

**$100-$300 for the audit**. Higher end if the site is large or the niche is competitive. This is often the entry-point offer that opens the door to bigger work: content briefs, monthly retainers, implementation projects.

#### How to deliver it

Use the ⚡ [SEO Research Assistant](https://www.skool.com/rank-expand-academy/classroom/cf75b5d7?md=0bd1eb137f75485aa465213119a71732) in [AI Tools & Workflows](https://www.skool.com/rank-expand-academy/classroom/cf75b5d7?md=60df637bff0847d1ad1bc9e6fb631fad). Run the URL through the audit prompts, validate the output, format as a 3-5 page executive document. Deliver as a PDF with the top 5 issues, why each matters, and the specific actions to fix them. Optional 15-min Loom walkthrough adds perceived value at no extra time cost.

### Schema Audit

🎥 [Loom](https://www.loom.com/share/00bc56e862b645a0863d6f6502f1b35e) · 17:14

#### **What it is**

A complete schema markup audit for a client's website. Identifies missing schema, validates existing markup, surfaces opportunities, and delivers the JSON-LD code ready to drop in. Optional add-on: implement the schema yourself if the client doesn't have a developer.

Most sites are missing schema or have it wrong. Google reads it, AI search reads it, and it's invisible to non-technical buyers - which is exactly why they pay specialists to handle it.

#### **Who buys it**

Local service businesses (where local schema drives map pack visibility), eCommerce stores (product, review, FAQ schema directly impacts SERP appearance), and content sites that want rich snippets. The phrase you'll hear is *"I know I should have schema but I don't know what's missing or how to add it."*

#### **What to charge**

- $150-$500 for audit + implementation plan.
- $500-$1,500 if you implement the markup yourself.
- Recurring add-on: $50-$150/month for schema monitoring on sites that update frequently.

#### **How to deliver it**

Use the ⚡ [Structured Data Expert GPT](https://www.skool.com/rank-expand-academy/classroom/cf75b5d7?md=7687c0857f6544279d0b964fe5f67e7b) in [AI Tools & Workflows](https://www.skool.com/rank-expand-academy/classroom/cf75b5d7?md=60df637bff0847d1ad1bc9e6fb631fad). Feed it the client's URL, get back an audit + the JSON-LD code for missing schema. Deliver as a PDF report with an executive summary, the audit findings, and the code snippets ready to paste. For implementation, work with the client's CMS directly or hand the snippets to their developer with installation instructions.

Deliver a Loom walkthrough alongside the document - clients consistently value the walkthrough over the written report. Optional add-on: rewrite the page yourself as a [Web Build](https://www.skool.com/rank-expand-academy/classroom/8135b167?md=39642b5c3123478aafc548d199b569d5) for an additional $300-$800.

### SEO Content Plan

🎥 [Loom](https://www.loom.com/share/afa45e028c8143b88268a37b40942b8a) · 8:10

#### **What it is**

Three related deliverables, sold individually or as a bundle:

- **Keyword research** - niche or topic, returns a clustered list of target keywords with intent mapping and difficulty estimates
- **SEO content briefs** - per-article briefs with target keywords, outline, search intent, semantic terms, and competitor analysis

The bundle is the highest-value sale. Most clients need all three to actually move on content.

#### **Who buys it**

Content marketers, in-house SEO teams, agencies who outsource content production, founders who want to do their own writing but don't know what to write about. The phrase you'll hear is *"I have a writer but I don't know what they should be writing."*

#### **What to charge**

- Keyword research: $100-$300 per niche.
- Content brief: $50-$150 per article (or $300-$500 for a pack of 5).
- 3-month content plan: $300-$800.
- Bundle (all three): $500-$1,200.
- Recurring: monthly content brief retainer at $300-$800/month for 4-10 briefs.

#### How to deliver it

Use the ⚡ [Keyword Research Workflow](https://www.skool.com/rank-expand-academy/classroom/cf75b5d7?md=f3b53e4fde434317821d9b6c82a651b4), ⚡ [SEO Content Briefing Workflow](https://www.skool.com/rank-expand-academy/classroom/cf75b5d7?md=d7a689169a2b4f10bcec3fe8d9355267), and ⚡ [SEO Content Plan Workflow](https://www.skool.com/rank-expand-academy/classroom/cf75b5d7?md=3893e4c110324c19b6207a4e41556313) in [AI Tools & Workflows](https://www.skool.com/rank-expand-academy/classroom/fc4882c1?md=6ebb61c8fb5647cdbc148f53c02024a1). Deliver as Google Docs (briefs) and Google Sheets (research + plans) so clients can collaborate. Recurring retainers are the highest-margin version of this offer.

🎥 [Loom](https://www.loom.com/share/38fae7b498114f13af90b54273b209c1) · 9:20

The complete local SEO package. This is where you stack everything you've already learned to sell - the audits, the schema work, the content plan - into one done-for-you retainer for a service business.

**Full local SEO audit**: Google Business Profile, local citations, on-page local signals, schema, competitor positioning, with a 60-day action plan. Optional follow-up: monthly optimization rounds where you implement the plan and report on progress.

The audit is the entry sale. The optimization retainer is the real business.

Service businesses (dentists, lawyers, contractors, salons, home services - anything local) where most of their business comes from local search. They know they need to rank in the map pack. They don't know how. The phrase you'll hear is *"We're not showing up when people search [our service + our city]."*

### Raw Lead Lists

#### **What it is**

Targeted B2B lead lists pulled from Google Maps for a specific niche and geography. Each list includes business name, website, phone, address, owner name where available, and qualification signals - does the business have a website, is it updated, are they running ads. Delivered as a CSV ready to drop into a CRM or cold-outreach tool.

You're already running this workflow for your own outreach. Selling the byproduct.

#### **Who buys it**

Local marketing agencies, cold-outreach operators, SDRs who need filtered prospect lists, anyone running B2B outreach campaigns. The phrase you'll hear is *"I need 200 [niche] businesses in [city] with verified contact details."*

#### **What to charge**

$50-$200 per list, depending on size and qualification depth. Bulk pricing: $1-$2 per qualified lead for larger orders (500+). Recurring: monthly lists for cold-outreach agencies at $200-$500/month for a steady feed.

#### **How to deliver it**

Use the **Business Lead Discovery via Google Maps** workflow in AI Tools & Workflows. Run the workflow with the client's niche and geography parameters, filter for qualification signals, deliver as a CSV via email or shared Drive folder. Optional add-on: enrichment (LinkedIn profiles, email verification) for an additional $0.50–$1 per lead.

### Local Lead-Gen Lease Model

🎥 [Loom](https://www.loom.com/share/d0b14eaa66b74369b23abf07024528b3) · 8:53

#### **What it is**

You build and rank a microsite for a specific local niche ("emergency plumber Phoenix," "asbestos removal Denver"). Once it's ranking and generating leads, you lease the site to one local business in that area. They pay you a fixed monthly fee. You keep the asset; they get exclusive access to the leads it produces.

#### **Who buys it**

Local service businesses in lead-hungry niches: plumbers, electricians, roofers, locksmiths, movers, cleaning companies, anyone whose business is feast-or-famine on incoming inquiries. The phrase you'll hear is *"we never know where next month's work is coming from."* They want predictable leads more than they want to learn marketing.

#### **What to charge**

$500-$2,500/month per site or a package of sites, depending on niche and volume. Pricing benchmark: roughly 10-20% of average customer value, multiplied by expected leads per month. A plumber whose average job is $400 and gets 20 leads/month from the site can comfortably pay $800-$1,500/month and still be profitable. Lock in 6-12 month minimum terms to make the build economics work.

#### **How to deliver it**

Build using [The Microsite Playbook](https://www.skool.com/rank-expand-academy/classroom/e93b5251?md=8308276ebac948dcad22277824546969). Rank to first page before approaching businesses. Use the [Outreach at Scale course](https://www.skool.com/rank-expand-academy/classroom/b72c8844?md=f9bf2dc8c9794b27806d3135568d95c3) to find and pitch local operators, or the **⚡ **[Business Lead Discovery via Google Maps workflow](https://www.skool.com/rank-expand-academy/classroom/cf75b5d7?md=cc72a89ba10341879715cd89f952bee5). Lease agreement covers: exclusivity in the geography, forwarding setup (calls + form submissions go to the client), monthly performance reporting, and your right to terminate if they don't pay or don't answer their phone. This is the play Microsite Empire is built to scale.

### Pay-Per-Lead Arrangements

🎥 [Loom](https://www.loom.com/share/66c76ecdd751488aa1f281d8e2422fee) · 9:24

#### **What it is**

Same site, different commercial structure. Instead of a flat monthly fee, you charge the client a fixed amount for each qualified lead the site delivers. $40 per lead, $100 per phone call over 60 seconds, $250 per booked appointment, whatever fits the niche economics. Performance-based pricing, transparent reporting, no fixed commitment.

#### **Who buys it**

Businesses that are skeptical of marketing spend, have been burned by agencies, or are too small to commit to a monthly retainer. Also: businesses that want to *test* you before committing to a lease arrangement. The phrase you'll hear is *"I'll pay for results, not promises."* This is often the entry-point deal that converts into a lease arrangement once they trust the lead quality.

#### **What to charge**

Set the per-lead price at 5-10% of their average customer value. For a $2,500 average customer (e.g. emergency plumbing), $125-$250 per qualified lead is fair. Define *qualified* in writing upfront: phone call over X seconds, completed form with valid contact, booked appointment, whatever's measurable. Otherwise you'll spend half your time arguing about which leads count.

#### **How to deliver it**

Same build as the lease model: rank a microsite, capture leads, forward them. The infrastructure difference is the *tracking layer*: call-tracking numbers, form submissions logged with timestamps, monthly reports with audio recordings or form copies as evidence. Invoice monthly based on actual lead count. The risk for you is you carry the build-out costs without guaranteed revenue. Only viable in niches where you're confident in volume.

### Rev-Share Lead-Gen Partnerships

🎥 [Loom](https://www.loom.com/share/59bf6348147647a6b1a07f71a78c34c6) · 9:50

#### **What it is**

You provide leads at no upfront cost. The client pays you a percentage of the revenue from any deal closed using your leads. 10-20% of the closed deal value, typically. You're effectively a sales partner, not a service provider. Your incentive is fully aligned with theirs.

#### **Who buys it**

High-ticket businesses where the math justifies giving up margin: custom home builders ($25K+ jobs), commercial services, B2B consulting, premium home services. Also businesses that can't or won't pay upfront but have good sales infrastructure. The phrase you'll hear is *"I have plenty of sales capacity, I just need more deals at the top of the funnel."* This is the highest-trust arrangement of the three. You're betting on the client's sales operation, not just their ability to pay.

#### **What to charge**

10-20% of closed deal revenue is the standard band. Go higher (20-25%) for niches where leads are scarce and conversion is high. Lock in a minimum term (12 months typical) and an audit clause: you need to verify revenue, not just take their word for it. Some operators take a small monthly retainer ($250-$600) plus a smaller rev-share (5-10%) as a hybrid. This covers your costs and tests their commitment without leaving all the upside on the table.

#### **How to deliver it**

Same lead-gen infrastructure as the other two models, but the contract work is heavier. Written agreement covering: how revenue is tracked (CRM access, monthly reconciliation, your audit rights), what counts as a lead "from you" (attribution window, typically 90 days), payment terms (within 30 days of client receipt), and termination clauses. This model only works if the client has clean books and you can verify deal flow. Don't enter rev-share with a business whose CRM is a notepad (or only when you absolutely completely trust them).
