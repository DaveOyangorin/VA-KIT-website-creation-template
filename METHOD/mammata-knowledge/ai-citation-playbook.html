01The verdict

Six findings that change what we sell. Everything after this section is the evidence.

Finding 01

A listing is worthless if the directory blocks the AI bot

Yelp is the #1 cited source for "near me" answers in Google AI Overviews — and gets zero citations in ChatGPT. Yelp's robots.txt blocks AI crawlers from /biz/ but never blocked Googlebot. Same listing, opposite result, depending on the engine. Nobody checks this before selling a listing.

Finding 02

The same tactic helps weak pages and hurts strong ones

Adding citations, quotes and statistics gives a rank-5 page +115% AI visibility and costs a rank-1 page −30%. Generic "add stats" advice actively damages our best-performing clients. Advice must be segmented by current rank.

Finding 03

Most agencies block the wrong bot

GPTBot is the training crawler. OAI-SearchBot is the one that decides whether you appear in ChatGPT answers. The 2024 "block the AI scrapers" wave taught thousands of sites to block the harmless one — and some blocked the critical one by accident.

Finding 04

Earned media is 84% of all AI citations. Paid is 0.3%.

Across 25M+ links and three study editions, advertorial and paid placement account for 0.3% of citations. Press releases are under 2%. You cannot buy a citation. You can only earn one or be structurally readable enough to be picked.

Finding 05

Half our standard AEO deliverables are net-negative

A matched controlled test (1,885 pages vs 4,000 controls) found adding schema moved Google AI Overview citations −4.6%. FAQ blocks score −5.74% on absorption. Keyword density scores −8.3%. llms.txt was fetched by zero AI bots across 137,210 domains.

Finding 06

One AI answer proves nothing at all

Reliability of a single response is ≈1%. The brand you are measuring accounts for 0.7% of total variance in AI answers. Any tool or report built on running a prompt once is selling noise. Minimum defensible spec is 5 runs per prompt, 60+ prompts, 3+ engines.

⏱ Time-critical — 15 September 2026

Cloudflare changes its defaults in three weeks, and the change has already broken sites. New domains will block Training and Agent crawlers on ad-displaying pages. The trap: Cloudflare judges multi-purpose crawlers on all their behaviours, so setting "AI Training = Block" also returns 403 to Googlebot, Bingbot and Applebot. That is not theoretical — it was reported and confirmed in August 2026, with Googlebot getting 403s on sitemap fetches. Check every client zone before the 15th. Full detail and the opt-out path in Section 14.

The one-line version

Stop selling submissions. Start selling crawler access audits, rank-segmented content surgery, earned placement on the five directories that are actually readable, and measurement with a stated error bar.

The Yelp paradox — and what it teaches

Yelp's two hostnames combine to 393,048 mentions — Yelp is the single biggest source of local AI answers on Google, ahead of every Google-owned property. Yet a parallel pull found zero Yelp citations across 7,472 restaurant-query mentions in ChatGPT, and yellowpages.com outranked it there at 438.

The explanation is one file. Yelp's robots.txt blocks GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot and friends from /biz/ — while leaving Googlebot alone. Google AI Overviews rides the ordinary Google index, so it reads Yelp fine. ChatGPT cannot see it at all.

The general rule: a directory's value is not its authority or its traffic. It is whether that specific engine's crawler is allowed through the door. Fetch robots.txt before recommending any listing, every time.

Cross-checks against published datasets

  • Profound (680M citations) — ChatGPT: Wikipedia 7.8%, Reddit 1.8%, Forbes 1.1%, G2 1.1%. Perplexity: Reddit 6.6%, YouTube 2.0%, Gartner 1.0%, Yelp 0.8%.
  • Semrush (230K prompts) — documents a hard break: ChatGPT's Reddit reliance fell from ~60% of responses to ~10% around 11 Sep 2025. Any benchmark older than that is stale.
  • Cross-engine overlap is 11%. Only 11% of domains are cited by both ChatGPT and Perplexity. AI Overviews and AI Mode cite the same URL just 13.7% of the time. One directory list cannot serve all engines.

Denominator warning — do not skip

Published "most cited" numbers disagree by 10×+ because they measure different things. "Reddit is 1.8% of ChatGPT citations" and "Reddit appears in 60% of ChatGPT responses" are both true at once. Always state whether you are quoting share of all citations, share of responses containing the domain, or share of top-10 sources. Never average across them.

03The gatekeeper: robots.txt

This is the layer almost every "get listed everywhere" playbook ignores, and it decides whether a listing can be cited at all. Modern robots.txt splits AI bots into two tiers that behave completely differently.

Training bots vs retrieval bots

Training crawlers feed model weights. Retrieval crawlers decide whether you show up in a live answer today. Blocking training while allowing retrieval keeps you citable while withholding your data from the next model. Many sites do exactly this on purpose — and many block the wrong one by accident.

Two silent killers to audit on every new client

1. nosnippet / max-snippet:0. Google's own docs: a page must "be eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet" to appear in AI Overviews. Sites that set these defensively removed themselves from AI Overviews entirely while keeping their blue links. Nobody audits for it.

2. Google-Extended: Disallow. Removes you from Gemini grounding while leaving AI Overviews untouched. Clients who "opted out of AI" in 2024 usually have no idea which half they turned off.

Two more access facts worth money

  • AI crawlers do not run JavaScript. A one-month study across nextjs.org and the Vercel network found no JS execution by OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, ByteDance or Perplexity crawlers. Only AppleBot and Gemini render. A JS-rendered client site is invisible to most of the field.
  • Broken links are eating a third of the crawl budget. Same study: 34.8% of ChatGPT fetches and 34.2% of Claude fetches hit 404s, versus 8.2% for Googlebot. That is an immediately billable audit finding on nearly every site.
  • ChatGPT's retriever strips JSON-LD during HTML→Markdown conversion, ignores meta descriptions, and rejects pages over 4 MB outright with an HTTP 400.
  • Cloudflare's new default from 15 Sep 2026 blocks Training and Agent crawlers on ad-displaying pages. Landmine: multi-purpose crawlers are judged on all their behaviours, so an aggressive Training block can take Googlebot down with it.

Negotiating leverage

These platforms have lost 76–92% of their organic traffic in two years while remaining AI-citation-critical. They know it. That is the moment to negotiate placement terms, not to pay rate card.

Two structural facts that reframe the category

  • G2 bought Capterra, Software Advice and GetApp from Gartner for ~$110M — announced 29 Jan 2026, closed 5 Feb 2026. Any playbook treating these as four competitors is stale. One portal now: app.g2digitalmarkets.com.
  • Second-tier open directories beat first-tier walled ones. RestaurantJi, RestaurantGuru, GoodFirms, TheManifest, Sortlist and ToolRadar get cited because they are crawlable. Authority is not the variable. Access is.

Query type inverts the assumption

Explicit "review" queries pull in a review platform 49% of the time. "Software/tools" queries 39.4%. But "best/top" queries — only 17.1%. Directory listings defend the bottom of the funnel. They do not win the discovery question.

05Twelve hidden levers

Under-exploited because they are counter-intuitive, undocumented, or buried in a policy page nobody reads.

Lever 01 · Access

Bing Webmaster Tools "AI Performance"

The only first-party AI-citation dataset any engine publishes, and it is free. Launched Feb 2026, expanded June 2026 with Intents, Topics, Citation Share and Compare. It even shows the grounding queries Copilot generated internally. Set it up on every client on day one.

Lever 02 · Content

Your H1 and first ~200 characters is your ChatGPT snippet

ChatGPT's own index freezes a snippet per URL at index time — not per query. Hard cap 202 characters, contains the H1 in 8 of 10 cases. Meta descriptions are ignored entirely. The first image's alt text eats ~50 characters of that budget.

Lever 03 · Video

YouTube views and subscribers are ~zero-correlated with citation

Correlation of views to citation: r ≈ −0.03. Subscribers: −0.03. Meanwhile 40.8% of AI-cited videos have under 1,000 views and 35% of cited channels have under 10k subs. What weakly predicts it: description length (0.31) and recency (0.3). Long-form is 94% of citations. LLMs read transcripts, not audio — an indexed transcript is the necessary condition.

Lever 04 · Retrieval

Claude's backend is almost certainly Brave

Brave joined Anthropic's subprocessor list Mar 2025; result-overlap testing found 86.7% match with Brave's top organic results (p<0.0001), implying little re-ranking. Brave's index is far cheaper to penetrate than Google's. Never officially confirmed — treat as a strong working hypothesis.

Lever 05 · Reddit

Cited Reddit threads are old, unpopular and comparison-shaped

80% of AI-cited Reddit posts have under 20 upvotes. 70% have under 20 comments. Average age ~900 days. Q&A threads are over half of all Reddit citations. Median cited post is ~80 words. Virality is irrelevant. You cannot buy this — and Reddit demonstrated in court that it plants honeypots to catch scrapers.

Lever 06 · Claude

Keep your quotable claim inside 150 contiguous characters

Claude's cited_text is hard-capped at 150 characters. A claim that cannot survive inside one contiguous 150-character span cannot be quoted cleanly.

Lever 07 · Grok

X posts are direct index insertion

Grok's x_search reads X natively — no crawl, no robots.txt, no indexation lag. It is the only surface where publishing is indexing. Also the priority engine for reputation/risk testing, because of the social corpus.

Lever 08 · Gemini

The API hands you the fan-out queries for free

Gemini's API exposes google_search_call.queries — the actual sub-queries the model generated to decompose a commercial question. Free reconnaissance on how an engine breaks down your client's category. Nobody mines this.

Lever 09 · Entity

Wikidata external-identifier seeding

Wikidata notability is met by "an entity describable with serious and publicly available references" — no Wikipedia article needed. Cite an SEC filing, Crunchbase listing, OpenCorporates registration or GLEIF LEI, attach the matching ID properties (P2088 Crunchbase, P4264 LinkedIn, P1320 OpenCorporates), and the Q-node works as an entity anchor immediately. Free, instant, contestable.

Lever 10 · Reporting

"Cited" and "mentioned" are different KPIs that diverge 62% of the time

61.7% of appearances are "ghost citations" — the site is cited as a source but the brand is never named. ChatGPT: 87% citation rate but only 20.7% mention rate. Gemini is inverted: 21.4% citation, 83.7% mention. Selling "citations" when the client wants to be named is a mis-sold deliverable most of the time.

Lever 11 · Query shape

Short conversational queries produce 30–50× more brand mentions

…than long structured prompts. And comparative queries produce 2.4× more mentions than informational ones. This shapes both what content to build and what prompts to test.

Lever 12 · Commerce

OpenAI's product feed has a literal visibility boolean

is_eligible_search — a flag in OpenAI's merchant product feed spec controlling ChatGPT search visibility for a catalog. Approved partners only; apply at chatgpt.com/merchants. Also new: OAI-AdsBot. ChatGPT now has a paid surface alongside organic citation.

06The rank-segmented rule

The single most commercially useful finding in the literature, and it is buried in Table 2 of a paper everyone only quotes Table 1 from.

The KDD 2024 GEO study ran 10,000 queries with controlled edits. Table 1 is famous — quotations and statistics lift visibility by up to +41%, keyword stuffing costs −8.3%. Table 2 is the one nobody quotes:

How this becomes a service

Rank 1–2 pages: leave them alone. Aggressive citation and statistics injection costs them 20–30% visibility. Protect, don't optimize.

Rank 3–5 pages: this is the whole opportunity. Same edits, up to +115%. Every content hour should go here.

Best combination: Fluency Optimization + Statistics Addition, more than 5.5% above any single method. Cite Sources is weak alone but averages +31.4% in combination — sequence it second, never first.

Three rules that fall straight out of that table

1. An old visible date is worse than no date at all. Either keep dates current or remove them.

2. Concrete facts beat prose polish. Price and specs are near-decisive. Pure formatting is statistically null.

3. Hedged writing loses by up to 754×. "May help" and "can sometimes" are costing citations.

Structure works — architecturally, not cosmetically

A separate study (200 articles, 377 real queries, 6 engines) found restructuring lifts citation rate +17.3%, p<0.001. The ablation is the useful part:

  • Macro-structure — 44.9% of the effect. Heading hierarchy, logical progression, internal linking, reference distribution.
  • Meso-structure — 39.7%. Paragraph-length variance, per-chunk topic coherence, content-density distribution.
  • Micro-structure — 15.4%. Emphasis markers, keyword placement, reading ease.

This reconciles the apparent contradiction with the null on formatting: retrievability is architectural. Prettiness is the 15.4% slice.

Evidence-genre uplift (21,143 citations, 18,151 pages)

Mean influence uplift when the genre is present: Code +76.9% · Numbers/statistics +61.6% · Definitions +57.3% · Comparisons +55.3% · How-to procedures +41.2% · Q&A formatting −5.7%. Top-quartile pages carry 11.4× the word count and 12.5× the headings of bottom-quartile pages.

07The 157-question matrix

Fill the placeholders once per client. Run the set on every engine. This is how you prove any of the above worked.

Define once in a variables.yaml per client: {brand} {domain} {service} {service_2} {category} {industry} {city} {region} {neighborhood} {competitor} {competitor_b} {problem} {persona} {budget} {urgency} {qualifier} {year}

Write prompts the way real people actually type

Two-thirds of real AI prompts are 15 words or fewer; the median is about 8. 60% are phrased as questions. 24.5% contain the word "best". 28% mention price. 32% include a personal attribute. Target library mix: ~30% short keyword-shaped, ~70% conversational. Do not fill the library with 60-word synthetic prompts no human types.

Where to spend measurement budget

Variance reduction per additional query, ranked. Repeats are dead last.

  1. More languages — 6.16 × 10⁻⁵ per query
  2. More paraphrases — 5.00 × 10⁻⁵
  3. More engines — 3.33 × 10⁻⁵
  4. More repeats — 1.19 × 10⁻⁵

A repeat past the fifth reduces relative-error variance by 0.0003. Going from 1 to 20 repeats moves reliability only 0.347 → 0.365. Fix reps at 5, forever. Every dollar past that buys more prompts, more engines, more locales.

What to report

  • Prompt Coverage (the "3 of 5" rule) — the share of prompts where the brand appears in at least 3 of 5 runs. This is the number to put in front of a client. It answers "does this question reliably surface us?" and it collapses the largest variance component.
  • Recommendation Rate — does the model actively advise hiring them. Use this, not sentiment. 91.9% of sentiment scores come back exactly neutral, making sentiment a near-useless outcome.
  • Share of Voice, position-weighted, restricted to competitors appearing in ≥10% of runs. Never count one-off mentions.
  • Citation Rate, split three ways — owned (expect ~3%), earned third-party editorial (this is where the volume is: 59% of page-level citations), and platform (YouTube, Reddit, directories, GBP).
  • Bootstrap confidence intervals, 10,000 resamples, resampled at the prompt level. A composite without a CI is exactly the failure mode the research condemns.

Controls that make the dataset defensible

  • Canary prompt — one branded control (what is {brand}) that must return a mention. Canary fails → discard the whole cycle, the config broke.
  • Holdout brands — two non-clients tracked alongside. If everyone moved together, the engine changed, not your SEO. Do not bill that as a win.
  • All engines inside a 6-hour window. Cross-day comparison inside a cycle is invalid.
  • Randomize prompt order per rep. Space reps ≥5 minutes apart.
  • Leave temperature at engine default. You are measuring the product, not the model.

If an AI says something false about a client

The landscape just changed and most agencies have not caught up.

  • Germany, 28 May 2026 (LG München I): the first liability holding anywhere. The court held AI Overviews constitute original speech by Google, not display of third-party content, and enjoined repetition. Fines up to €250,000 per violation.
  • United States: much harder. Walters v. OpenAI granted summary judgment for OpenAI in May 2025, partly because hallucination disclaimers meant a reasonable reader could not take output as actual fact. The disclaimer defence substantially insulates providers in US courts.
  • Providers largely cannot correct output. OpenAI has stated it cannot correct inaccurate information — only filter or block a name, which suppresses all information about that subject. Suppression is not correction, and these channels are weak-to-absent for companies.

Recourse ladder

Fix the underlying corpus first — thin authoritative content is usually why the negative source wins. Preserve evidence with model, version, prompt and timestamp. Send a written correction demand before litigating. Then litigate — hard in the US, meaningfully easier in the EU. Never respond by seeding reviews or injecting prompts. That converts a reputation problem into an FTC problem.

And the honesty clause

GEO is zero-sum and the window is closing. A benchmark study found most current tactics "not only largely ineffective but also frequently have a negative impact on document ranking," and that gains shrink as adoption rises — "a congested and zero-sum nature." A separate scan of 10,095 live pages found 8.9% are already GEO-optimized, rising to 16.4% among pages modified in 2026 — and that GEO-optimized content is now detectable at F1 0.944. Assume the engines can classify it.

11The 90-day run sheet

The order matters. Access before content, content before measurement, measurement before any claim.

Week 1 — Access audit (do this before anything else)

  1. Fetch and read the client's robots.txt. Confirm OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Googlebot, bingbot and all three *-User bots are allowed.
  2. Grep the site for nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet. Any of these kills AI Overview eligibility.
  3. Check for Google-Extended: Disallow. It silently kills Gemini grounding.
  4. Check the Cloudflare / WAF bot rules. From 15 Sep 2026 new defaults may block more than intended, and multi-purpose crawlers are judged on all their behaviours.
  5. Measure the 404 rate for AI crawlers in server logs. Expect ~35% and bill the fix.
  6. Test whether key content renders without JavaScript. Most AI crawlers do not run it.
  7. Check page weight — ChatGPT rejects pages over 4 MB outright.
  8. Set up Bing Webmaster Tools and enable AI Performance. Free, first-party, and the only real citation data any engine gives you.
  9. Set up IndexNow. Free. Host the key file at the domain root, wire the ping into the publish flow. Gets new pages into Bing's index in a day or two instead of weeks — which is the candidate pool Copilot draws from. See Section 12 for exact mechanics.

Week 2 — Entity foundation

  1. Create or claim the Wikidata item. Seed with external identifiers (Crunchbase P2088, LinkedIn P4264, OpenCorporates P1320). Disclose paid editing on your user page.
  2. Claim Crunchbase. It doubles as Wikidata notability evidence.
  3. Add Organization schema with consistent sameAs links on the client's own domain. For Knowledge Graph entity resolution — not as an AI citation lever.
  4. Audit NAP consistency across Google Business Profile, Bing Places (enable the auto-sync toggle), Apple Business Connect (configure Showcases), BBB free listing, Yellow Pages.

Week 3 — Baseline measurement, before you change anything

  1. Fill the placeholder dictionary. Derive competitors from what the models actually return, not from what the client thinks.
  2. Run the Standard tier: 60 prompts × 2 paraphrases × 6 engines × 5 reps.
  3. Capture retrieved-vs-cited for every run. Flag every case where the client domain was retrieved but not cited.
  4. Run the full N-series risk prompts on Grok first. Escalate anything factual-sounding within 24 hours.
  5. Record bootstrap CIs. This is the baseline every later claim is measured against.

Weeks 4–8 — Content surgery, rank-segmented

  1. Pull current SERP rank for every money page. Split into 1–2 (protect) and 3–5+ (rebuild).
  2. Rank 3–5 pages only: add statistics with dates, direct quotes, price, specs. Sequence fluency first, citations second.
  3. Replace hedged phrasing with confident phrasing throughout.
  4. Fix dates: current, or removed. An old visible date is worse than none.
  5. Restructure at the macro level — heading hierarchy, logical progression, internal linking. That is 45% of the structural effect; cosmetics are 15%.
  6. Remove FAQ blocks and FAQ schema from priority pages. Both measure net-negative.
  7. Rewrite every H1 and opening 200 characters — that is literally the ChatGPT snippet.

Weeks 6–12 — Earned surfaces, routed by vertical

  • B2B software: G2 Digital Markets free listing (Capterra is the only directory that lets training bots through). Drive to 10 reviews for Grid inclusion. Target award and comparison pages, not just the profile.
  • Agencies: Clutch Verified at $499/yr — needs 3 collected reviews via analyst phone interview. Target city×service programmatic grids.
  • Consumer / ecommerce: Trustpilot. The lever is responding to reviews, not volume. Link the singular /review/{domain} path, which is crawlable, never /reviews/, which is not.
  • Everyone: YouTube with a proper indexed transcript. Long-form. Description length matters more than views — and views correlate at r ≈ −0.03.
  • Everyone: earned media. It is 84% of all AI citations; paid is 0.3%. Cited releases carry 2× the statistics and 2.5× the bullet points of uncited ones.
  • Reddit: rules-compliant participation only, 90/10 at most, affiliation disclosed. Position it to the client as high-yield and high-volatility, not a durable asset — Reddit is currently litigating against two AI labs and blocks every crawler in its public robots.txt.

Week 13 — Re-measure and report

  1. Re-run the identical Standard panel. Same 60 prompts, same paraphrases, same engines.
  2. Test with McNemar's paired test, not an unpaired comparison.
  3. Report only changes that clear the minimum detectable threshold for the tier and have non-overlapping CIs and persist for two cycles and did not move the holdout brands.
  4. Everything else is labelled "directional, not yet significant" with the threshold printed beside it.

Section 09 says schema does not raise AI citations. That is true and stays true. This section is the other half: schema still does real work for rich results and entity resolution, and knowing which types are alive in 2026 is billable on its own.

Where the evidence actually landed

  • Google, in writing: "You don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup to appear in these features. There's also no special schema.org structured data that you need to add."

Never put a fact only in markup

A controlled test across 5 engines and 8 injection scenarios found JSON-LD-only content was extracted by zero engines — even when the value appeared nowhere else on the page. Hidden microdata and hidden RDFa also scored zero. Where visible HTML and JSON-LD disagreed, every engine trusted the visible HTML. A separate test found the JSON-LD block does survive into the model's context as raw text — but as prose, not as a parsed graph.

Both studies converge on the same rule, and it matches Google's own policy ("don't mark up content that is not visible to readers"): markup is a duplicate of visible content, never a substitute for it.

Two more format facts

  • Stay on JSON-LD. There is no positive evidence that any AI engine parses microdata or RDFa. Google Search parses all three, so the format choice matters for Google and is irrelevant for AI.
  • Client-side-injected schema was missed by every engine tested. If your CMS or tag manager writes the JSON-LD after page load, it does not exist as far as AI is concerned.

The +30% study, read correctly

A controlled RAG experiment across four domains found JSON-LD markup alone gave "only modest improvements" (effect size d = 0.18) — but a full entity hub page with agent instructions, breadcrumbs and interlinking produced +29.6% to +29.8%. That result gets quoted as proof schema works. It is not. It is a content-architecture finding wearing a markup costume. Build the entity hub; the markup is the smallest part of it.

14The firewall audit

A repeatable procedure for the single most common invisible failure: a WAF, CDN or host quietly refusing AI crawlers while the client insists nothing is blocked. Run this on every new account.

The deadline, in full

Announced 1 July 2026, effective 15 September 2026. Cloudflare, verbatim: "New domains onboarding to Cloudflare receive updated defaults: Bots classified as Training or as Agent are blocked on pages that display ads, while Search remains allowed."

And the part that breaks sites, also verbatim: "Multi-purpose crawlers that combine Search and Training will be affected by the new defaults to block Training." Cloudflare names them — Googlebot, Applebot and BingBot. Cloudflare Radar's own May 2026 figure is that 35.7% of AI crawler traffic comes from mixed-use crawlers.

Opt-out path: dash.cloudflare.com/?to=/:account/:zone/security/settings. The legacy "Block AI bots" toggle deprecates the same day. Scope caveat: Cloudflare's primary sources say new domains; aggregators claim it also hits existing free zones. Check per-zone rather than assuming either way.

Documented accidents — the symptom patterns to recognise

  • August 2026, confirmed and current. A site owner set AI Training = Block. Googlebot and Bingbot immediately began receiving HTTP 403 on sitemap fetches. Cloudflare's own dashboard showed them as blocked, not spoofed — ruling out impersonation. Reversed instantly on disabling. Google's John Mueller asked the reporter to DM him so Google could investigate.
  • Bot Fight Mode blocking Googlebot — recurring since 2021. One reported case: 75% organic traffic drop. Bot Fight Mode runs outside Cloudflare's rules engine, so it cannot be customised and has no verified-bot exemption. Cloudflare's own docs warn it may challenge API and mobile traffic.
  • Cloudflare vs Perplexity, Aug 2025. Cloudflare ran honeypot domains — newly registered, unindexed, Disallow: / — and Perplexity summarised them anyway, using an undeclared Chrome-on-macOS user agent rotating across IPs outside its published ranges, at 3–6M requests/day. Cloudflare delisted Perplexity as a verified bot. This case matters for your audit: your spoofed test request looks exactly like Perplexity's stealth crawler, which is why the next box exists.

A spoofed user agent from your office IP is an impersonator. A 403 on that probe proves nothing by itself. Run a differential: same IP, same minute, same URL, browser UA vs bot UA.

The procedure

Phase 1 — no access needed, 5 minutes. Identify the edge from response headers (server, cf-ray, x-iinfo, x-sucuri-id, Netlify-Agent-Category). Fetch robots.txt and grep for the ~20 AI tokens plus any Content-Signal: lines — this is the only IP-independent test, and the only way to check Google-Extended and Applebot-Extended. Then run the differential probe with real user-agent strings, at least a second apart.

Phase 2 — client dashboard, 15 minutes. Cloudflare → AI Crawl Control → Crawlers: look specifically for Googlebot with a non-zero blocked count — that is the August 2026 mixed-crawler bug, diagnosable on one screen. Check Security → Settings for Bot Fight Mode, Super Bot Fight Mode, AI bot policies and managed robots.txt. Check the 15 September opt-out. Then Search Console → Crawl Stats → Host status, and Page Indexing for "Blocked due to access forbidden (403)".

Phase 3 — logs, definitive. Pull AI-bot hits by status code. Then verify every claimed bot IP against the vendor's published list, to separate a genuine bot being blocked (a real problem) from a spoofer being blocked (correct behaviour). This is the step that turns an opinion into a finding.

15Sitemaps, canonical, freshness

Three questions every client asks. Two have real answers. One is built almost entirely on a number that does not exist.

Do AI crawlers read your sitemap?

Only Bing says so. Every other vendor's crawler documentation is silent on sitemaps — verified by direct fetch of OpenAI's, Anthropic's, Perplexity's, Meta's, Amazon's and Apple's pages. Bing is first-party explicit: a submitted sitemap is fetched immediately and revisited "typically at least once per day."

Log evidence that the others read sitemaps exists but is thin. A 48-day single-domain log study (288,566 log files, 12,099 AI-bot requests, 19 bots) recorded sitemap hits from Bingbot (139) and Claude-SearchBot (135) — but ClaudeBot's first-ever sitemap request was 18 March 2026 and GPTBot's was 19 March, two requests total. That is not a basis for a promise.

Two corrections that matter more than the sitemap answer

1. ChatGPT no longer runs on Bing. Direct measurement across 1,200 ChatGPT answers and 88,000 results found ChatGPT uses its own index — only 1.5% of its URLs appear in Bing's top 20 for the same queries. Bing is now the substrate for Copilot only. Any playbook telling you to "optimise for Bing to win ChatGPT" is running on 2024 information.

2. From the same 48-day log study: zero AI bots requested llms.txt. Independent confirmation of Section 09.

Keep the sitemap clean anyway. It is cheap, Google AI Overviews and Copilot both depend on it, and AI crawlers are wildly inefficient without one — 34.8% of ChatGPT fetches and 34.2% of Claude fetches hit 404s versus Googlebot's 8.2%. They are guessing at URLs. And as Section 14 shows, sitemap 403s are a real, documented failure mode.

Does <lastmod> make AI crawlers come back?

For Google and Bing, yes — with the caveat that Google treats lastmod trust as effectively binary per site, and ignores <priority> and <changefreq> entirely. Since AI Overviews and AI Mode ride the Google index, that transfers.

For OpenAI, Anthropic and Perplexity: no evidence either way, and one strong counter-signal. ChatGPT's reading cache ignores Cache-Control entirely, holds a ~30-minute freshness window, has been observed serving pages 90+ days after first fetch — and re-crawl frequency is driven by how often users ask about the page, not by a schedule or a date you supply. Anyone telling you lastmod drives GPTBot re-crawl is asserting something with no published basis.

Canonical, duplicates and pagination

Google is the only engine where canonical behaviour is first-party established — AI Overviews and AI Mode inherit the normal Search canonicalization pipeline.

Everywhere else it is a coin flip. The only real test — nine publishers syndicating to Yahoo and MSN, four engines — found: Perplexity consistently cited the syndication partner, sometimes above the original. ChatGPT was mixed. Claude frequently cited both URLs side by side in the same answer. Gemini often cited neither. Cross-domain canonical was described as "not foolproof — both urls are often indexed." The recommendation from that work: noindex the syndicated copy rather than relying on canonical.

Infinite scroll is invisible. This one is not close.

A 41-day controlled experiment on ~2,400 pages with sitemaps deliberately disabled found GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Bingbot and every other AI crawler discovered exactly ZERO pages behind JavaScript navigation. Googlebot reached 2%.

Corroborated independently: a study of 500M+ GPTBot fetches found zero evidence of JavaScript execution. ChatGPT fetches JS files in 11.5% of requests and Claude in 23.8% — and never runs them.

A third study (a single page with 62 tagged content-injection methods, tested across 6 assistants — vendor-run, n=1 page) reports the same for lazy-loading: content 2,000px below the fold scored 0 of 6. Its more useful secondary finding, worth verifying yourself before acting on: tabs and accordions are fine — collapsed content in the HTML scored 5–6 of 6 and display:none is read — while CSS-generated ::before/::after content scored zero.

The rule: paginated content must live at real, server-rendered, linkable URLs in the raw HTML. Infinite scroll layered over real URLs is safe. Infinite scroll instead of URLs is invisible to ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini. "View all" pages are fine if server-rendered and under 4 MB — ChatGPT rejects larger pages outright.

A small practical one worth knowing

ChatGPT appends utm_source=chatgpt.com to outbound citation links. So every AI-referred visitor lands on a unique URL variant, full-page and CDN caches miss, and your best-cited content serves slowest to exactly the traffic you worked for.

Freshness: what survives scrutiny

Reject: "95% of ChatGPT citations are under 10 months old"

This number is everywhere. It has no study behind it. Traced to a verbal remark by a vendor CEO during a webinar, then restated by dozens of blogs — and mutated along the way from "pages updated within 10 months" to "content published under 10 months," a different claim.

It is contradicted by the two largest datasets. Across 16,975,000 cited URLs, the mean age of a ChatGPT-cited page is 958 days — about 2.6 years. A separate 1.4M-prompt study puts the median at ~500 days. Neither is compatible with 95% under 10 months.

Also reject, all with no traceable original: "76.4% of most-cited pages updated within 30 days" · "content under 30 days old earns 3.2× more citations" · "4.5-week citation half-life" · "40–60% visibility loss per year."

The best-supported freshness finding: update, don't publish

Across 216,524 pages in 20 niches: content updated within the past three months averages 6.0 citations vs 3.6 for older equivalents — +67% on the same page. Meanwhile brand-new content under two months old averages 3.6, statistically indistinguishable from content 1.5 to 5 years old at 3.1.

Independently corroborated on 7,683 pages and 47,097 citations across three engines and four verticals: "The freshness LLMs reward is being manufactured by updates, not by new publishing." Among pages with both dates readable, 72% looked fresh by update date but only 42% were actually published in the last year. Refreshed older content beat genuinely new pages in all four verticals.

Honest counterweight: in the 17M-citation dataset the premium on last-updated date (13.1%) is half the premium on publication date (25.7%). And no true A/B — update URL A vs publish new URL B, matched topics — has ever been published. Two large observational datasets agree; that is strong, not conclusive.

Keep freshness in proportion

From the same 216,524-page model, ranked by predictive power: referring domains are the strongest single predictor (under 2,500 referring domains → 1.6 citations; over 350,000 → 8.4). Pages over 20,000 characters average 10.18 citations versus 2.39 for very short pages. Sections of 120–180 words between headings average 4.6 versus 2.7 for sections under 50 words. Authority and depth both outrank freshness. Update cadence is a multiplier on a page that already deserves to be cited — it is not a substitute for one.

Crawl is not citation — the most common error in this field

One site received 78,000 GPTBot requests but only 642 ChatGPT-User requests. High GPTBot volume is evidence that OpenAI is building its index. It is not evidence of AI visibility. Report user-triggered fetches, not crawl volume.

  • Whether Google Search Console's new AI report splits AI Overviews from AI Mode. Sources conflict; verify in a live property.
  • Apple's own confirmation that Siri reads Business Connect data — universally asserted by SEO sources, confirmed by none at primary-source level.
  • Data aggregators' role in LLM pipelines. Asserted, never confirmed.
  • The claimed −86.4% Reddit collapse in ChatGPT on 14 Aug 2026. Single source, could not be corroborated.
  • There's An AI For That review turnaround time. Pricing is published ($49 / $347); approval time is not, in any source found.
  • Perplexity merchant programme mechanics. Confirmed to exist; feed spec, eligibility and approval process are not documented in reachable sources the way OpenAI's are.
  • Vendor-specific WAF block signatures for Akamai, Fastly, Imperva and Sucuri, the AWS CategoryAI default action, and Cloudflare's exact dashboard menu paths. Single-sourced. See the provenance note in Section 14 — the audit procedure does not depend on them.
  • Whether AI crawlers other than Bingbot meaningfully use XML sitemaps. Only Bing confirms it first-party. The log evidence for GPTBot and ClaudeBot amounts to roughly 16 requests on one domain starting mid-March 2026. Keep sitemaps clean regardless — it is cheap and Google AI surfaces depend on it — but do not promise it moves AI citations.
  • Whether <lastmod> drives re-fetch for GPTBot, ClaudeBot or PerplexityBot. No vendor documents it and no study measures it. One mechanistic counter-signal: ChatGPT's re-crawl appears demand-driven, not date-driven.
  • Update-an-existing-URL vs publish-a-new-one. Two large observational datasets agree that updating wins. No controlled A/B has ever been published. Strong, not conclusive.
  • Whether IndexNow submission measurably changes AI citation rate. The causal chain — ping → Bing index → Copilot candidate pool → citation — is sound and each link is documented, but no study measures the end-to-end effect. It is free, so do it anyway; just do not promise a number.
