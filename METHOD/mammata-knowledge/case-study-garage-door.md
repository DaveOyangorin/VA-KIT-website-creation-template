# SEO Case Study: $18,000 Investment → $1.7M Revenue in 11 Months
**Source:** The Edward Show Podcast — Guest: Sarvesh Ostrava  
**Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MOBaAgQkdcI&t=1291s  
**Niche:** Garage Door Company (Local Home Services)

---

## Overview

A garage door company was struggling with zero calls, living off word of mouth and random referrals. The owner was almost ready to shut it all down. After implementing this exact local SEO strategy, they were booked out weeks in advance and turning away low-ticket jobs.

- **Client investment:** ~$18,000 over 11 months (monthly retainer)
- **Revenue generated:** $1.7 million
- **Timeline:** 11 months

---

## Strategy Breakdown

### 1. Google Business Profile First — Before the Website

> "Google Business Profile was lacking completely. They didn't even have the basics done right."

Most business owners either ignore their GBP entirely or focus 100% on the website. The fix was to attack GBP on Day 1.

**Day 1 Full Audit included:**
- Primary category audit — compared to top 3–4 competitors in the map pack
- Secondary subcategories — what competitors had selected (not guessing)
- Photo count comparison vs. competitors
- Total reviews vs. competitors
- **Review velocity** — how many new reviews per month (not just total)

**Key insight on review velocity:**
> "For Google, the number of reviews coming in is an indication of how popular you are *right now*. They do not have access to your CRM."

Google signals used as popularity proxy:
- Phone calls coming in
- Driving directions requests
- New reviews per month
- Star rating trends (1-star vs 5-star)

**What was fixed on GBP:**
- Primary category (based on what top 3 map pack competitors use — not guessing)
- Secondary subcategories (data-backed from competitor analysis)
- Business description (keyword-optimized, not generic)
- Real photos (geotagged, collected from client via Google Drive — NOT stock images)
- Products and services listings (with descriptions)
- Citations (added over time)

**Primary category selection method:**
1. Search your primary keyword (e.g., "garage door repair Houston") on Google Maps
2. Look at top 3–4 results — what is their primary category?
3. Select the same. That simple.
4. Use Claude / AI to analyze competitor GBP data and confirm your selection

---

### 2. Review Generation System

**The problem:** Reviews were coming in randomly based on luck.  
**The fix:** A repeatable system with incentives.

**The system:**
1. After every job, an automated message goes to the customer via CRM asking for a review
2. The field technician verbally asks for a review while still on-site
3. Tech says: *"Can you please leave a review on our Google profile? Here's the link. Please mention how your experience was with me — mention my name."*
4. The homeowner feels responsible for the tech's bonus → higher completion rate

**Incentive structure:**
- $10–$30 flat bonus per review collected (per tech)
- If customer mentions the tech's name → that tech gets the credit + bonus
- A tech collecting 10 reviews/month earns an extra $200–$300

**Why it works psychologically:**
> "As a human you become responsible. You paid the company but the work was done by this person. You can repay them just by leaving a review."

**Week 1 goal:** Collect 10 reviews immediately — forces the mental shift in the business owner.

**Incentivizing reviews is NOT against Google's rules** — you are not paying for positive reviews, just incentivizing the *act* of leaving a review.

---

### 3. Service Area Pages (The Money Pages)

> "The pages that actually rank are a combination of service + location. This is the page business owners do NOT create."

**What most sites have (wrong):**
- 5–6 generic service pages
- 10 location pages ("Areas we serve")

**What actually ranks:**
- **Service + Location combination pages** → "Garage Door Repair Houston", "Garage Door Installation Austin"

**Formula:**
```
[Service] + [Location] = 1 Page
```

If you have 5 services × 10 locations = **50 unique pages to create**

**Critical rule — NO templated pages:**
> "Don't create a template and replicate all 50 pages changing only the location name. Those pages do NOT rank."

Why templated pages fail:
- Google calls these "doorway pages" — against their guidelines
- If both you and a competitor can push a button and publish 50 identical pages with AI, Google has no reason to pick one over the other
- Google wants proof you actually belong to that location

**What makes a service area page unique:**
- Your knowledge of local geography
- Local weather-specific problems (e.g., garage door issues in Houston humidity vs. cold climates)
- Local landmarks or neighborhood references
- Actual experience in that specific area
- Unique testimonials from customers in that area

**Page structure (H1 / Title tag):**
- Page title and H1 can be the same OR slightly different
- Use different keyword variations: "garage door repair" (title) vs "garage door repairs" (H1)
- If only one variation exists, keep them identical
- Include modifiers like "affordable," "same day service," "no hidden fees" — these match buyer intent

**Page roll-out pace (for a low-authority site):**
- Start with **10–15 service area pages per month** (not 50 at once)
- Supplement with **10–12 blog posts per month**
- Why? A DR0 site publishing 50 pages at once → indexing becomes a serious challenge
- Slow and steady = Google picks up and indexes pages properly
- Simultaneously build backlinks to increase domain authority → flywheel effect

---

### 4. Blog Content (Local + Topical Authority)

> "Most business owners think blogs are just for big brands. Nope."

**Example blog for garage door company:**  
"How to protect your garage door before Houston storms destroy it"

**Why blogs work for local businesses:**

1. **Trust building** — visitors skim your blog and see you're an expert → multiple touch points → they call YOU when ready to buy
2. **Topical authority** — covering all questions in your niche signals to Google you're the authority
3. **Internal linking** — blogs link to service pages, passing link juice, helping indexing
4. **AI search citations** — indexed blogs can get cited by AI search results (SGE, Perplexity, etc.)
5. **Local news backlinks** — hyperlocal timely content can get picked up by local news editors

**Blog keyword strategy:**
- Map the buyer journey: What questions do people ask BEFORE booking a garage door repair?
- Add location modifiers to informational topics
- Ask AI: "What specific questions do people in [city] ask about [service]?"
- Target PAA (People Also Ask) boxes

**Blog → Service page connection:**
- Every blog should link to the relevant service area page it covers
- Example: Blog about "Houston garage door storm damage" → links to "Garage Door Repair Houston" page

**Local news link strategy:**
1. Write timely, hyperlocal content (storm coming → write prep guide)
2. Proactively email local news editors with the article
3. Offer to write a contributor article for their site
4. Once published once → you have a direct contact → reach out again for future coverage
5. Journalists write for multiple publications → one relationship = multiple domain links

---

### 5. Internal Linking (Most Underrated SEO Activity)

> "There is no site we have implemented internal linking on that we haven't seen immediate results."

**What this site had:** Zero links between pages — "a maze with no doors"

**What was built:**
- Proper internal silo structure
- Service pages cross-linked to related service pages
- Blog posts linked to relevant service area pages
- Large location hub pages linking to all services in that location
- Homepage linked to highest-priority pages (most revenue-generating)

**Link juice distribution:**
- Most backlinks built to the homepage
- Homepage equally distributes authority across all internal pages via internal links
- Ensures all pages benefit from external link building

**Priority pages get the most love:**
- Identify top 3 services by revenue potential
- Identify top 3 locations by revenue potential
- These pages get: navigation links, footer links, most internal links, and direct backlinks

**Site structure for multiple locations:**
```
yoursite.com/[location]/[service]
yoursite.com/brooklyn/garage-door-repair
yoursite.com/brooklyn/garage-door-installation
yoursite.com/queens/garage-door-repair
yoursite.com/queens/garage-door-installation
```

**URL test:** Does the URL alone tell Google exactly WHERE you are and WHAT service you provide? If yes — correct structure.

**Hub pages:** Linked from main navigation and/or footer. Location hub pages link out to all services within that location.

### 6. Link Building (Quality Over Quantity)

> "In local businesses, you don't need 10 links a month. You need 1–2 links a month of the best possible quality."

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't buy the cheapest guest post link you find ($10 vs $50 — don't pick $10)
- Don't buy links from PBN marketplaces (easy to buy = low quality, possibly penalized)
- Don't rely only on Better Business Bureau subscription — your competitors have the same link
- Don't spam "near me" keywords on pages — Google's algorithm already knows where the user is

**Google is much stricter on off-page/backlinks than on-page** because buying links = artificially manipulating their algorithm.

**What WORKS:**

**Guest Posts:**
- Target sites with DR 40+
- Target sites with 2,000–3,000+ monthly organic traffic (per Ahrefs)
- Check that the site doesn't rank for "link farm" type keywords
- A good link building team can identify a link farm at a glance
- Include backlink cost within the agency retainer — don't leave link buying decisions to clients (they'll always pick cheapest)

**Local News Links:**
- Much more lenient on metrics — genuine news site is valuable regardless of DR
- Approach: "We represent [business]. We'd like to get featured on your platform. How do the commercials look?"
- If they say "we don't do sponsored content" → move on
- If they quote a price → negotiate, request no "sponsored" label
- Local news outlets don't get many deals → most will negotiate
- Goal: Get them into a conversation first, then negotiate

**Journalist relationship tactic (advanced):**
1. Find a journalist you like who covers relevant topics
2. Pay them to write a guest article for YOUR business blog
3. Build the relationship
4. Ask: "Are you working on anything where I could comment as an expert?"
5. Journalists write for multiple outlets → one relationship = links across multiple domains

**Local business link swaps (3-way):**
- Business owners already know other local business owners
- 3-way link swap: Site A links to Site C, Site B links to Site A, etc.
- Only valuable if those businesses have decent SEO themselves (DR0 site swap = minimal value)
- Franchisee groups = great source of quality local links

**Advanced outreach tactic being tested:**
1. Get a guest post on a high-DR site (e.g., 200K monthly visitors)
2. In that article, mention the client as "top 5 businesses in [city]"
3. Approach local businesses: "We're linking to you from [big site]. In return, link to our client from your site."
4. You're offering a link from a much better domain = much higher acceptance rate

### 7. Local Keywords Only — Stop Chasing National Terms

> "Garage door repair company" vs "garage door repair company New York" — these are two completely different ballgames.

**Common business owner mistake:**
- Wanting to rank for "best garage door company" or "top garage doors" (nationwide)
- These keywords: massive competition, irrelevant traffic, won't generate local calls

**The reality:**
- If you're based in Austin, what do you do with leads from New York?
- Even if you ranked nationally — completely useless for a local service business

**The fix:**
- Every page targets: **[Service] + [Location]**
- Google knows where the searcher is (IP address, device location)
- A page titled "garage door repair Austin" will outrank "garage door repair" for someone searching in Austin

**Stop doing this:**
- "Near me" spam in headings and subheadings
- Google's algorithm is sophisticated enough to know what "near me" means for each user
- Just put your location name — Google handles the rest

## The Complete Flywheel

```
GBP Optimized → Reviews Coming In → Map Pack Rankings Improve
       ↓
Service Area Pages Created → Internal Links Built → Indexing Improves
       ↓
Blog Content Published → Topical Authority Established → Rankings Climb
       ↓
Quality Backlinks Acquired → Domain Authority Increases → All Pages Rank Higher
       ↓
More Calls → More Jobs → More Reviews → GBP Ranks Higher → [Repeat]
```

## Key Mindset Shifts

1. **GBP before website** — most businesses get this backwards
2. **Review velocity > review total** — 10/month beats 500 total acquired over 5 years
3. **Slow page rollout wins long-term** — 10–15 pages/month > 100 pages in month 1
4. **1–2 great links > 10 cheap links** — quality always beats quantity for local
5. **Local keywords only** — national rankings = useless for service businesses
6. **Blogs are for SEO, not traffic** — topical authority and internal linking are the goals
7. **Incentivize your team** — $10–30/review = review velocity transforms
